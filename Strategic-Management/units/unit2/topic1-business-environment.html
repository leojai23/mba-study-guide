Unit 2Business Environment & External Analysis

Book: Strategic Management (BA4301, Anna University MBA Sem III) — Dr. G. Pandi Selvi & Dr. M. Hemalatha, Thakur Publication

1. Overview

This topic opens Unit 2 by defining the business environment that surrounds every organisation, and the systematic process (environmental appraisal) used to scan it for opportunities and threats.

2. Detailed Notes

1.1 Meaning & Definition of Business Environment

W.F. Glueck & Lawrence R. Jauch: "Business environment includes the factors outside the firm that can lead to opportunities for or threats to the firm. Although there are many factors, the most important of the sectors are socio-economic, technological, supplier, competitor and government."

Keith Davis: "Business environment is the aggregate of all conditions, events, and influences that surround and affect it."

1.2 Nature of Business Environment

  • Inseparable from Business: No business can function without an interactive relationship with its environment.
  • Dynamic: Environmental changes occur according to shifts in customer tastes and preferences, technology, and government policy.
  • Business Lacks Control Over Environment: A business can only consider and adapt to external factors — it cannot control them.
  • Impact and Consequences: Business decisions can influence the environment, and the environment in turn affects business decisions — the relationship runs both ways.
  • Internal and External Factors: The environment is made up of both internal (micro) and external (macro) factors that together shape the business.
  • Multifaceted: The same environmental change can be interpreted differently (as an opportunity or a threat) by different firms or people.
  • Regulates the Scope of Business: The environment sets the boundaries within which a business can expand or diversify.
  • Long-Lasting Impact: Environmental changes can affect a business positively or negatively over a long period.
  • Opportunities and Obstacles: Every environmental change brings both opportunities to exploit and threats/obstacles to manage.
  • Uncertain: Since environmental changes are hard to predict precisely, businesses must always stay prepared for change.

1.3 Internal vs External Environment

Internal EnvironmentExternal Environment
Factors within the organisation's control (financial resources, HR, mission/vision, management structure)Factors outside the organisation's control (Micro: suppliers, customers, intermediaries, competitors, public; Macro: political, economic, social, technological, legal, international)

1.4 Components of the External Business Environment

Micro-environment (immediate operating environment): Suppliers, Customers, Market Intermediaries, Competitors, Public.

Macro-environment (broad forces): Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and International/Global Business Environment.

1.5 Procedure of External Analysis (Abell's Steps)

  1. Understand the nature of the external environment (its dynamism and complexity).
  2. Identify the critical external factors relevant to the business.
  3. Identify the past and future changes taking place in the environment.
  4. Study the opportunities and threats that arise from the environment.
  5. Analyse the industry and competitors.
  6. Study the strategic position of the organisation in relation to its environment.

1.6 Significance of Environmental Appraisal

  • Helps identify opportunities and provides early warning signals of threats.
  • Helps tap useful resources from the environment.
  • Helps the organisation adapt to and cope with rapid environmental changes.
  • Provides a base for continuous learning and improvement.
  • Helps forecast productivity and effectiveness of strategies.

1.7 Techniques of Environmental Analysis

TechniqueWhat it does
ETOP (Environmental Threat and Opportunity Profile)A structured technique that diagnoses the environment and structures it into distinct sectors, helping managers identify environmental threats and opportunities specific to their industry.
SWOT AnalysisAnother well-known technique that studies an organisation's internal Strengths and Weaknesses alongside external Opportunities and Threats, providing a simple, holistic view of the business's current position.
PEST AnalysisStudies the Political, Economic, Social, and Technological factors of the macro-environment that affect a business's strategic planning.
QUEST (Quick Environmental Scanning Technique)A wide-ranging, "quick" technique for scanning dynamic environmental factors, giving executives a fast, high-level view of the environment before deeper analysis.

ETOP and SWOT are typically used together: ETOP structures the environment sector-by-sector, while SWOT summarises the resulting threats/opportunities against the firm's own strengths/weaknesses.

3. Key Terms

Business Environment
The aggregate of all external factors and conditions that surround and affect a business.
Micro-environment
Immediate factors close to the business: suppliers, customers, intermediaries, competitors, and the public.
Macro-environment
Broad societal forces: economic, political-legal, social, technological, demographic, and international factors.
Environmental Appraisal
The systematic process of scanning and analysing the environment to identify opportunities and threats.
ETOP
Environmental Threat and Opportunity Profile — a technique that structures the environment into sectors to identify industry-specific threats and opportunities.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Business environment = all external factors/conditions surrounding and affecting a business (Keith Davis).
  • Nature: inseparable from business, dynamic, uncertain, complex, and relative to place/time.
  • Micro-environment (suppliers, customers, intermediaries, competitors, public) vs Macro-environment (economic, political-legal, social, technological, demographic, international).
  • External analysis procedure: understand environment → identify critical factors → track changes → study opportunities/threats → analyse industry/competitors → assess strategic position.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define business environment and explain its nature.
    Show model answer
    Business environment is the aggregate of all external conditions and factors that surround and affect a business (Keith Davis). Its nature is inseparable from the business, dynamic, uncertain, complex, and relative to time and place.
  2. Distinguish between micro and macro components of the business environment.
    Show model answer
    Micro-environment consists of factors in the immediate operating environment — suppliers, customers, market intermediaries, competitors, and the public. Macro-environment consists of broader forces — economic, political-legal, social, technological, demographic, and international factors.
  3. Explain the procedure followed in external environmental analysis.
    Show model answer
    It involves understanding the nature of the environment, identifying critical external factors, tracking past and future changes, studying opportunities and threats, analysing the industry and competitors, and assessing the organisation's strategic position relative to its environment.
