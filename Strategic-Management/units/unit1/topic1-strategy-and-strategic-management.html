Unit 1Concept of Strategy & Strategic Management

Book: Strategic Management (BA4301, Anna University MBA Sem III) — Dr. G. Pandi Selvi & Dr. M. Hemalatha, Thakur Publication

1. Overview

This topic lays the foundation for the whole subject: what "strategy" actually means, the different levels at which it operates in an organisation, how a strategy is formed and why it matters, and what "strategic management" adds on top of strategy itself — a continuous, disciplined process of forming, implementing, and controlling strategy.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Concept of Strategy

The word "strategy" comes from the Greek word strategos, meaning "generalship" — originally a military term for the art of using resources to win a war, now used in business for using resources to achieve long-term objectives.

  • Alfred D. Chandler: "Strategy is the determination of the basic long-term goals and objectives of an enterprise and the adoption of courses of action and the allocation of resources necessary for carrying out these goals."
  • Igor Ansoff: "Strategy is the common thread among the organisation's activities and product markets that defines the essential nature of business that the organisation was or planned to be in future."
  • William F. Glueck & Lawrence R. Jauch: "Strategy is a unified, comprehensive and integrated plan designed to assure that the basic objectives of the enterprise are achieved."
  • George A. Steiner: Strategy is not as simple as it seems — logical understanding of its theory helps to grasp it and work with more ease.

2.2 Nature of Strategy

  • Provides Direction: Gives the organisation a clear image and a structure for allocating resources to achieve its goals.
  • Integrated Approach: Follows an integrated approach that guides rational decision-making and resource allocation across the enterprise.
  • Relates the Organisation to its Environment: Matches the organisation's internal strengths and weaknesses with external opportunities and threats.
  • Future-Oriented: Deals with fresh problems and probable future opportunities/threats the organisation has not faced before.
  • System-Oriented: Operates as a system built from a combination of the organisation's internal and external factors.
  • Combination of Internal & External Factors: Blends internal capabilities with external environmental conditions into a single strategic view.
  • Involves Contradictory Actions: Since it responds to environmental factors, some strategic decisions may occur simultaneously or even conflict with existing rules and standards.
  • Complex, Not Simple: Strategy is not as simple as it looks — a logical, theoretical understanding is needed to fully grasp it.

2.3 Levels of Strategy

Strategy exists at different levels in an organisation, each with its own scope:

LevelWho forms itFocus
Corporate StrategyTop management (Board/CEO)What businesses to be in; overall direction and shape of the whole organisation (e.g. diversification, turnaround).
Business StrategyStrategic Business Unit (SBU) headsHow to compete in a particular market/industry with a specific product line to gain competitive advantage.
Functional StrategyFunctional/department heads (marketing, finance, HR, R&D, operations)How each department converts corporate/business strategy into day-to-day action plans.

Example used in the book: Nokia's turnaround strategy (corporate level) had to be supported by functional strategies in R&D, marketing, and manufacturing before it could achieve its stated goal of becoming a leader in smartphones again.

2.4 Strategy Formation Process

Strategy formulation is a step-by-step process usually shown as a wheel/cycle:

  1. Environmental Scanning — monitoring, evaluating, and disseminating information from the internal and external environment.
  2. Strategy Formulation — developing long-range plans using Vision/Mission, SWOT, and objectives.
  3. Strategy Implementation — putting strategy into action through programmes, budgets, and procedures.
  4. Evaluation & Control — monitoring performance and taking corrective action if results deviate from the plan.

2.5 Roles of Strategy

Strategy plays several roles for an organisation:

  • Serves as an operational planning framework.
  • Acts as a decision-maker's framework.
  • Assists managers in looking for fresh avenues for growth — a "choice of best alternative" process.

2.6 Approaches to Strategy Making

ApproachIdea
AutocraticThe strategic leader has complete authority; makes decisions alone.
TransformationalThe leader inspires employees toward a common vision through transformational communication.
RationalAnalysing pros and cons of alternatives systematically, then choosing the best.
LearningStrategy evolves continuously as the organisation learns from experience.
PoliticalStrategy is shaped through negotiation and bargaining among different interest groups within the organisation.

2.7 Importance of Strategy

  • Provides Direction: Helps the organisation direct its efforts toward achieving a clear and specific goal.
  • Facilitates Proper Allocation of Resources: Strategy and strategic planning guide the enterprise in allocating and utilising resources efficiently.
  • Ensures Proper Decision-Making: Strategic decisions provide clarity to managers, who otherwise face unclear and non-specific choices.
  • Synchronises Comparison of Alternative Actions: Strategy helps in comparing company-wide actions before selecting the best one to pursue.
  • Enables Organisation to Formulate Objectives: Helps in analysing and setting the objectives and mission of the organisation clearly.
  • Improves Comparison of Alternative Actions: Strategy provides a framework for evaluating different courses of action against each other.
  • Long-Term Issues: Strategy is inherently long-term in nature, providing dynamic and comprehensive direction to the organisation.
  • Clarifies Direction and Objectives: A business with clarity in direction and specific objectives is better placed to achieve reliable and quantifiable results.

2.8 Meaning & Definition of Strategic Management

Strategic Management is the process of formulating, implementing, and evaluating cross-functional decisions that enable an organisation to achieve its objectives. It combines strategy formulation with the formulation of business objectives, keeping the organisation's mission in mind.

2.9 Characteristics of Strategic Management

  • Long-Term Issues: Deals with decisions regarding long-term issues that affect the future of the company.
  • Impact on Operations: Strategic decisions have a wide impact on the day-to-day operations carried out at lower and middle management levels.
  • Competitive Advantage: Helps a firm gain an edge over rivals by aligning its resources and strategy effectively.
  • Uncertain and Unpredictable: Deals with future situations, so managers cannot be fully certain of the outcome of their strategic decisions.
  • Complex: Applied to situations involving both internal and external environmental change, which makes analysis complex.
  • Organisation-Wide: Affects the entire organisation, since it shapes the core, routine operations and activities across every department.
  • Long-Term Implications: The effects of formulating and implementing strategy are felt over a long, systematic time horizon.

2.10 Conceptual Framework for Strategic Management

The book presents strategic management as a four-stage process (Figure 1.2, the Strategic Management Model):

  1. Environmental Scanning: Monitoring, evaluating, and disseminating information from the external and internal environment to key people within the organisation. Its purpose is to identify strategic factors that will determine the future of the organisation.
  2. Strategy Formulation: Developing long-range plans for effective management of environmental opportunities and threats, in light of organisational strengths and weaknesses. Includes defining:
    • Vision of the Organisation: A vision statement conveys the future state the organisation aspires to.
    • Mission: The purpose and reason for the organisation's existence.
    • Objectives: The end results of planned activity, stated as what is to be accomplished, by when, and quantified if possible.
    • Policies: Broad guidelines set by top management for decision-making at lower levels throughout the organisation.
  3. Strategy Implementation: Putting strategies and policies into action through the development of programmes, budgets, and procedures.
  4. Evaluation and Control: Monitoring corporate activities and performance results, so that actual performance can be compared with desired performance and corrective action taken where needed.

2.11 Importance of Strategic Management

Strategic management helps an organisation in the following ways:

  • Fulfilling the Objectives: Helps meet the objectives of an organisation by breaking them down into daily tasks and duties for the board, senior management, and employees.
  • Helps in Measuring the Progress: Comparing actual results against the strategy lets the organisation assess how effective its strategy formulation has been.
  • Provides an Organisational Viewpoint: Forces managers to think broadly and stay aware of internal and external factors as a whole, rather than in isolation.
  • Helps in Assessing Environmental Changes: Keeps the organisation's internal and external environment under continuous scan, which managers might otherwise overlook.
  • Develops a Decision-Making Framework: Gives managers a structured framework so that, instead of putting all efforts into meeting only short-term challenges, they stay focused on the organisation's wider objectives and performance.
  • Identifies SWOT: Helps identify the organisation's Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats as a whole.
  • Strengthens Brand Management: Helps in creating a new product image in the customer's mind, and assists in succession planning and organisational compensation plans.
  • Builds a Strong, Skilled Workforce: Through better recruitment, yearly appraisals, and training, employees become a highly skilled workforce able to craft and execute strategy.
  • Helps Accomplishing Goals: Enables a company to achieve its market position by allocating resources needed, training employees, and enhancing production capacity.

2.12 Limitations of Strategic Management

Strategic management also has several drawbacks:

  • Time-Consuming: Strategic management is an extremely long-term process, requiring several months of staffing, pricing, and planning-related discussions.
  • Costly: Appointing highly skilled and specialised workers to craft and execute a strategy is difficult and expensive; it takes a Master's degree to implement effectively.
  • Challenging Process of Implementing Strategy: In order to survive, an organisation may implement a strategy different from the one that was originally planned, making the process very difficult to control.
  • Unexpected Outcomes: Many companies have proved that even the best-crafted strategic plans can fail to deliver expected results because of the dynamic nature of the environment.
  • Limited to a Set of Rules: An organisation may find it challenging to apply the same strategic management process uniformly, since strategy formulation is based on a theory of the changes in the environment.
  • Poor Adaptability to Change: Once strategies are prescribed as schedules, programmes, and rules, this becomes a hindrance to strategy formulation whenever the situation demands a quick change of course.

2.13 Difference between Strategy and Policy

Basis of DifferenceStrategyPolicy
MeaningA plan of action formulated to achieve an organisation's specific objectives.A set of guidelines that direct decision-making and everyday operations.
NatureDynamic — formulated for competitive and constantly changing situations; requires a constant response.Static and stable — formulated to remain applicable over a longer time-frame without frequent change.
FeaturesDeals with critical issues that have not occurred yet and are repetitive in nature.Deals with situations that have already occurred and recur in the organisation's operations.
OrientationAction-oriented — concerned with what needs to be done.Thought/decision-oriented — concerned with the thinking that guides how things should be done.
ScopeBroader in scope; formulated on the basis of the actions needed to achieve objectives.Narrower in scope; formulated as the basis for taking those actions consistently.
Focus/ObjectivesFocused on achieving specific organisational objectives.Focused on providing consistent guidelines to achieve the organisation's objectives.
Overall GoalStrategies are formulated to achieve the organisation's objectives efficiently.Policies are the applied instructions used to achieve those same objectives operationally.
Implementation at Level of OrganisationFormulated by top management and implemented at every level, involving the whole organisation.Formulated by top management but implemented mainly at middle and lower levels through employees carrying out daily operations.

3. Key Terms

Strategy
A unified, comprehensive plan of action and resource allocation designed to achieve an organisation's long-term objectives.
Corporate Strategy
Top-level strategy deciding which businesses/industries the organisation should be in.
Business Strategy
SBU-level strategy on how to compete successfully in a particular market.
Functional Strategy
Department-level strategy that operationalises corporate/business strategy.
Strategic Management
The continuous process of formulating, implementing, and evaluating strategy to achieve organisational objectives.
SWOT
Strengths, Weaknesses (internal) and Opportunities, Threats (external) — the core internal/external environmental scan feeding strategy formulation.
Policy
A set of stable guidelines that direct everyday decision-making and operations, as distinct from strategy's action-oriented plan.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Strategy = long-term plan + resource allocation to achieve objectives (Chandler, Ansoff, Glueck & Jauch definitions).
  • Three levels: Corporate (what businesses) → Business/SBU (how to compete) → Functional (how each department executes).
  • Strategy formation = Environmental Scanning → Formulation → Implementation → Evaluation & Control (cyclical, not one-time).
  • 5 approaches to strategy-making: Autocratic, Transformational, Rational, Learning, Political.
  • Strategic management = strategy + cross-functional implementation + continuous evaluation; characterised by uncertainty, complexity, org-wide scope, and long-term orientation.
  • Importance of strategic management: fulfils objectives, measures progress, gives an organisational viewpoint, assesses environmental change, builds a decision-making framework, identifies SWOT, strengthens brand management, and builds workforce skill.
  • Limitations: time-consuming, costly, hard to implement consistently, can produce unexpected outcomes, limited by rigid rules, and poor adaptability once schedules/programmes are fixed.
  • Strategy vs Policy: Strategy is dynamic and action-oriented (what to do); Policy is stable and thought-oriented (guidelines for how things should be done), implemented mainly at middle/lower levels.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define strategy. State any two definitions given by different authors.
    Show model answer
    Strategy is a unified, comprehensive, integrated plan that assures achievement of an enterprise's basic objectives (Glueck & Jauch). Chandler defines it as the determination of long-term goals and objectives along with the adoption of courses of action and resource allocation to carry them out.
  2. Explain the three levels of strategy with an example.
    Show model answer
    Corporate level decides which businesses to be in (e.g. Nokia's decision to focus on becoming a smartphone leader again); Business/SBU level decides how to compete in that specific market; Functional level (marketing, R&D, HR, finance) converts that into department-specific action plans.
  3. Describe the strategy formation process.
    Show model answer
    It is a cyclical process: (1) Environmental Scanning of internal & external factors, (2) Strategy Formulation using vision/mission and SWOT, (3) Strategy Implementation via programmes and budgets, (4) Evaluation & Control comparing actual results to targets and correcting deviations.
  4. What is meant by strategic management? State its characteristics.
    Show model answer
    Strategic management is the ongoing process of formulating, implementing, and evaluating cross-functional decisions to achieve organisational objectives. It is characterised by uncertainty, complexity, organisation-wide impact, and a long-term orientation.
  5. Discuss the various approaches to strategy-making.
    Show model answer
    Autocratic (leader decides alone), Transformational (leader inspires shared vision), Rational (systematic weighing of alternatives), Learning (strategy evolves from experience), and Political (shaped through internal negotiation/bargaining).
  6. Explain the importance of strategy to an organisation.
    Show model answer
    Strategy clarifies direction, enhances organisational effectiveness and efficiency, improves decision-making and communication, ensures proper resource allocation, synchronises departments, and gives the whole organisation a clear sense of purpose.
  7. What is the importance of strategic management to an organisation?
    Show model answer
    It helps fulfil objectives by breaking them into daily tasks, measures progress against results, gives managers a broad organisational viewpoint, keeps environmental changes under review, provides a decision-making framework, identifies SWOT, strengthens brand management, and builds a skilled workforce.
  8. Discuss the limitations of strategic management.
    Show model answer
    It is time-consuming and costly, can be difficult to implement consistently (organisations may deviate from the original plan just to survive), can still produce unexpected outcomes despite good planning, is limited by being based on a fixed set of rules, and once schedules/programmes are set, adapting quickly to sudden change becomes difficult.
  9. Differentiate between Strategy and Policy.
    Show model answer
    Strategy is a dynamic, action-oriented plan formulated to achieve specific objectives and requires a constant response to changing conditions; Policy is a stable, thought-oriented set of guidelines that directs routine decision-making. Strategy is broader in scope and implemented across the whole organisation, while policy mainly guides middle- and lower-level operations.
