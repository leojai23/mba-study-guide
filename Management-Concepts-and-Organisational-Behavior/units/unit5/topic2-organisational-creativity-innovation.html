Unit 5Organisational Creativity and Innovation

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Organisational creativity and innovation are increasingly treated as something management can deliberately cultivate, not just something that happens by chance. This topic covers what drives creativity at work and how organisations convert ideas into innovation.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Meaning of Organisational Creativity

Organisational creativity is the generation of novel and useful ideas by individuals or groups working together within an organisational context.

2.2 Meaning of Innovation

Innovation is the successful implementation of creative ideas — turning a novel idea into a new product, service, process or practice that creates value. Creativity is the starting point; innovation is creativity put into practice.

2.3 Factors Influencing Organisational Creativity

FactorInfluence
Individual FactorsExpertise, creative thinking skills, and intrinsic motivation of employees.
Organisational ClimateA climate that tolerates reasonable risk-taking and failure encourages more creative effort.
Leadership SupportLeaders who encourage experimentation and new ideas rather than only rewarding conformity.
Resources and AutonomyGiving employees enough time, resources and freedom to explore new approaches.
DiversityDiverse teams bring varied perspectives that often spark more creative solutions.

2.4 The Innovation Process

  1. Idea Generation: Creating or discovering a new idea, often through creative individuals or teams.
  2. Idea Development: Refining and testing the idea to assess its practicality and value.
  3. Championing: An internal advocate promotes the idea and builds support for it.
  4. Implementation: The idea is put into practice as a new product, service or process.
  5. Diffusion: The innovation spreads throughout the organisation or market.

2.5 Barriers to Creativity and Innovation

  • A rigid, highly bureaucratic structure that discourages risk-taking.
  • A culture that punishes failure rather than treating it as part of learning.
  • Short-term performance pressure that crowds out exploratory thinking.
  • Insufficient resources, time, or autonomy given to employees.

2.6 Encouraging Creativity and Innovation

  • Creating psychological safety so employees feel safe proposing unconventional ideas.
  • Rewarding and recognising creative effort, not just successful outcomes.
  • Building diverse, cross-functional teams to generate a wider range of ideas.
  • Providing dedicated time or resources (e.g. innovation labs, hackathons) for exploratory work.

3. Key Terms

Organisational Creativity
The generation of novel and useful ideas by individuals or groups within an organisational context.
Innovation
The successful implementation of creative ideas into a new product, service, process or practice that creates value.
Psychological Safety
A climate in which employees feel safe to propose unconventional ideas or admit mistakes without fear of punishment.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Creativity = generating novel, useful ideas; Innovation = successfully implementing those ideas.
  • Factors influencing creativity: Individual factors, Organisational climate, Leadership support, Resources/Autonomy, Diversity.
  • Innovation process: Idea Generation → Idea Development → Championing → Implementation → Diffusion.
  • Barriers: rigid bureaucracy, fear of failure, short-term pressure, lack of resources/autonomy.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Differentiate between creativity and innovation.
    Show model answer
    Creativity is the generation of novel and useful ideas, while innovation is the successful implementation of those ideas into a new product, service, process or practice that creates value — creativity is the starting point, innovation is creativity put into practice.
  2. What factors influence organisational creativity?
    Show model answer
    Individual factors (expertise, creative skills, motivation), organisational climate (tolerance for risk and failure), leadership support for experimentation, adequate resources and autonomy, and team diversity bringing varied perspectives.
  3. Explain the stages of the innovation process.
    Show model answer
    Idea Generation creates a new idea; Idea Development refines and tests it; Championing has an internal advocate build support; Implementation puts the idea into practice; and Diffusion spreads the innovation through the organisation or market.
