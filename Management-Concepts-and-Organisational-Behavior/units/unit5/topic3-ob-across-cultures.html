Unit 5Organisational Behaviour Across Cultures

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

As organisations operate across borders, understanding how culture shapes organisational behaviour becomes essential. This topic covers how OB differs across cultures and the conditions that affect cross-cultural organisational operations.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Organisational Behaviour Across Cultures

Organisational Behaviour across cultures (Cross-Cultural OB) studies how national culture shapes employee attitudes, motivation, communication and management effectiveness, recognising that OB theories developed in one cultural context do not automatically apply everywhere.

3.2 Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions

Geert Hofstede's research identified key dimensions along which national cultures differ, with major implications for management:

DimensionMeaning
Power DistanceThe degree to which a culture accepts unequal distribution of power between superiors and subordinates.
Individualism vs CollectivismWhether people see themselves primarily as independent individuals or as part of a tightly-knit group.
Uncertainty AvoidanceThe degree to which a culture feels threatened by ambiguous or uncertain situations and tries to avoid them through rules.
Masculinity vs FemininityThe degree to which a culture values assertiveness and achievement (masculine) versus cooperation and quality of life (feminine).
Long-Term vs Short-Term OrientationWhether a culture emphasises future rewards like persistence and thrift, or values tradition and quick results.

3.3 Conditions Affecting Cross-Cultural Organisational Operations

  • Legal and Regulatory Differences: Labour law, taxation and compliance requirements vary significantly by country.
  • Economic Conditions: Wage levels, inflation and market maturity differ across the countries an organisation operates in.
  • Political Stability: Political risk affects an organisation's ability to plan and operate confidently.
  • Language and Communication Norms: Direct differences in language, and indirect differences in communication style (direct vs indirect, high-context vs low-context).
  • Religious and Social Customs: Local customs around holidays, dress, gender roles and etiquette that an organisation must respect.

3.4 Implications for Management

  • Leadership and motivation approaches that work well in one culture may need to be adapted for another (e.g. individual-focused incentive pay may motivate less in a highly collectivist culture).
  • Decision-making style should be adjusted to local expectations around hierarchy and participation.
  • Managers operating internationally benefit from cultural-awareness training before taking on cross-border responsibilities.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain Hofstede's cultural dimensions.
    Show model answer
    Power Distance measures acceptance of unequal power distribution; Individualism vs Collectivism measures whether people identify as independent or as part of a group; Uncertainty Avoidance measures comfort with ambiguity; Masculinity vs Femininity measures emphasis on achievement versus quality of life; and Long-Term vs Short-Term Orientation measures emphasis on future rewards versus tradition and quick results.
  2. What conditions affect cross-cultural organisational operations?
    Show model answer
    Legal and regulatory differences, economic conditions, political stability, language and communication norms, and religious and social customs that vary across the countries an organisation operates in.
  3. What are the management implications of cross-cultural differences?
    Show model answer
    Leadership and motivation approaches may need adapting to local cultural expectations, decision-making style should reflect local norms around hierarchy and participation, and managers benefit from cultural-awareness training before taking on international responsibilities.
