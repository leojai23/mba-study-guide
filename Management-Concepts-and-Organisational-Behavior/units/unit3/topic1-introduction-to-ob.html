Unit 3Introduction to Organisational Behaviour

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

This opening topic of Unit 3 defines Organisational Behaviour (OB), the disciplines it draws from, and why understanding how individuals behave at work matters for managers.

2. Detailed Notes

1.1 Meaning and Definition of Organisational Behaviour

Organisational Behaviour (OB) is the systematic study of the actions and attitudes of individuals and groups within an organisation, and how that behaviour affects the organisation's performance. It examines behaviour at three levels: the individual, the group, and the organisation as a whole.

1.2 Nature of Organisational Behaviour

  • A Field of Study: OB is a distinct field of study, not a discipline in the formal, established sense, since it draws heavily on other disciplines.
  • Interdisciplinary: It applies knowledge from psychology, sociology, social psychology, anthropology and political science to workplace behaviour.
  • Applied Science: OB knowledge is applied to solve real organisational and managerial problems.
  • Normative and Value-Centred: OB offers explanations of how things are, and also judgements about how to improve workplace outcomes for both people and the organisation.
  • Human-Centred: Its core concern is understanding, predicting and influencing human behaviour at work.

1.3 Contributing Disciplines of Organisational Behaviour

DisciplineContribution to OB
PsychologyUnderstanding individual behaviour — learning, motivation, personality, perception, attitudes, job satisfaction, decision-making, and training.
SociologyUnderstanding group behaviour within organisations — group dynamics, organisational culture, formal organisation theory, and organisational change.
Social PsychologyBlending psychology and sociology to study how individuals influence and are influenced by groups — attitude change, communication patterns, and group decision-making.
AnthropologyStudying the influence of culture and the environment on the values, attitudes and behaviour of people across societies, relevant to comparative and cross-cultural management.
Political ScienceStudying the behaviour of individuals and groups in a political environment — power, conflict, and organisational politics.

1.4 Importance of Organisational Behaviour

  • Helps managers understand why employees behave the way they do, enabling better people-management decisions.
  • Improves a manager's ability to predict, explain and influence behaviour toward organisational goals.
  • Supports better leadership, motivation, communication and conflict-resolution practices.
  • Helps build a more positive organisational climate and culture, improving both performance and employee well-being.

1.5 Levels of Analysis in OB

LevelFocus
Individual LevelPerception, learning, personality, attitudes, values, motivation and individual decision-making.
Group LevelGroup dynamics, teams, leadership, power and politics, conflict and communication within and between groups.
Organisational LevelOrganisational structure, culture, change and development, and how the organisation as a whole functions.

3. Key Terms

Organisational Behaviour (OB)
The systematic study of the actions and attitudes of individuals and groups within an organisation, and their effect on organisational performance.
Social Psychology
A discipline blending psychology and sociology, studying how individuals influence and are influenced by groups.
Levels of Analysis in OB
The individual, group, and organisational levels at which organisational behaviour is studied.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • OB = systematic study of individual/group actions and attitudes within an organisation and their effect on performance.
  • 5 contributing disciplines: Psychology (individual behaviour), Sociology (group behaviour), Social Psychology (individual-group interaction), Anthropology (culture), Political Science (power/politics).
  • 3 levels of OB analysis: Individual, Group, Organisational.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define Organisational Behaviour. Explain its nature.
    Show model answer
    OB is the systematic study of the actions and attitudes of individuals and groups within an organisation and how that behaviour affects performance. Its nature: it is an interdisciplinary field of study, an applied science, both normative and value-centred, and fundamentally human-centred.
  2. Explain the disciplines that contribute to Organisational Behaviour.
    Show model answer
    Psychology contributes understanding of individual behaviour (learning, motivation, personality); Sociology contributes understanding of group behaviour and organisational structure; Social Psychology studies how individuals and groups influence each other; Anthropology studies the influence of culture on behaviour; and Political Science studies power, conflict and politics within organisations.
  3. Why is the study of OB important to a manager?
    Show model answer
    It helps managers understand why employees behave as they do, improves their ability to predict and influence behaviour toward organisational goals, supports better leadership, motivation and conflict resolution, and helps build a more positive organisational climate.
