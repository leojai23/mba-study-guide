Unit 3Attitudes, Values and Beliefs

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Attitudes, values and beliefs together shape how employees evaluate their work and workplace, and ultimately influence their behaviour, satisfaction and performance.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Meaning and Definition of Attitude

An attitude is a relatively stable evaluative statement or judgement — favourable or unfavourable — about a person, object or event.

4.2 Components of Attitude

ComponentDescription
Cognitive ComponentThe beliefs, opinions and knowledge a person holds about the attitude object.
Affective ComponentThe emotional or feeling part of an attitude — how the person feels about the object.
Behavioural ComponentThe intention to behave in a particular way toward the attitude object.

4.3 Formation of Attitudes

  • Direct Experience: Attitudes often form from personal interactions and experiences.
  • Social Learning: Attitudes are learned from family, peers, teachers and other role models.
  • Classical/Operant Conditioning: Attitudes can be shaped through association and reinforcement, similar to learned behaviours.

4.4 Attitude and Behaviour at Work

Key work-related attitudes include Job Satisfaction (a positive feeling about one's job resulting from an evaluation of its characteristics), Job Involvement (the degree of psychological identification with one's job), and Organisational Commitment (identifying with and wanting to remain part of a particular organisation). Positive work attitudes are generally associated with lower absenteeism and turnover, and better citizenship behaviour.

4.5 Meaning and Definition of Values

Values are broad, enduring beliefs about what is important and desirable, guiding a person's judgement of right and wrong across situations. Unlike attitudes, which are more specific and situational, values are more general and stable over a lifetime.

4.6 Sources and Types of Values

  • Sources: Family upbringing, culture, religion, education and significant life experiences.
  • Terminal Values: Desirable end-states of existence, the goals a person would like to achieve in their lifetime (e.g. a comfortable life, wisdom, self-respect).
  • Instrumental Values: Preferable modes of behaviour, or the means of achieving the terminal values (e.g. honesty, ambition, responsibility).

4.7 Importance of Values

  • Shape a person's perception of what is fair, good and right at work.
  • Influence motivation and the kinds of rewards an employee finds meaningful.
  • Underlie organisational culture when shared widely across the workforce.

4.8 Beliefs

A belief is a conviction about the truth of something, often formed without complete objective evidence. In the workplace, beliefs shape how employees interpret events and the trust (or distrust) they place in management's intentions.

Relationship Between Attitude and Belief

Beliefs form the cognitive foundation on which attitudes are built — what a person believes to be true about something strongly shapes how favourably or unfavourably they come to feel about it.

3. Key Terms

Attitude
A relatively stable evaluative statement or judgement, favourable or unfavourable, about a person, object or event.
Job Satisfaction
A positive feeling about one's job resulting from an evaluation of its characteristics.
Values
Broad, enduring beliefs about what is important and desirable, guiding judgements of right and wrong.
Terminal Values
Desirable end-states of existence that a person wants to achieve in their lifetime.
Instrumental Values
Preferable modes of behaviour that serve as the means of achieving terminal values.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Attitude = stable evaluative judgement; 3 components: Cognitive (belief), Affective (feeling), Behavioural (intention to act).
  • Attitude formation: direct experience, social learning, conditioning.
  • Key work attitudes: Job Satisfaction, Job Involvement, Organisational Commitment.
  • Values = broad enduring beliefs about what is desirable; Terminal Values (end-states) vs Instrumental Values (means/behaviours).
  • Beliefs form the cognitive basis on which attitudes are built.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define attitude. Explain its components.
    Show model answer
    An attitude is a relatively stable evaluative judgement about a person, object or event. It has three components: Cognitive (the beliefs held about the object), Affective (the feelings toward it), and Behavioural (the intention to act toward it).
  2. How are attitudes formed? Explain their relevance to work behaviour.
    Show model answer
    Attitudes form through direct experience, social learning from role models, and conditioning/association. At work, attitudes such as job satisfaction, job involvement and organisational commitment influence absenteeism, turnover and citizenship behaviour.
  3. Define values. Differentiate between terminal and instrumental values.
    Show model answer
    Values are broad, enduring beliefs about what is important and desirable. Terminal values are the desirable end-states a person wants to achieve (e.g. a comfortable life), while instrumental values are the preferred modes of behaviour used to achieve those end-states (e.g. honesty, ambition).
  4. What is the relationship between beliefs and attitudes?
    Show model answer
    Beliefs are convictions about the truth of something, and they form the cognitive foundation on which attitudes are built — what a person believes to be true about an object strongly shapes how favourably or unfavourably they come to feel about it.
