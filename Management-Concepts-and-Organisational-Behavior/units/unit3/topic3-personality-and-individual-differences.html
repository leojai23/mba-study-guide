Unit 3Personality and Individual Differences

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Personality shapes how each employee characteristically thinks, feels and behaves. This topic covers what determines personality, the major theories used to describe it, and why individual differences matter for managing people at work.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Meaning and Definition of Personality

Personality is the relatively stable set of psychological characteristics and behavioural tendencies that make a person's pattern of responses to situations distinct from other people.

3.2 Determinants of Personality

FactorInfluence
HeredityGenetic factors inherited from parents, influencing physical traits and some behavioural tendencies.
EnvironmentCulture, family upbringing, social norms and life experiences that shape personality over time.
SituationThe specific context a person is in, which can bring out different aspects of their personality.

3.3 Theories of Personality

  1. Trait Theory: Describes personality as a combination of measurable traits (the "Big Five": Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism) that remain relatively stable across situations.
  2. Psychoanalytic Theory (Freud): Views personality as shaped by unconscious drives, structured into the Id (instinctual desires), Ego (rational mediator), and Superego (internalised moral standards).
  3. Humanistic Theory (Maslow, Rogers): Emphasises personal growth, self-actualisation and the individual's inherent drive toward realising their full potential.
  4. Social-Cognitive Theory (Bandura): Views personality as developing through the continuous interaction between a person, their behaviour, and their environment.

3.4 Personality Traits Relevant to Work

  • Locus of Control: The degree to which people believe they control events affecting them (internal) versus believe outside forces are in control (external).
  • Self-Esteem: The degree to which individuals like or dislike themselves, affecting confidence and risk-taking at work.
  • Self-Monitoring: The ability to adjust one's behaviour to external, situational factors.
  • Risk-Taking Propensity: How willing a person is to take chances, relevant to decision speed and quality.

3.5 Individual Differences

Individual differences refer to the ways people vary from one another — in ability, personality, values, perception and motivation — and why no single management style works equally well for everyone.

Why Individual Differences Matter

  • They explain why employees respond differently to the same job, leadership style, or incentive.
  • They help managers match people to roles that suit their particular strengths.
  • They highlight the need for flexible, individualised approaches to motivation and development.

Individual Differences Influencing Work Behaviour

  • Ability: Intellectual and physical capacity to perform tasks.
  • Personality: Stable traits affecting how a person approaches work and relationships.
  • Perception: How a person interprets their job, colleagues and feedback.
  • Values and Attitudes: What a person considers important, shaping their motivation and job satisfaction.

3. Key Terms

Personality
The relatively stable set of psychological characteristics and behavioural tendencies that distinguish one person's pattern of responses from another's.
Big Five Personality Traits
A trait-theory model of personality: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism.
Locus of Control
The degree to which a person believes they (internal) or outside forces (external) control the events that affect them.
Individual Differences
The ways people vary from one another in ability, personality, values, perception and motivation.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Personality determinants: Heredity, Environment, Situation.
  • Personality theories: Trait (Big Five), Psychoanalytic (Freud's Id/Ego/Superego), Humanistic (Maslow/Rogers), Social-Cognitive (Bandura).
  • Work-relevant traits: Locus of Control, Self-Esteem, Self-Monitoring, Risk-Taking Propensity.
  • Individual differences (ability, personality, perception, values) explain why the same job/style affects different employees differently.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define personality. Explain its determinants.
    Show model answer
    Personality is the relatively stable set of psychological characteristics that make a person's responses distinct from others'. It is determined by heredity (genetic inheritance), environment (culture, upbringing, experience), and the specific situation a person is in.
  2. Explain any two theories of personality.
    Show model answer
    Trait Theory describes personality through measurable, stable traits such as the Big Five (Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism). Psychoanalytic Theory (Freud) views personality as shaped by unconscious drives structured into the Id, Ego and Superego.
  3. What is meant by individual differences? Why are they important at work?
    Show model answer
    Individual differences are the ways people vary in ability, personality, values, perception and motivation. They matter because they explain why employees respond differently to the same job or leadership style, helping managers match people to suitable roles and adopt more flexible management approaches.
