Unit 3Communication: Types, Process and Barriers

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Communication is the thread that connects every other OB topic in this unit. This closing topic covers the communication process, its types, the barriers that distort it, and how to make communication more effective.

2. Detailed Notes

6.1 Meaning and Definition of Communication

Communication is the process of transmitting information and understanding from one person or group to another, so that the meaning intended by the sender is accurately received and understood by the receiver.

6.2 Importance of Communication

  • Coordinates the efforts of individuals and departments toward common goals.
  • Enables managers to plan, organise, direct and control effectively.
  • Builds trust and relationships between management and employees.
  • Supports better, more informed decision-making.

6.3 Process of Communication

  1. Sender: The person who originates and wants to share a message.
  2. Encoding: Converting the idea into words, symbols, or gestures.
  3. Message: The actual content being communicated.
  4. Channel/Medium: The method used to transmit the message (spoken, written, electronic).
  5. Receiver: The person who receives the message.
  6. Decoding: The receiver interpreting the message to extract meaning.
  7. Feedback: The receiver's response, confirming whether the message was understood as intended.
  8. Noise: Anything that distorts the message at any stage of the process.

6.4 Types of Communication

BasisTypes
ModeVerbal (spoken or written words) vs Non-Verbal (body language, tone, facial expressions).
DirectionDownward (manager to subordinate), Upward (subordinate to manager), Horizontal (between peers), and Diagonal (across levels and departments).
FormalityFormal (through official organisational channels) vs Informal (through the grapevine).

6.5 Barriers to Effective Communication

  • Semantic Barriers: Words or symbols meaning different things to different people, causing misunderstanding.
  • Psychological Barriers: Emotional states, prejudice, or distrust that distort how a message is received.
  • Organisational Barriers: Long hierarchies, unclear authority, or information overload within a large structure.
  • Physical/Mechanical Barriers: Noise, distance, or faulty equipment that disrupts transmission.
  • Cultural Barriers: Differences in language, norms and non-verbal cues across cultures.

6.6 Techniques to Overcome Barriers to Communication

  • Use clear, simple language appropriate to the receiver.
  • Actively seek and use feedback to confirm the message was understood.
  • Choose the most appropriate channel for the message and the audience.
  • Practise active listening rather than merely hearing.
  • Be sensitive to the receiver's emotional state and cultural background.
  • Follow up important messages in writing to reduce ambiguity.

3. Key Terms

Communication
The process of transmitting information and understanding from a sender to a receiver so the intended meaning is accurately received.
Encoding
Converting an idea into words, symbols or gestures to be communicated.
Noise (Communication)
Anything that distorts a message at any stage of the communication process.
Semantic Barrier
A communication barrier arising when words or symbols mean different things to different people.
Grapevine
The informal channel through which information flows outside official organisational communication channels.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Communication process: Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Receiver → Decoding → Feedback, with Noise able to distort any stage.
  • Types of communication: Verbal/Non-Verbal; Downward/Upward/Horizontal/Diagonal; Formal/Informal (grapevine).
  • Barriers: Semantic, Psychological, Organisational, Physical/Mechanical, Cultural.
  • Overcoming barriers: clear language, seeking feedback, choosing the right channel, active listening, cultural sensitivity, written follow-up.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain the process of communication.
    Show model answer
    Communication flows from a Sender, who encodes an idea into a Message, sent through a Channel to a Receiver, who decodes it and provides Feedback confirming understanding — with Noise able to distort the message at any stage.
  2. Explain the types of communication.
    Show model answer
    By mode: Verbal (spoken/written) and Non-Verbal (body language, tone). By direction: Downward, Upward, Horizontal and Diagonal. By formality: Formal (official channels) and Informal (the grapevine).
  3. What are the barriers to effective communication?
    Show model answer
    Semantic barriers (words meaning different things to different people), Psychological barriers (emotions, prejudice, distrust), Organisational barriers (long hierarchies, information overload), Physical/Mechanical barriers (noise, distance, faulty equipment), and Cultural barriers (differences in language and norms).
  4. Suggest techniques for making communication effective.
    Show model answer
    Use clear and simple language, actively seek feedback, choose the channel best suited to the message and audience, practise active listening, be sensitive to the receiver's emotional and cultural context, and follow up important messages in writing.
