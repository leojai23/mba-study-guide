Unit 3Perception and Learning

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Perception and learning are two fundamental psychological processes that shape how employees interpret their workplace and how their behaviour changes with experience over time.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Meaning and Definition of Perception

Perception is the process by which individuals select, organise and interpret sensory information to give meaning to their environment. Two people can perceive the same event very differently, because perception is shaped by the perceiver, not just by the raw stimulus itself.

2.2 The Perceptual Process

  1. Receiving Stimuli: The senses receive raw information from the environment.
  2. Selecting: The individual selectively attends to some stimuli and ignores others.
  3. Organising: The selected information is organised into a meaningful pattern.
  4. Interpreting: Meaning is assigned to the organised information, based on the individual's own experience and expectations.
  5. Responding: The individual reacts based on their interpretation.

2.3 Factors Influencing Perception

CategoryFactors
Characteristics of the PerceiverAttitudes, motives, interests, past experience, and expectations.
Characteristics of the TargetNovelty, motion, sounds, size, and other attributes of the thing being perceived.
Characteristics of the SituationTime, work setting, and social context in which the perception occurs.

2.4 Perceptual Errors and Distortions

  • Stereotyping: Judging someone based on the perception of the group they belong to, rather than their individual characteristics.
  • Halo Effect: Forming an overall impression of a person based on a single trait.
  • Selective Perception: Interpreting what is seen selectively, based on the perceiver's own interests, background and attitudes.
  • Projection: Attributing one's own characteristics or feelings to other people.
  • Contrast Effect: Evaluating a person's characteristics in comparison to other people recently encountered, rather than on their own merits.

2.5 Meaning and Definition of Learning

Learning is any relatively permanent change in behaviour that occurs as a result of experience or practice.

2.6 The Learning Process and Theories

TheoryCore Idea
Classical Conditioning (Pavlov)A conditioned stimulus is paired with an unconditioned stimulus until the conditioned stimulus alone triggers the response.
Operant Conditioning (Skinner)Behaviour is a function of its consequences — behaviour followed by a pleasant consequence (reinforcement) is more likely to be repeated.
Social Learning Theory (Bandura)People learn by observing and imitating the behaviour of others (role models), not only through their own direct experience.

2.7 Characteristics of Learning

  • Learning involves change in behaviour, for better or worse.
  • The change must be relatively permanent, distinguishing true learning from temporary fluctuations (e.g. fatigue).
  • Learning occurs through experience, practice, or training.
  • Learning can be shown through actual or potential behaviour change.

3. Key Terms

Perception
The process by which individuals select, organise and interpret sensory information to give meaning to their environment.
Halo Effect
A perceptual error in which an overall impression of a person is formed based on a single trait.
Stereotyping
Judging a person based on the perception of the group they belong to, rather than their own individual characteristics.
Learning
Any relatively permanent change in behaviour that occurs as a result of experience or practice.
Operant Conditioning
A learning theory holding that behaviour is a function of its consequences, with reinforced behaviour more likely to be repeated.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Perceptual process: Receiving Stimuli → Selecting → Organising → Interpreting → Responding.
  • Factors influencing perception: Perceiver's characteristics, Target's characteristics, Situation's characteristics.
  • Perceptual errors: Stereotyping, Halo Effect, Selective Perception, Projection, Contrast Effect.
  • Learning = relatively permanent behaviour change from experience; theories: Classical Conditioning (Pavlov), Operant Conditioning (Skinner), Social Learning Theory (Bandura).

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define perception. Explain the perceptual process.
    Show model answer
    Perception is the process of selecting, organising and interpreting sensory information to give meaning to the environment. The process runs: receiving stimuli, selectively attending to some of them, organising the selected information, interpreting it based on personal experience, and responding.
  2. Explain the factors influencing perception.
    Show model answer
    Factors related to the perceiver (attitudes, motives, past experience), factors related to the target being perceived (novelty, size, motion), and factors related to the situation (time, setting, social context).
  3. Explain common perceptual errors with examples.
    Show model answer
    Stereotyping judges someone by their group rather than individually; the Halo Effect forms a whole impression from one trait; Selective Perception interprets information based on one's own interests; Projection attributes one's own feelings to others; and the Contrast Effect judges someone relative to others recently encountered.
  4. Define learning. Explain any two theories of learning.
    Show model answer
    Learning is any relatively permanent change in behaviour resulting from experience. Classical Conditioning (Pavlov) pairs a conditioned stimulus with an unconditioned stimulus until it alone triggers a response. Operant Conditioning (Skinner) holds that behaviour followed by a pleasant consequence is more likely to be repeated.
