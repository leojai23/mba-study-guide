Unit 2Forecasting: Meaning, Techniques and Methods

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Forecasting predicts future business conditions so that planning can be grounded in realistic expectations. This topic covers why forecasting matters and the qualitative and quantitative techniques used to produce a forecast.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Meaning and Definition of Forecasting

Business forecasting is the process of analysing current and past data, trends and other relevant information to make predictions about future business conditions — such as demand, sales, costs or economic activity — so that managers can plan with more confidence.

2.2 Importance of Forecasting

  • Reduces the uncertainty managers face when setting objectives and plans.
  • Helps anticipate changes in demand, enabling better production and inventory planning.
  • Supports budgeting and financial planning with realistic revenue and cost estimates.
  • Helps identify risks and opportunities early enough to respond to them.

2.3 Essentials of Effective Forecasting

  • Should be based on accurate, relevant and sufficient data.
  • Should use a method appropriate to the type of decision being supported.
  • Should be reviewed and updated periodically as new information becomes available.
  • Should be communicated clearly to the managers who will use it.

2.4 Qualitative Techniques (Opinion Polling / Judgemental Methods)

Qualitative techniques rely on judgement, experience and opinion rather than historical numerical data, and are especially useful when past data is limited or unavailable.

  1. Executive/Jury of Opinion Method: Senior executives pool their judgement and experience to arrive at a forecast.
  2. Sales Force Composite Method: Forecasts are built up from the individual estimates of salespeople who are closest to customers.
  3. Delphi Method: A panel of experts answers questionnaires anonymously over several rounds, converging toward a consensus forecast.
  4. Consumer/Market Survey Method: Customers are directly surveyed about their future purchase intentions.

2.5 Quantitative Techniques (Statistical/Analytical Methods)

  1. Time Series Analysis: Uses historical data over time to identify trends, seasonal patterns and cycles, projecting them forward.
  2. Trend Projection: Fits a mathematical trend line to historical data and extends it into the future.
  3. Regression Analysis: Studies the statistical relationship between the variable being forecast and one or more related variables.
  4. Moving Averages: Averages data over a recent fixed number of periods, smoothing out short-term fluctuations to reveal the underlying trend.
  5. Exponential Smoothing: A weighted-average forecasting technique that gives more weight to recent observations.

2.6 Choosing a Forecasting Method

Qualitative methods are preferred when historical data is scarce, the situation is new, or judgement from experienced people is especially valuable (e.g. launching a new product). Quantitative methods are preferred when sufficient reliable historical data exists and the pattern is expected to continue with reasonable stability.

3. Key Terms

Forecasting
The process of analysing current and past data to predict future business conditions and support planning.
Delphi Method
A qualitative forecasting technique where a panel of experts gives anonymous opinions over several rounds, converging toward a consensus.
Time Series Analysis
A quantitative forecasting technique that projects historical trends, seasonal patterns and cycles into the future.
Moving Average
A forecasting technique that averages data over a recent fixed number of periods to smooth out short-term fluctuations.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Forecasting = predicting future business conditions from current/past data, to support planning.
  • Qualitative (judgemental) methods: Executive/Jury of Opinion, Sales Force Composite, Delphi Method, Consumer/Market Survey.
  • Quantitative (statistical) methods: Time Series Analysis, Trend Projection, Regression Analysis, Moving Averages, Exponential Smoothing.
  • Choose qualitative when data is scarce/situation is new; choose quantitative when reliable historical data exists.
