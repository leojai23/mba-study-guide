Unit 2Decision-Making: Types, Process and Approaches

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Decision-making runs through every management function. This topic covers what a decision is, the types of decisions managers make, the rational decision-making process, and the factors that influence how decisions are actually made.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Concept of Decision-Making

Decision-making is the process of identifying a problem or opportunity and selecting the best course of action from among the available alternatives to resolve or capture it.

4.2 Types of Decisions

BasisTypes
Nature of the problemProgrammed Decisions (routine, recurring, handled by established rules/procedures) vs Non-Programmed Decisions (novel, unstructured, requiring judgement).
Importance/LevelStrategic Decisions (top management, long-term, far-reaching) vs Tactical/Operational Decisions (lower levels, short-term, routine).
Certainty of outcomeDecisions under Certainty, Risk, and Uncertainty, depending on how confidently the outcomes of each alternative can be predicted.
Who decidesIndividual Decisions vs Group Decisions, depending on whether one manager or a group of people makes the choice.

4.3 Rational Approach to Decision-Making (Decision-Making Process)

  1. Identifying the Problem: Recognising that a decision is needed and clearly defining the problem.
  2. Analysing the Problem: Gathering relevant information and understanding the causes and scope of the problem.
  3. Developing Alternatives: Generating a reasonable range of possible courses of action.
  4. Evaluating Alternatives: Weighing each alternative's costs, benefits and risks against the decision criteria.
  5. Selecting the Best Alternative: Choosing the alternative that best satisfies the objectives.
  6. Implementing the Decision: Putting the chosen alternative into action.
  7. Follow-Up and Evaluation: Monitoring the results and taking corrective action if the decision does not produce the expected outcome.

4.4 Factors Affecting Decision-Making

  • Availability of Information: Better, more complete information generally supports better decisions.
  • Time Pressure: Urgent situations may force a faster, less thorough decision process.
  • Organisational Policies: Existing policies can constrain which alternatives are actually available.
  • Risk Tolerance of the Decision-Maker: A manager's personal attitude toward risk shapes which alternatives seem acceptable.
  • Group Dynamics: Where decisions are made in groups, group pressures and politics can influence the outcome.
  • Past Experience: Managers often lean on precedent and experience from similar past decisions.

4.5 Techniques of Decision-Making

  • Marginal Analysis: Comparing the additional cost of an alternative against its additional benefit.
  • Cost-Benefit Analysis: Comparing the total expected costs and benefits of each alternative.
  • Decision Tree Analysis: Mapping out alternatives, possible outcomes and their probabilities in a tree diagram.
  • Brainstorming: A group technique for generating a wide range of alternative ideas without immediate criticism.

3. Key Terms

Decision-Making
The process of identifying a problem or opportunity and selecting the best course of action from the available alternatives.
Programmed Decision
A routine, recurring decision handled through established rules or procedures.
Non-Programmed Decision
A novel, unstructured decision that requires judgement because no established procedure applies.
Decision Tree Analysis
A decision-making technique that maps out alternatives, possible outcomes and their probabilities in a tree diagram.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Decision-making = identifying a problem/opportunity and selecting the best alternative.
  • Types of decisions: Programmed vs Non-Programmed; Strategic vs Tactical/Operational; Certainty/Risk/Uncertainty; Individual vs Group.
  • 7-step rational decision process: Identify Problem → Analyse → Develop Alternatives → Evaluate Alternatives → Select Best → Implement → Follow-Up.
  • Factors affecting decisions: information availability, time pressure, policies, risk tolerance, group dynamics, past experience.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain the types of decisions a manager makes.
    Show model answer
    Programmed decisions are routine and handled by established procedures, while Non-Programmed decisions are novel and require judgement. Decisions also vary by level (Strategic vs Tactical/Operational), by certainty of outcome (Certainty, Risk, Uncertainty), and by who decides (Individual vs Group).
  2. Explain the rational decision-making process.
    Show model answer
    Identifying the problem, analysing it by gathering relevant information, developing a range of alternatives, evaluating each alternative against the decision criteria, selecting the best one, implementing the decision, and finally following up to evaluate whether it produced the expected results.
  3. What factors affect decision-making?
    Show model answer
    Availability and quality of information, time pressure, existing organisational policies, the decision-maker's personal risk tolerance, group dynamics where decisions are made collectively, and the decision-maker's past experience with similar situations.
