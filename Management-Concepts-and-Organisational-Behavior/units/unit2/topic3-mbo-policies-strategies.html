Unit 2Management by Objectives, Policies and Strategies

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

This topic covers Management by Objectives (MBO), a goal-setting system developed by Peter Drucker, along with policies and strategies — two of the main types of plans introduced in Topic 1, examined here in greater depth.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Management by Objectives (MBO) — Introduction

Management by Objectives, a concept popularised by Peter Drucker, is a management system in which managers and employees jointly define objectives, agree on what each person is responsible for, and use those objectives as the standard for measuring and rewarding performance.

3.2 Process of MBO

  1. Setting Organisational Objectives: Top management defines overall objectives for the organisation.
  2. Cascading Objectives: Objectives are cascaded down and translated into objectives for each department and individual.
  3. Joint Goal Setting: Managers and subordinates jointly agree on individual objectives, ensuring they align with and support the higher-level goals.
  4. Action Planning: Specific action plans are developed for achieving the agreed objectives.
  5. Periodic Review: Progress is reviewed periodically, and corrective action is taken where needed.
  6. Performance Appraisal: Final performance is appraised against the originally agreed objectives.

3.3 Features of MBO

  • Objectives are set jointly by superior and subordinate, not imposed unilaterally.
  • Objectives are specific, measurable and time-bound wherever possible.
  • Performance is reviewed periodically against these objectives, with feedback provided.
  • It links individual goals directly to organisational goals.

3.4 Advantages of MBO

  • Improves clarity about what is expected of each employee.
  • Increases motivation and commitment, since employees participate in setting their own goals.
  • Improves communication between managers and subordinates.
  • Provides clear, objective criteria for performance appraisal.

3.5 Disadvantages of MBO

  • Can be time-consuming to administer, with extensive goal-setting and review meetings.
  • May overemphasise quantifiable, short-term objectives at the expense of qualitative, long-term goals.
  • Requires sustained top-management commitment to be effective; it tends to fail if treated as a one-off exercise.

3.6 Policies

A policy is a general statement or guideline that channels thinking and decision-making toward achieving an objective, without specifying the exact action to be taken in every situation.

Types of Policies

  • Originated Policies: Formally established by top management as official guidance.
  • Appealed Policies: Created in response to a specific situation referred upward for a decision, which then becomes precedent.
  • Implied Policies: Not formally stated, but inferred from the organisation's consistent pattern of behaviour over time.
  • Externally Imposed Policies: Imposed by external bodies such as government regulations, trade associations, or trade unions.

Guidelines for Effective Policy-Making

  • Policies should be clearly and simply worded to avoid misinterpretation.
  • Policies should be consistent with organisational objectives and with each other.
  • Policies should be flexible enough to accommodate reasonable exceptions.
  • Policies should be communicated clearly to everyone who needs to apply them.

3.7 Strategy

A strategy is a broad programme for deploying resources to achieve an organisation's objectives, formulated in light of the competitive environment the organisation faces.

Levels of Strategy

LevelFocus
Corporate-Level StrategyWhich businesses/industries the organisation should compete in as a whole.
Business-Level StrategyHow a specific business unit should compete within its industry.
Functional-Level StrategyHow individual departments (marketing, finance, operations) support the business-level strategy.

Characteristics of Strategy

  • Long-term in orientation and significant in its impact on the organisation.
  • Formulated at the top level but implemented across the organisation.
  • Deals with the organisation's relationship to its external competitive environment.
  • Involves a significant commitment of resources that is not easily reversed.

3. Key Terms

Management by Objectives (MBO)
A management system where managers and subordinates jointly set objectives and use them as the standard for measuring and rewarding performance.
Policy
A general statement that guides decision-making toward an objective without specifying the exact action for every situation.
Strategy
A broad programme for deploying resources to achieve objectives, formulated considering the competitive environment.
Corporate-Level Strategy
The level of strategy concerned with which businesses or industries an organisation should compete in as a whole.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • MBO (Peter Drucker): joint goal-setting between manager and subordinate, used as the standard for appraisal.
  • MBO process: Set organisational objectives → cascade down → joint goal setting → action planning → periodic review → performance appraisal.
  • MBO advantages: clarity, motivation, communication, objective appraisal; disadvantages: time-consuming, over-emphasises short-term/quantifiable goals, needs sustained top-management commitment.
  • 4 types of policy: Originated, Appealed, Implied, Externally Imposed.
  • 3 levels of strategy: Corporate, Business, Functional.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What is MBO? Explain its process.
    Show model answer
    Management by Objectives is a system where managers and subordinates jointly define objectives used as the standard for measuring performance. Its process: set organisational objectives, cascade them down to departments/individuals, jointly set individual goals, develop action plans, review progress periodically, and appraise final performance against the agreed objectives.
  2. State the advantages and disadvantages of MBO.
    Show model answer
    Advantages: clarity of expectations, higher motivation from participation, better communication, and objective appraisal criteria. Disadvantages: time-consuming administration, overemphasis on short-term quantifiable goals, and a need for sustained top-management commitment to succeed.
  3. What is a policy? Explain its types.
    Show model answer
    A policy is a general statement guiding decision-making toward an objective. Types include Originated Policies (set formally by top management), Appealed Policies (arising from a referred decision that becomes precedent), Implied Policies (inferred from consistent past behaviour), and Externally Imposed Policies (required by government or other outside bodies).
  4. Explain the levels of strategy in an organisation.
    Show model answer
    Corporate-level strategy decides which businesses or industries the organisation competes in; Business-level strategy decides how a specific business unit competes within its industry; Functional-level strategy decides how individual departments support the business-level strategy.
