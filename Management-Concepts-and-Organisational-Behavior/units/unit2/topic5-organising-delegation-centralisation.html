Unit 2Organising, Delegation and Centralisation/Decentralisation

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Organising turns a plan into a working structure of people, authority and responsibility. This topic covers the organising process, why and how authority is delegated, and the trade-offs between centralised and decentralised decision-making.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Nature and Process of Organising

Organising is the management function of identifying and grouping the work to be done, defining and delegating authority and responsibility, and establishing relationships so that people can work together effectively toward common objectives.

Process of Organising

  1. Identifying and Dividing Work: Breaking the total work of the organisation into manageable activities.
  2. Grouping Activities (Departmentation): Clustering related activities into departments or units.
  3. Assigning Duties: Allocating specific activities to specific individuals or positions.
  4. Delegating Authority: Granting the authority necessary for each person to carry out their assigned duties.
  5. Establishing Relationships: Defining reporting lines so that authority and responsibility are clear throughout the structure.

5.2 Importance of Organising

  • Facilitates administration by clarifying who is responsible for what.
  • Facilitates growth and diversification by providing a structure that can expand.
  • Ensures optimum use of resources and avoids duplication of effort.
  • Encourages efficient coordination between departments.

5.3 Delegation of Authority

Delegation is the process by which a manager assigns part of their work and the authority needed to accomplish it to a subordinate, while still remaining accountable for the overall outcome.

Elements of Delegation

  • Authority: The right to make decisions and direct the work of others, which is delegated.
  • Responsibility: The obligation to perform the assigned task, which is created when a subordinate accepts a delegation.
  • Accountability: The ultimate answerability for results, which can never itself be delegated away — the manager remains accountable even after delegating authority and responsibility.

Barriers to Effective Delegation

  • A manager's reluctance to let go of control ("I can do it better myself" attitude).
  • Lack of confidence in a subordinate's ability or willingness to accept responsibility.
  • Absence of clear controls that would let a manager safely delegate and still monitor results.
  • Fear, on the subordinate's side, of criticism for mistakes made while exercising delegated authority.

5.4 Centralisation and Decentralisation

Centralisation is the systematic retention of decision-making authority at the upper levels of management. Decentralisation is the systematic delegation of decision-making authority throughout the lower levels of the organisation.

BasisCentralisationDecentralisation
Decision-makingConcentrated at the topSpread across levels
Speed of local decisionsSlower (must refer upward)Faster (decided locally)
UniformityEasier to maintainHarder to maintain
SuitsSmall organisations, situations needing tight controlLarge, diversified organisations needing local responsiveness

Advantages of Decentralisation

  • Faster decisions closer to where problems actually occur.
  • Develops decision-making skills in middle and lower managers.
  • Reduces the burden on top management, freeing it for strategic matters.

Disadvantages of Decentralisation

  • Can lead to a lack of uniformity in decisions across the organisation.
  • Requires capable managers at lower levels, which takes time and investment to develop.
  • Can make coordination across units more difficult.

3. Key Terms

Organising
The management function of dividing work, grouping activities, and establishing authority relationships so people can work together toward common objectives.
Delegation of Authority
The process by which a manager assigns part of their work and the necessary authority to a subordinate, while remaining accountable for the result.
Accountability
The ultimate answerability for results, which cannot itself be delegated away even when authority and responsibility are.
Centralisation
The systematic retention of decision-making authority at the upper levels of management.
Decentralisation
The systematic delegation of decision-making authority throughout the lower levels of an organisation.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Organising process: Divide Work → Group Activities (Departmentation) → Assign Duties → Delegate Authority → Establish Relationships.
  • Delegation's 3 elements: Authority (delegated), Responsibility (created), Accountability (never delegated away).
  • Barriers to delegation: manager's reluctance, lack of confidence in subordinate, no clear controls, subordinate's fear of criticism.
  • Centralisation = authority concentrated at top; Decentralisation = authority spread to lower levels; decentralisation gives speed/local responsiveness at the cost of uniformity.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain the process of organising.
    Show model answer
    Identifying and dividing the total work into activities, grouping related activities into departments, assigning specific duties to positions, delegating the authority needed to carry them out, and establishing clear reporting relationships.
  2. What is delegation of authority? Explain its elements.
    Show model answer
    Delegation is the process by which a manager assigns part of their work and the authority to accomplish it to a subordinate. Its elements are Authority (the right delegated), Responsibility (the obligation created in the subordinate), and Accountability (the ultimate answerability, which the manager can never fully delegate away).
  3. What are the barriers to effective delegation?
    Show model answer
    A manager's reluctance to give up control, lack of confidence in a subordinate's ability, absence of clear controls to monitor delegated work, and the subordinate's fear of criticism for mistakes.
  4. Differentiate between centralisation and decentralisation.
    Show model answer
    Centralisation concentrates decision-making authority at the top of the organisation, giving tighter control and uniformity but slower local decisions. Decentralisation spreads authority to lower levels, giving faster, more locally responsive decisions and developing lower-level managers, at the cost of uniformity and coordination ease.
