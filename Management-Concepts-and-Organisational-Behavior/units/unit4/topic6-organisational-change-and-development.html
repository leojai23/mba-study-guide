Unit 4Organisational Change and Development

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

This closing topic of Unit 4 covers how organisations deliberately manage change, and the broader discipline of Organisational Development (OD) used to build an organisation's long-term capacity to adapt.

2. Detailed Notes

6.1 Meaning and Definition of Organisational Change

Organisational change is any alteration in people, structure, or technology within an organisation, undertaken to improve its effectiveness or adapt to a changing environment.

6.2 Forces for Change

  • External Forces: Changes in technology, market competition, government regulation, and economic conditions.
  • Internal Forces: Changes in organisational strategy, workforce composition, employee attitudes, and new equipment or processes.

6.3 Lewin's Three-Step Model of Change

  1. Unfreezing: Preparing the organisation for change by overcoming resistance and individual/group inertia in the current state.
  2. Changing (Moving): Actually implementing the change — moving the organisation toward the desired new state.
  3. Refreezing: Stabilising the new state by reinforcing the new behaviours, so the change becomes the new normal rather than reverting.

6.4 Resistance to Change

Why People Resist Change

  • Fear of the unknown and loss of job security.
  • Habit and comfort with established routines.
  • Concern over loss of status, power, or established relationships.
  • Lack of trust in management's reasons for the change.

Overcoming Resistance

  • Communicating openly about the reasons for and nature of the change.
  • Involving affected employees in planning and implementing the change.
  • Providing training and support to build confidence in new ways of working.
  • Negotiating and, where appropriate, offering incentives to ease the transition.

6.5 Organisational Development (OD)

Organisational Development is a planned, systematic effort, supported by top management, that uses behavioural-science knowledge to improve an organisation's health and effectiveness through planned interventions in its processes.

Characteristics of OD

  • Planned and long-term, not a one-time quick fix.
  • Oriented toward the whole organisational system, not just isolated parts.
  • Relies on collaboration between change agents and organisation members.
  • Draws on behavioural science theory and research.

Common OD Interventions

  • Survey Feedback: Collecting employee attitude data and feeding it back to work groups to diagnose and solve problems.
  • Team Building: Activities to improve trust, openness and collaboration within work teams.
  • Process Consultation: An outside consultant helps managers perceive and understand the process events happening around them.
  • Sensitivity Training: Unstructured group sessions aimed at increasing members' insight into their own and others' behaviour.

3. Key Terms

Organisational Change
Any alteration in an organisation's people, structure, or technology, undertaken to improve effectiveness or adapt to the environment.
Lewin's Three-Step Model
A change model consisting of Unfreezing (preparing for change), Changing (implementing it), and Refreezing (stabilising the new state).
Organisational Development (OD)
A planned, systematic, top-management-supported effort using behavioural-science knowledge to improve organisational health and effectiveness.
Survey Feedback
An OD intervention that collects employee attitude data and feeds it back to work groups to diagnose and solve problems.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Organisational change = alteration in people/structure/technology to improve effectiveness or adapt to environment; forces: External (tech, market, regulation) and Internal (strategy, workforce, process).
  • Lewin's 3-step model: Unfreezing → Changing (Moving) → Refreezing.
  • Resistance to change: fear of unknown, habit, loss of status/power, lack of trust; overcome via communication, involvement, training, negotiation.
  • OD = planned, systematic, top-management-supported, whole-system, behavioural-science-based effort; interventions: Survey Feedback, Team Building, Process Consultation, Sensitivity Training.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain Lewin's three-step model of change.
    Show model answer
    Unfreezing prepares the organisation by overcoming resistance and inertia in the current state; Changing (Moving) implements the actual change toward the desired new state; Refreezing stabilises the new state by reinforcing new behaviours so the change sticks.
  2. Why do employees resist change? How can resistance be overcome?
    Show model answer
    Employees resist change due to fear of the unknown, comfort with habit, concern over losing status or power, and lack of trust in management. Resistance can be reduced through open communication, involving employees in the change process, providing training and support, and negotiating or offering incentives.
  3. What is Organisational Development (OD)? Explain its characteristics.
    Show model answer
    OD is a planned, systematic effort supported by top management, using behavioural-science knowledge to improve organisational effectiveness. It is long-term rather than a quick fix, focused on the whole system, collaborative between change agents and members, and grounded in behavioural science.
  4. Explain any two OD interventions.
    Show model answer
    Survey Feedback collects employee attitude data and feeds it back to work groups to diagnose and solve problems. Team Building uses structured activities to improve trust, openness and collaboration within a work team.
