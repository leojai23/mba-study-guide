Unit 4Groups and Teams: Definition, Difference and Types

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Unit 4 shifts focus from the individual to the group. This opening topic defines what a group and a team are, how they differ, and the various types of teams found in organisations.

2. Detailed Notes

1.1 Meaning and Definition of a Group

A group is two or more individuals, interacting and interdependent, who have come together to achieve particular objectives.

Types of Groups

  • Formal Groups: Deliberately created by the organisation to perform specific tasks (e.g. a department, a project committee).
  • Informal Groups: Formed naturally by members for social or psychological reasons, not officially structured by the organisation.
  • Command Group: A formal group defined by the organisation chart, consisting of a manager and their direct subordinates.
  • Task Group: A formal group of people working together to complete a specific job or project, which may cross reporting lines.
  • Interest Group: An informal group that forms around members sharing a common interest or goal.
  • Friendship Group: An informal group that forms because members share common characteristics or simply enjoy each other's company.

1.2 Meaning and Definition of a Team

A team is a small number of people with complementary skills who are committed to a common purpose, a set of performance goals, and an approach for which they hold themselves mutually accountable.

1.3 Difference Between Groups and Teams

BasisGroupTeam
GoalShare information, members have individual goalsCollective performance, shared common goals
AccountabilityIndividual accountabilityIndividual and mutual accountability
SkillsMay be random or overlappingComplementary skills, deliberately combined
SynergyNeutral, sometimes negativePositive synergy — the whole exceeds the sum of individual efforts
LeadershipOften a single strong leaderShared or rotating leadership roles

1.4 Types of Teams

  1. Problem-Solving Teams: Employees from the same department meeting to discuss ways to improve quality, efficiency or the work environment.
  2. Self-Managed (Self-Directed) Teams: Teams that operate without a manager and take on traditional managerial responsibilities such as planning and scheduling their own work.
  3. Cross-Functional Teams: Teams made up of employees from different departments working together on a common task.
  4. Virtual Teams: Teams that use technology to work together despite being physically dispersed, communicating across distance.
  5. Project Teams: Temporary teams assembled for the duration of a specific project and disbanded once it is complete.

3. Key Terms

Group
Two or more individuals, interacting and interdependent, who come together to achieve particular objectives.
Team
A small number of people with complementary skills, committed to a common purpose and goals, holding themselves mutually accountable.
Self-Managed Team
A team that operates without a manager, taking on traditional managerial responsibilities such as planning its own work.
Cross-Functional Team
A team made up of employees from different departments, working together on a common task.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Group = 2+ interacting, interdependent individuals pursuing objectives; types: Formal (Command, Task) and Informal (Interest, Friendship).
  • Team = small group with complementary skills, common purpose, mutual accountability, and positive synergy.
  • Group vs Team: individual goals/accountability vs collective goals/mutual accountability; neutral synergy vs positive synergy.
  • Types of teams: Problem-Solving, Self-Managed, Cross-Functional, Virtual, Project.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define a group. Explain its types.
    Show model answer
    A group is two or more interacting, interdependent individuals who come together to achieve particular objectives. Formal groups (Command groups defined by the org chart, Task groups formed for a specific job) are deliberately created by the organisation, while Informal groups (Interest groups, Friendship groups) form naturally for social reasons.
  2. Differentiate between a group and a team.
    Show model answer
    A group's members often pursue individual goals with individual accountability and neutral synergy, while a team shares a common goal, holds mutual accountability, combines complementary skills deliberately, and produces positive synergy where the collective output exceeds the sum of individual efforts.
  3. Explain the various types of teams found in organisations.
    Show model answer
    Problem-Solving Teams discuss improvements within a department; Self-Managed Teams operate without a manager, handling their own planning; Cross-Functional Teams bring together people from different departments; Virtual Teams use technology to collaborate across distance; and Project Teams are temporary, assembled for a specific project's duration.
