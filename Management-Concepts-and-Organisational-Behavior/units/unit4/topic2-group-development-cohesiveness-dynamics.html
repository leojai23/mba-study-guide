Unit 4Stages of Group Development, Cohesiveness and Dynamics

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Groups don't function effectively from the moment they are formed — they develop through predictable stages. This topic covers that development process, what makes a group cohesive, and the dynamics that shape how a group actually behaves.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Stages of Group Development (Bruce Tuckman)

  1. Forming: Members get to know each other, testing boundaries and establishing ground rules; characterised by uncertainty about the group's purpose and structure.
  2. Storming: Conflict emerges as members resist the constraints the group places on their individuality, and disagreements surface over leadership and direction.
  3. Norming: The group develops close relationships, cohesiveness increases, and norms for acceptable behaviour emerge and are accepted.
  4. Performing: The group structure is fully functional and accepted; energy has moved from getting to know one another to actually performing the task.
  5. Adjourning: For temporary groups/teams, this final stage involves wrapping up activities and disbanding, rather than high performance.

Not every group experiences these stages in strict sequence — some move back and forth between storming and norming as new challenges or members arise.

2.2 Group Cohesiveness

Group cohesiveness is the degree to which members are attracted to the group and motivated to remain part of it.

Factors Affecting Cohesiveness

  • Time spent together and shared experiences.
  • Severity of initiation into the group.
  • Group size (smaller groups tend to be more cohesive).
  • External threats, which often increase internal cohesiveness.
  • Past success, which reinforces members' desire to stay in the group.

Consequences of Cohesiveness

Highly cohesive groups with goals aligned to the organisation's goals tend to be more productive. However, a highly cohesive group whose goals are not aligned with the organisation's goals can actually become less productive, since the group's energy is directed away from organisational objectives. Excessive cohesiveness can also lead to groupthink — a tendency to suppress dissenting views in favour of consensus and harmony.

2.3 Group Dynamics

Group dynamics refers to the pattern of interactions, processes and forces operating within and between groups — how members influence and are influenced by each other, including roles, norms, status, and communication patterns.

Elements of Group Dynamics

  • Roles: The set of expected behaviour patterns attributed to a particular position within the group.
  • Norms: The acceptable standards of behaviour shared by group members.
  • Status: The socially defined position or rank given to a group or its members by others.
  • Group Size: Affects overall group behaviour — larger groups are good for gathering diverse input, smaller groups for taking action quickly.
  • Group Composition: The mix of skills, backgrounds and characteristics among members, which affects group performance.

3. Key Terms

Tuckman's Five Stages
The stages of group development: Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing, and Adjourning.
Group Cohesiveness
The degree to which members are attracted to a group and motivated to remain part of it.
Groupthink
A tendency within a highly cohesive group to suppress dissenting views in favour of consensus and harmony.
Group Dynamics
The pattern of interactions, processes and forces operating within and between groups.
Norms
The acceptable standards of behaviour that are shared and enforced by members of a group.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Tuckman's 5 stages: Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning.
  • Group cohesiveness = attraction to and motivation to remain in a group; factors: time together, initiation severity, size, external threats, past success.
  • Highly cohesive + goals aligned with organisation = high productivity; highly cohesive + misaligned goals = can reduce productivity; excess cohesiveness risks groupthink.
  • Group dynamics elements: Roles, Norms, Status, Group Size, Group Composition.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain the stages of group development.
    Show model answer
    Forming (members get acquainted, uncertain about purpose), Storming (conflict over leadership and direction), Norming (cohesiveness builds, norms accepted), Performing (fully functional, task-focused), and Adjourning (wrapping up and disbanding, for temporary groups).
  2. What is group cohesiveness? What factors affect it?
    Show model answer
    Group cohesiveness is the degree to which members are attracted to the group and motivated to stay in it. It is affected by time spent together, the severity of initiation, group size, external threats, and the group's past success.
  3. What is groupthink? Why is it a risk?
    Show model answer
    Groupthink is the tendency within a highly cohesive group to suppress dissenting opinions in favour of consensus and harmony. It is a risk because it can lead to poor decisions, since alternative viewpoints and critical evaluation are discouraged.
  4. Explain the key elements of group dynamics.
    Show model answer
    Roles (expected behaviour patterns for a position), Norms (shared standards of acceptable behaviour), Status (socially defined rank within the group), Group Size, and Group Composition (the mix of skills and backgrounds among members).
