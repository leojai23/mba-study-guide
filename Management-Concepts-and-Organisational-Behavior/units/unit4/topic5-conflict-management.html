Unit 4Conflict: Concept, Sources, Types and Management

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Wherever people and groups with different goals interact, conflict is inevitable. This topic covers what conflict is, where it comes from, its types and levels, and how it can be managed constructively.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Concept and Meaning of Conflict

Conflict is a process that begins when one party perceives that another party has negatively affected, or is about to negatively affect, something the first party cares about.

5.2 Sources of Conflict

  • Communication Factors: Misunderstandings, semantic difficulties, and insufficient information exchange.
  • Structural Factors: Competition for limited resources, interdependence between departments, and ambiguous jurisdictions.
  • Personal Factors: Differences in values, personality clashes, and individual goals that differ from others'.

5.3 Types/Levels of Conflict

LevelDescription
Intrapersonal ConflictConflict within a single individual, often due to competing goals or roles.
Interpersonal ConflictConflict between two or more individuals.
Intragroup ConflictConflict among members within the same group or team.
Intergroup ConflictConflict between two or more groups or departments.
Organisational ConflictConflict that is broader and involves multiple groups across the organisation, often over policy or strategic direction.

Functional vs Dysfunctional Conflict

Functional conflict supports the group's goals and improves performance, for example by surfacing better ideas through healthy debate. Dysfunctional conflict hinders group performance, for example by damaging relationships or distracting from the task at hand.

5.4 The Conflict Process

  1. Potential Opposition/Incompatibility: The underlying conditions (communication, structure, personal factors) that create the possibility for conflict.
  2. Cognition and Personalisation: One or both parties become aware of and emotionally invested in the conflict.
  3. Intentions: The parties decide how to respond — competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, or accommodating.
  4. Behaviour: The conflict becomes visible through statements, actions and reactions.
  5. Outcomes: The conflict is resolved (or not), producing functional or dysfunctional results for the group.

5.5 Management of Conflict

StyleWhen Appropriate
CompetingQuick, decisive action is needed, or on important issues where unpopular action must be taken.
CollaboratingBoth parties' concerns are too important to compromise; aims for a win-win solution.
CompromisingGoals are moderately important but not worth the disruption of more assertive approaches.
AvoidingThe issue is trivial, or more information is needed before acting.
AccommodatingPreserving harmony matters more than the issue itself, or when one realises they are wrong.

3. Key Terms

Conflict
A process that begins when one party perceives that another party has negatively affected, or is about to affect, something the first party cares about.
Functional Conflict
Conflict that supports a group's goals and improves its performance.
Dysfunctional Conflict
Conflict that hinders a group's performance, for instance by damaging relationships or distracting from the task.
Intergroup Conflict
Conflict occurring between two or more groups or departments within an organisation.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Conflict = process beginning when one party perceives another has negatively affected something they care about.
  • Sources: Communication factors, Structural factors (limited resources, interdependence), Personal factors (values, personality).
  • Levels: Intrapersonal, Interpersonal, Intragroup, Intergroup, Organisational.
  • Conflict process: Potential Opposition → Cognition/Personalisation → Intentions → Behaviour → Outcomes.
  • 5 conflict-handling styles: Competing, Collaborating, Compromising, Avoiding, Accommodating.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What is conflict? Explain its sources.
    Show model answer
    Conflict is a process beginning when one party perceives another has negatively affected something they care about. Its sources include communication factors (misunderstandings), structural factors (competition for resources, interdependence), and personal factors (differing values and personalities).
  2. Explain the levels/types of conflict.
    Show model answer
    Intrapersonal (within an individual), Interpersonal (between individuals), Intragroup (within a group), Intergroup (between groups), and Organisational (broader, involving multiple groups over policy or strategy).
  3. Differentiate between functional and dysfunctional conflict.
    Show model answer
    Functional conflict supports a group's goals and improves performance, for example through healthy debate that surfaces better ideas. Dysfunctional conflict hinders performance, for example by damaging relationships or distracting the group from its task.
  4. Explain the styles of conflict management.
    Show model answer
    Competing suits urgent, important issues needing quick action; Collaborating seeks a win-win when both parties' concerns matter; Compromising suits moderately important goals; Avoiding suits trivial issues or when more information is needed; and Accommodating suits situations where preserving harmony matters more than winning the point.
