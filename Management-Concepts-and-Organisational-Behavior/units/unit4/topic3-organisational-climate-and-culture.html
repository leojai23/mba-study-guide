Unit 4Organisational Climate and Culture

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Organisational climate and culture together shape how things are done in a workplace, influencing everything from employee morale to strategic execution.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Meaning and Definition of Organisational Climate

Organisational climate is employees' shared perception of the work environment — how it feels to work in the organisation at a given point in time. It reflects relatively short-term, surface-level conditions such as morale, communication quality, and management style.

3.2 Dimensions of Organisational Climate

  • Structure: The degree of formality, rules and red tape employees perceive.
  • Responsibility: The degree of autonomy employees feel they have.
  • Reward: How fairly employees feel they are recognised and compensated.
  • Warmth and Support: The perceived friendliness and helpfulness among colleagues and from management.
  • Conflict: How openly disagreements are expressed and resolved.

3.3 Meaning and Definition of Organisational Culture

Organisational culture is the shared system of values, beliefs, assumptions and norms that governs how members of an organisation think, feel and behave. Unlike climate, culture is deeper, more stable, and develops over a much longer period.

3.4 Characteristics of Organisational Culture

  • Shared: Held in common by most members of the organisation.
  • Learned: Transmitted to new members through socialisation.
  • Stable: Resistant to change once established.
  • Implicit: Often operates below conscious awareness, guiding behaviour without members realising it.

3.5 Difference Between Climate and Culture

BasisOrganisational ClimateOrganisational Culture
NatureSurface-level, perceptualDeep-rooted, foundational
StabilityRelatively short-term, can shift quicklyLong-term, changes slowly
MeasurementMeasured through employee perception surveysInferred from shared values, stories, rituals and artefacts
ScopeCan vary across departments within one cultureUsually more uniform across the whole organisation

3.6 Factors Affecting Organisational Climate and Culture

  • Leadership style and the example set by top management.
  • Organisational structure and the degree of formalisation.
  • Communication practices and transparency.
  • Reward and recognition systems.
  • The organisation's founding history and the values of its founders.

3.7 Building and Maintaining a Positive Climate and Culture

  • Leaders modelling the desired values consistently in their own behaviour.
  • Recognising and rewarding behaviour consistent with the desired culture.
  • Communicating openly and regularly with employees at every level.
  • Using onboarding and training to socialise new members into the culture.

3. Key Terms

Organisational Climate
Employees' shared perception of the work environment at a given point in time — a relatively short-term, surface-level condition.
Organisational Culture
The shared system of values, beliefs, assumptions and norms that governs how members of an organisation think, feel and behave.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Climate = shared perception of the work environment (surface-level, short-term); Culture = shared values/beliefs/norms (deep, long-term).
  • Climate dimensions: Structure, Responsibility, Reward, Warmth/Support, Conflict.
  • Culture characteristics: Shared, Learned, Stable, Implicit.
  • Climate vs Culture: short-term/surveyed vs long-term/inferred from values and artefacts.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define organisational climate. What are its dimensions?
    Show model answer
    Organisational climate is employees' shared perception of their work environment at a given time. Its dimensions include Structure, Responsibility, Reward, Warmth and Support, and Conflict.
  2. Define organisational culture. Explain its characteristics.
    Show model answer
    Organisational culture is the shared system of values, beliefs, assumptions and norms governing how members think, feel and behave. It is shared across members, learned through socialisation, stable over time, and often implicit, operating below conscious awareness.
  3. Differentiate between organisational climate and culture.
    Show model answer
    Climate is a surface-level, short-term perception of the work environment that can vary across departments, while culture is a deep-rooted, long-term, usually organisation-wide system of shared values and beliefs that is harder and slower to change.
