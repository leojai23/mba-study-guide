Unit 4Leadership: Styles, Approaches, Power and Politics

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Leadership determines how well a group's or team's efforts are directed, while power and politics describe the underlying currents that shape influence within an organisation. This topic covers all three together.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Meaning and Definition of Leadership

Leadership is the ability to influence a group toward the achievement of a vision or set of goals.

4.2 Leadership Styles

StyleDescription
AutocraticThe leader makes decisions unilaterally, with little input from subordinates.
Democratic (Participative)The leader involves subordinates in decision-making, seeking their input before deciding.
Laissez-Faire (Free-Rein)The leader gives subordinates near-complete freedom to make decisions and complete work on their own.
TransactionalThe leader motivates through a system of rewards and punishments tied to performance.
TransformationalThe leader inspires and motivates followers by appealing to a shared vision, higher ideals and values, encouraging followers to exceed their own self-interest for the group.

4.3 Approaches/Theories to Leadership

  1. Trait Theory: Early approach assuming effective leaders possess certain inborn personal traits (intelligence, confidence, integrity).
  2. Behavioural Theory: Focuses on what leaders actually do — their observable behaviour — rather than their traits (e.g. task-oriented vs people-oriented behaviour).
  3. Contingency/Situational Theory: Holds that the most effective leadership style depends on the specific situation — the task, the followers, and the environment.
  4. Transformational/Charismatic Theory: Focuses on leaders who inspire extraordinary commitment and performance by connecting followers to a compelling vision.

4.4 Power

Power is the capacity to influence the behaviour of others so that they act in accordance with one's wishes.

Sources/Bases of Power (French and Raven)

  • Legitimate Power: Derived from a person's formal position or role in the organisation.
  • Reward Power: Derived from the ability to give valued rewards to others.
  • Coercive Power: Derived from the ability to punish or withhold something of value.
  • Expert Power: Derived from specialised knowledge or skill that others need.
  • Referent Power: Derived from being liked, admired, or respected by others.

4.5 Organisational Politics

Organisational politics refers to activities carried out to acquire, develop and use power and other resources to obtain one's preferred outcomes in situations where there is uncertainty or disagreement. It is not inherently negative — it becomes problematic mainly when it is used purely for self-interest at the expense of organisational goals.

Why Organisational Politics Occurs

  • Limited resources that different individuals or departments compete for.
  • Ambiguous goals or unclear performance criteria that leave room for interpretation and influence.
  • Organisational change, which creates uncertainty and opportunities to gain or lose influence.
  • Differences in values, goals and perspectives across departments and individuals.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Leadership styles: Autocratic, Democratic/Participative, Laissez-Faire, Transactional, Transformational.
  • Leadership approaches: Trait Theory, Behavioural Theory, Contingency/Situational Theory, Transformational/Charismatic Theory.
  • 5 bases of power (French and Raven): Legitimate, Reward, Coercive, Expert, Referent.
  • Organisational politics occurs due to limited resources, ambiguous goals, organisational change, and differing values — not inherently negative.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain the various styles of leadership.
    Show model answer
    Autocratic (unilateral decisions), Democratic/Participative (involves subordinates), Laissez-Faire (gives subordinates freedom), Transactional (motivates through rewards/punishment), and Transformational (inspires through vision and higher ideals).
  2. Explain the approaches to the study of leadership.
    Show model answer
    Trait Theory looks at inborn personal traits of effective leaders; Behavioural Theory looks at what leaders actually do; Contingency/Situational Theory holds that the best style depends on the situation; and Transformational/Charismatic Theory focuses on leaders who inspire extraordinary commitment through a compelling vision.
  3. Explain the bases of power identified by French and Raven.
    Show model answer
    Legitimate Power from formal position, Reward Power from the ability to give rewards, Coercive Power from the ability to punish, Expert Power from specialised knowledge, and Referent Power from being liked or admired by others.
  4. What is organisational politics? Why does it occur?
    Show model answer
    Organisational politics is the use of power and resources to obtain preferred outcomes amid uncertainty or disagreement. It occurs due to limited resources, ambiguous goals, organisational change, and differing values and perspectives across the organisation; it is not inherently negative unless used purely for self-interest.
