Unit 1Classical Era — Fayol's Administrative Management & Weber's Bureaucracy

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Alongside Taylor's shop-floor focus, Henri Fayol developed principles for managing the whole organisation from the top down, while Max Weber described the ideal rational-legal bureaucracy. Together they complete the Classical Era's picture of formal organisational structure.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Henri Fayol — Administrative Management

Henri Fayol, a French mining engineer and Managing Director of a large mining company, approached management from the top management's point of view — the opposite of Taylor's shop-floor focus. He explained his ideas in "Administration Industrielle et Generale," published in French in 1916 and later translated into English in 1929 as "General and Industrial Management."

4.2 Fayol's Functions of Management

Fayol identified five functions of management: Planning, Organisation, Command, Co-ordination, and Control — the forerunner of the Planning/Organising/Staffing/Directing/Controlling framework used today.

4.3 Fayol's 14 Principles of Management

  1. Division of Labour: Specialisation of work increases efficiency and output.
  2. Authority and Responsibility: Authority (the right to give orders) and responsibility (accountability for results) must go hand in hand.
  3. Discipline: Obedience and respect for the organisation's rules and agreements are essential for smooth functioning.
  4. Unity of Command: Every employee should receive orders from only one superior to avoid confusion.
  5. Unity of Direction: Activities with the same objective should be directed by one manager using one plan.
  6. Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest: The interest of the organisation should prevail over the interest of any individual employee.
  7. Fair Remuneration to Employees: Pay should be fair, satisfying both employees and the organisation.
  8. Centralisation and Decentralisation: The degree to which decision-making authority is concentrated or dispersed should be balanced appropriately for the situation.
  9. Scalar Chain: A clear, unbroken chain of authority runs from the top of the organisation to the bottom (though a "gang plank" can shortcut communication between peers at the same level when urgent).
  10. Order: There should be a fixed, proper place for every person and every thing in the organisation.
  11. Equity: Managers should treat employees with kindness and justice, applying rules fairly.
  12. Stability of Tenure of Personnel: High employee turnover is inefficient; management should minimise unnecessary staff turnover.
  13. Initiative: Employees should be encouraged to develop and carry out their own plans and ideas within their area of work.
  14. Esprit de Corps: Management should promote team spirit, unity and harmony among employees.

4.4 Max Weber — Bureaucracy

Max Weber, a German sociologist, contributed the theory of Bureaucracy as the ideal, most rational and efficient form of organisation for large-scale administration.

4.5 Features of Bureaucracy

  1. Division of Work: Jobs are broken down into specialised tasks, with a clear division of labour.
  2. Hierarchy of Positions: A clear, well-defined hierarchy of authority exists, with each lower position controlled and supervised by a higher one.
  3. Rules and Regulations: Formal rules and procedures govern operations and decisions, ensuring consistency.
  4. Impersonal Conduct: Members conduct their official business impersonally, without regard to personal feelings or relationships; another "depersonalisation" aspect means members do not have a personal claim on their positions — they belong to the organisation, not to the individual.
  5. Staffing: Employees are hired and promoted based on technical competence and qualifications, not favouritism.

4.6 Weber's Three Types of Authority

TypeBasis
Traditional AuthorityDerived from custom, tradition, or hereditary position (e.g. a monarchy or family business).
Charismatic AuthorityDerived from the exceptional personal qualities or charisma of an individual leader.
Rational-Legal AuthorityDerived from a formal system of legally established rules and office — the basis of modern bureaucratic organisations.

3. Key Terms

Fayol's 14 Principles of Management
General guidelines for administration proposed by Henri Fayol, covering division of labour, authority, discipline, unity of command, and ten others.
Scalar Chain
The unbroken chain of authority and communication running from the top of an organisation to the bottom.
Esprit de Corps
Fayol's principle that management should promote team spirit, unity and harmony among employees.
Bureaucracy
Max Weber's ideal form of rational, rule-based organisation characterised by division of work, hierarchy, formal rules, impersonal conduct, and merit-based staffing.
Rational-Legal Authority
Authority derived from a formal system of legally established rules and office, the basis of bureaucratic organisations.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Fayol studied management from the top-management view; his 5 functions: Planning, Organisation, Command, Co-ordination, Control.
  • Fayol's 14 principles: Division of Labour, Authority & Responsibility, Discipline, Unity of Command, Unity of Direction, Subordination of Individual to General Interest, Fair Remuneration, Centralisation/Decentralisation, Scalar Chain, Order, Equity, Stability of Tenure, Initiative, Esprit de Corps.
  • Weber's Bureaucracy features: Division of Work, Hierarchy of Positions, Rules & Regulations, Impersonal Conduct, Merit-based Staffing.
  • Weber's 3 types of authority: Traditional, Charismatic, Rational-Legal (basis of bureaucracy).

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain any five of Fayol's 14 principles of management.
    Show model answer
    Division of Labour (specialisation increases efficiency); Unity of Command (one superior per employee); Scalar Chain (unbroken chain of authority top to bottom); Equity (fair, kind treatment of employees); and Esprit de Corps (promoting team spirit and unity).
  2. Differentiate between the approaches of Taylor and Fayol.
    Show model answer
    Taylor approached management from the shop floor upward, focusing on worker efficiency through scientific study of tasks. Fayol approached management from the top management's point of view, focusing on general administrative principles applicable to managing the whole organisation.
  3. Explain the features of bureaucracy as described by Max Weber.
    Show model answer
    Division of work into specialised tasks, a clear hierarchy of positions with each level supervised by the one above, formal rules and regulations governing decisions, impersonal conduct of official business, and staffing based on technical competence rather than favouritism.
  4. Explain Weber's three types of authority.
    Show model answer
    Traditional Authority, based on custom or hereditary position; Charismatic Authority, based on a leader's exceptional personal qualities; and Rational-Legal Authority, based on a formal system of legally established rules and office, which underpins modern bureaucratic organisations.
