Unit 1Neo-Classical Era — Mayo & the Hawthorne Experiments

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

The Hawthorne Experiments, led by Elton Mayo at the Western Electric Company, revealed that social and psychological factors — not just physical working conditions — strongly influence worker productivity, launching the Neo-Classical, human-relations era of management thought.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Background to the Hawthorne Experiments

The Hawthorne Experiments were a series of studies conducted at the Western Electric Company's Hawthorne Works in the late 1920s and early 1930s, initially to study the effect of physical working conditions (like lighting) on worker productivity. Elton Mayo and his research team led the studies, and the surprising results shifted management thinking away from Scientific Management's purely mechanical view of workers.

5.2 The Experiments

  1. Illumination Experiments: Researchers varied the lighting levels for groups of workers to study the effect on productivity. Surprisingly, output increased for both the group whose lighting was improved and the control group whose lighting was unchanged — showing that some factor other than physical lighting was influencing productivity.
  2. Relay Room Experiments (Relay Assembly Test Room): A small group of workers was separated, and researchers systematically varied rest pauses, working hours and other conditions. Productivity rose steadily throughout, including when conditions were later returned to the original harsher levels — suggesting the workers responded to the special attention they received rather than to the physical changes themselves.
  3. Bank Wiring Observation Room Experiment: Researchers observed a group of workers without changing their conditions, to study the influence of informal group dynamics. They found that the group had developed its own informal production norms and would deliberately restrict output to match them, regardless of what management's incentive scheme encouraged.
  4. Interview Programme: Around 20,000 employees were interviewed about their attitudes toward their work, supervisors and the company. Interviewers found that it was less important to give employees direct answers than to allow them to express their views and feelings freely — employees valued simply being listened to.

5.3 Key Findings of the Hawthorne Experiments

  1. Social Factors Govern Workers: Workers are motivated as much by social and psychological needs — belonging, recognition, security — as by physical working conditions or pay.
  2. Group Influence: Informal groups form naturally among workers and strongly influence individual behaviour and productivity, sometimes more than formal management directives do.
  3. Supervision: The style and quality of supervision (attentive, participative) affects morale and output.
  4. Communication: Effective two-way communication between management and workers helps resolve problems and build trust.
  5. Conflicts: Conflicts between formal organisational goals and the informal group's own norms can arise and affect output.
  6. Leadership: Good informal leadership within a work group can positively influence morale and performance alongside formal supervision.

5.4 Implications of the Hawthorne Experiments

The findings are often summarised as the "Hawthorne Effect" — the tendency for people to improve their behaviour or performance simply because they know they are being observed or because they are receiving special attention. The implications for management include:

  • Recognising workers as social beings with psychological and social needs, not just economic units responding to pay.
  • Involving workers in decisions and listening to their views improves motivation and cooperation.
  • Good leadership and effective communication matter as much as physical working conditions.
  • Informal groups and their norms must be understood and worked with, not ignored, since they can support or undermine formal goals.

These findings launched the Human Relations (Neo-Classical) movement in management thought, directly addressing the psychological and social dimensions that the Classical Era's Scientific Management approach had largely overlooked.

3. Key Terms

Hawthorne Experiments
A series of studies at Western Electric's Hawthorne Works led by Elton Mayo, which found that social and psychological factors strongly influence worker productivity.
Hawthorne Effect
The tendency for people to change or improve their behaviour because they know they are being observed or are receiving special attention.
Informal Group
A naturally forming social group among workers, not created by management, which develops its own norms and can strongly influence productivity.
Human Relations Movement
The Neo-Classical school of management thought, launched by the Hawthorne findings, focused on the social and psychological needs of workers.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Hawthorne Experiments (Western Electric, Elton Mayo): Illumination, Relay Room, Bank Wiring Observation Room, Interview Programme.
  • Key finding: social/psychological factors (not just physical conditions or pay) drive productivity — the 'Hawthorne Effect' of workers responding to attention.
  • Bank Wiring Room showed informal groups set their own output norms, sometimes restricting output against management's incentive scheme.
  • Findings launched the Human Relations (Neo-Classical) movement, correcting Scientific Management's narrow, mechanical view of workers.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What were the Hawthorne Experiments? Who conducted them?
    Show model answer
    A series of studies at the Western Electric Company's Hawthorne Works in the late 1920s/early 1930s, led by Elton Mayo, originally intended to study the effect of physical working conditions (like lighting) on productivity.
  2. Explain the findings of the Illumination and Relay Room experiments.
    Show model answer
    In the Illumination Experiments, productivity rose for both the test group and the control group regardless of lighting changes, suggesting a non-physical factor was at work. In the Relay Room experiments, productivity kept rising even when conditions were returned to their original state, suggesting workers responded to the special attention they received rather than the physical changes themselves.
  3. What did the Bank Wiring Observation Room experiment reveal?
    Show model answer
    It revealed that workers form informal groups with their own production norms, and that the group would deliberately restrict output to match these norms regardless of management's formal incentive scheme.
  4. What is the 'Hawthorne Effect'?
    Show model answer
    The tendency for people to change or improve their behaviour or performance simply because they know they are being observed or are receiving special attention, rather than because of any actual change in physical working conditions.
  5. What are the main implications of the Hawthorne Experiments for management?
    Show model answer
    Workers are social beings motivated by psychological and social needs as well as pay; involving and listening to workers improves motivation; good leadership and communication matter; and informal groups must be understood and worked with, since they can support or undermine formal organisational goals.
