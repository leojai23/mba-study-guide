Unit 1Evolution of Management Thought

Book: Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour (BA4102, Anna University MBA Sem I)

1. Overview

Management thought has evolved through several competing schools of theory. This topic surveys how writers have classified these schools — famously described as a 'management theory jungle' — before the next topics examine the Classical, Neo-Classical and Modern eras in detail.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Why Management Thought Evolved

As organisations grew larger and more complex through the Industrial Revolution and into the 20th century, thinkers from different backgrounds — engineers, sociologists, psychologists, and mathematicians — each approached the problem of managing people and work from their own discipline. This produced several distinct, sometimes competing, schools of management thought rather than one single theory.

2.2 The Management Theory Jungle (Harold Koontz)

Writing in the 1960s, Professor Harold Koontz observed that the proliferation of management theories had created a confusing "jungle" of overlapping schools of thought, each using different terms and definitions to describe similar ideas. He identified several schools, including:

  1. The Management Process School: Views management as a universal process of planning, organising, staffing, directing and controlling, applicable to any kind of enterprise.
  2. The Empirical School: Studies management through the analysis of actual managerial experience and case studies, believing that lessons can be drawn from what successful managers have done.
  3. The Human Behaviour School: Focuses on interpersonal relationships, treating management essentially as getting things done through people, drawing heavily on psychology.
  4. The Social System School: Views the organisation as a system of cultural interrelationships, drawing on sociology to study how groups and individuals cooperate within it.
  5. The Decision Theory School: Centres on rational decision-making as the core of management, studying the process, techniques and people involved in making organisational decisions.
  6. The Mathematical School: Treats management as a system of mathematical models and processes, using quantitative techniques to represent and solve managerial problems.

2.3 Broad Classification of Approaches

For practical purposes, these schools are usually grouped into three broad approaches, which also correspond to three historical eras of management theory:

ApproachCore IdeaKey Contributors
Classical ApproachFocuses on structure, efficiency and formal principles of organising work.F.W. Taylor (Scientific Management), Henri Fayol (Administrative Management), Max Weber (Bureaucracy)
Behavioural (Neo-Classical) ApproachFocuses on people — their needs, motivation and social behaviour at work.Elton Mayo and the Hawthorne Experiments
Management Science / Modern ApproachApplies quantitative methods, systems thinking and situational analysis to management problems.Systems Approach, Contingency Approach

2.4 Why This Classification Matters

No single school fully explains management on its own — each contributes a partial but valuable lens. The Classical approach gives structure and efficiency, the Behavioural approach restores attention to people, and the Modern approaches add flexibility and rigour for dealing with complex, changing environments. The remaining topics in this unit examine each era's key contributions in turn.

3. Key Terms

Management Theory Jungle
Harold Koontz's term for the confusing proliferation of overlapping management theories and schools of thought.
Management Process School
Views management as a universal process built from the functions of planning, organising, staffing, directing and controlling.
Classical Approach
An early school of management thought focused on structure, efficiency and formal principles of organising work.
Behavioural (Neo-Classical) Approach
A school of management thought focused on people, their needs, motivation and social behaviour at work.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Koontz's 'management theory jungle' identifies several overlapping schools: Management Process, Empirical, Human Behaviour, Social System, Decision Theory, Mathematical.
  • Three broad approaches/eras: Classical (structure/efficiency: Taylor, Fayol, Weber), Behavioural/Neo-Classical (people: Mayo/Hawthorne), Modern (Systems & Contingency).
  • No single school is complete on its own; each contributes a partial but valuable lens on management.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What did Harold Koontz mean by the 'management theory jungle'?
    Show model answer
    He used the term to describe how, by the 1960s, so many overlapping schools of management thought had developed — each with its own terminology — that the field had become confusing to navigate, like a jungle.
  2. List and briefly explain any three schools identified in the management theory jungle.
    Show model answer
    The Management Process School views management as a universal process of planning, organising, staffing, directing and controlling. The Human Behaviour School focuses on interpersonal relationships and getting things done through people. The Decision Theory School centres on rational decision-making as the core of management.
  3. What are the three broad approaches/eras of management thought?
    Show model answer
    The Classical Approach (structure and efficiency, e.g. Taylor, Fayol, Weber), the Behavioural/Neo-Classical Approach (people and motivation, e.g. Mayo and the Hawthorne Experiments), and the Modern/Management Science Approach (systems and contingency thinking).
