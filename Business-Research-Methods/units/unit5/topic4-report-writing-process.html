Unit 5Report Writing Process

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Beyond deciding what sections to include, writing an effective report requires attention to process, audience, tone and presentation. This topic covers the practical craft of report writing.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Introduction to Report Writing

Report writing is the process of translating research findings into a document that communicates clearly and persuasively to its intended readers, requiring as much care as the research itself.

4.2 Process of Writing Research Reports

  1. Organise the findings and supporting data logically before drafting begins.
  2. Prepare a detailed outline matching the report's intended structure.
  3. Write the first draft, following the outline section by section.
  4. Revise for clarity, accuracy, and logical flow.
  5. Proofread and format the final version.

4.3 Writing the Draft

Role of Audience

The language, level of technical detail, and emphasis of the report should be tailored to who will read it — a technical report for researchers can use statistical terminology freely, while a management report should minimise jargon and foreground implications for decisions.

Comprehension

Use clear, simple sentence structure, define technical terms when first used, and support complex findings with charts or tables so they are easier to grasp.

Tone

Maintain an objective, professional tone throughout, presenting both supporting and contradicting evidence fairly rather than only evidence that favours a particular conclusion.

Final Proof

Carefully proofread the final draft for factual accuracy, consistency between the text and any figures/tables, correct referencing, and grammar/spelling errors before submission.

4.4 Presentation Considerations

  • Use headings, white space and consistent formatting to make the report easy to navigate.
  • Use charts, graphs and tables to present quantitative findings more clearly than text alone.
  • Keep visual elements simple, correctly labelled, and directly relevant to the point being made.

4.5 Precautions in Writing the Report

  • Avoid overstating conclusions beyond what the data actually supports.
  • Avoid excessive technical jargon when writing for a non-technical audience.
  • Ensure every claim in the report is traceable back to the underlying data or a cited source.
  • Avoid presenting personal opinion as if it were a research finding.

3. Key Terms

Report Writing
The process of translating research findings into a document that communicates clearly and persuasively to its intended readers.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Writing process: organise findings → outline → draft → revise → proofread/format.
  • Tailor language/detail to the audience: technical report vs management report.
  • Maintain objective tone: present both supporting and contradicting evidence fairly.
  • Precautions: don't overstate conclusions, avoid excess jargon, keep claims traceable to data, avoid stating opinion as finding.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Why must the role of the audience be considered when writing a research report?
    Show model answer
    The language, level of technical detail and emphasis of a report should be tailored to its readers. A technical report for fellow researchers can use statistical terminology freely, while a management report should minimise jargon and foreground the practical implications for decisions, since the two audiences need the findings communicated differently.
  2. What precautions should be taken while writing a research report?
    Show model answer
    Writers should avoid overstating conclusions beyond what the data actually supports, avoid excessive technical jargon when writing for a non-technical audience, ensure every claim is traceable back to underlying data or a cited source, and avoid presenting personal opinion as if it were a research finding.
  3. Outline the process of writing a research report.
    Show model answer
    The process begins with organising the findings and supporting data logically, preparing a detailed outline matching the report's structure, writing the first draft section by section, revising for clarity and logical flow, and finally proofreading and formatting the finished version.
