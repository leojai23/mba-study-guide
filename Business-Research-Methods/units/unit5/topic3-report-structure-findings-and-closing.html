Unit 5Report Structure: Findings and Closing

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Continuing the report structure, this topic covers the sections that present and close out the research: interpretation of results, conclusions, recommendations, limitations, and references.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Interpretation of Results

Explains what the analysed data actually means in the context of the research objectives — going beyond raw statistics to explain their practical significance for the business problem under study.

3.2 Conclusions

States, clearly and directly, the answers the study has found to its research questions, each conclusion tied back to specific findings presented earlier in the report.

3.3 Suggestions and Recommendations

Translates the conclusions into specific, actionable recommendations for management, ideally prioritised and, where possible, linked to an estimate of their likely impact or feasibility.

3.4 Limitations of the Study

Honestly states the constraints that may affect how far the findings can be generalised or relied upon — such as sample size, geographic scope, time period, or methodological constraints — so readers can correctly judge the findings' applicability.

3.5 Reference Section

Lists, in a consistent citation format, every source cited in the report — books, journal articles, reports and websites — allowing readers to locate the original material and verify claims.

3.6 Putting It Together: Report Format Flow

Section GroupPurpose
Front matter (Title → Research Method)Orients the reader: what was studied, why, and how.
Results and closing (Interpretation → References)Presents what was found and what should be done about it.

3. Key Terms

Interpretation of Results
The section of a report that explains what analysed data means in the context of the research objectives.
Recommendations
Specific, actionable suggestions for management derived from a study's conclusions.
Limitations of the Study
An honest statement of the constraints affecting how far a study's findings can be generalised or relied upon.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Closing sequence: interpretation of results → conclusions → suggestions/recommendations → limitations → reference section.
  • Conclusions = direct answers to research questions, tied to specific findings.
  • Recommendations = actionable, ideally prioritised by impact/feasibility.
  • Limitations section = honestly states constraints (sample size, scope, time, method) affecting generalisability.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Why must a research report include a section on its own limitations?
    Show model answer
    A report must honestly state its limitations, such as sample size, geographic scope, time period or methodological constraints, so that readers can correctly judge how far the findings can be generalised or relied upon, rather than over-applying conclusions beyond what the study actually supports.
  2. Distinguish between conclusions and recommendations in a research report.
    Show model answer
    Conclusions are direct, evidence-based answers to the research questions, each tied back to specific findings. Recommendations go a step further, translating those conclusions into specific, actionable suggestions for management, ideally prioritised by their likely impact or feasibility.
  3. What is the purpose of the reference section in a report?
    Show model answer
    The reference section lists, in a consistent citation format, every source cited in the report, allowing readers to locate the original material, verify claims, and assess how the current study relates to prior work.
