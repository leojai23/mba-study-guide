Unit 5Ethics in Business Research

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

The final topic of the course addresses research ethics — the principles that govern how research should be conducted and reported responsibly, and the balance between subjectivity and objectivity in the research process.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Introduction to Ethics in Research

Research ethics are the moral principles that guide how a study is designed, conducted and reported, ensuring that participants, clients and the broader public are treated fairly and that findings can be trusted.

5.2 Principles of Ethical Research

  • Informed consent — participants should understand and voluntarily agree to take part.
  • Confidentiality and privacy — respondent data should be protected and used only for its stated purpose.
  • Avoidance of harm — the research process should not cause physical, psychological or financial harm to participants.
  • Honesty and transparency — findings must be reported accurately, including results that do not support the researcher's expectations.
  • Avoidance of deception — participants should not be misled about the nature or purpose of the study without strong justification and appropriate debriefing.

5.3 Ethical Behaviour of the Researcher

An ethical researcher avoids data fabrication or manipulation, properly credits the work of others through accurate citation, discloses any conflicts of interest, and respects the rights and dignity of respondents throughout the study.

5.4 Subjectivity in Research

Subjectivity refers to the influence of the researcher's own beliefs, expectations or interests on how a study is designed, data is interpreted, or conclusions are framed. Some subjectivity is unavoidable (e.g. in choosing what to study), but uncontrolled subjectivity can bias findings.

5.5 Objectivity in Research

Objectivity is the practice of basing conclusions strictly on the evidence, using methods and reasoning that another researcher could independently verify, and actively guarding against the influence of personal bias on the results.

5.6 Significance of Ethics in Research

  • Protects the rights, dignity and wellbeing of research participants.
  • Maintains the credibility and trustworthiness of research findings.
  • Protects the reputation of the researcher and the sponsoring organisation.
  • Ensures research can be built upon reliably by future studies.

3. Key Terms

Research Ethics
The moral principles that guide how a study is designed, conducted and reported, protecting participants and the integrity of findings.
Informed Consent
The principle that research participants should understand and voluntarily agree to take part in a study.
Subjectivity (in Research)
The influence of a researcher's own beliefs, expectations or interests on the design, interpretation or framing of a study.
Objectivity (in Research)
The practice of basing research conclusions strictly on verifiable evidence, free from personal bias.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Ethical principles: informed consent, confidentiality/privacy, avoidance of harm, honesty/transparency, avoidance of deception.
  • Ethical researcher behaviour: no data fabrication, proper citation, disclosed conflicts of interest, respect for respondents.
  • Subjectivity = researcher's own beliefs/bias influencing the study; Objectivity = conclusions based strictly on evidence.
  • Ethics matters for: participant protection, research credibility, researcher/organisation reputation, future research reliability.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What are the key principles of ethical research?
    Show model answer
    Key principles include informed consent (participants understand and voluntarily agree to take part), confidentiality and privacy of respondent data, avoidance of harm to participants, honesty and transparency in reporting findings (including unfavourable results), and avoidance of deception without strong justification and proper debriefing.
  2. Distinguish between subjectivity and objectivity in research.
    Show model answer
    Subjectivity refers to the influence of the researcher's own beliefs, expectations or interests on how a study is designed, data is interpreted, or conclusions are framed. Objectivity is the practice of basing conclusions strictly on verifiable evidence using methods another researcher could independently check, actively guarding against personal bias.
  3. Why is ethics significant in business research?
    Show model answer
    Ethics in research protects the rights, dignity and wellbeing of research participants, maintains the credibility and trustworthiness of the findings, protects the reputation of the researcher and sponsoring organisation, and ensures that the research can be reliably built upon by future studies.
