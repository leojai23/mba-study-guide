Unit 5Report Structure: Front Matter

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This topic covers the opening sections of a standard research report format — everything from the title page through to the statement of the research method, which together orient the reader before the findings are presented.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Introduction to Report Structure

While exact formats vary by organisation, most research reports follow a broadly standard structure so that readers familiar with research reports in general can quickly locate the section they need.

2.2 Title of the Report

The title should concisely and accurately convey the subject of the study, be specific enough to distinguish it from other work, and avoid unnecessary jargon.

2.3 Table of Contents and Chapterisation

The table of contents lists every major section and sub-section with page numbers, organised into a logical chapter structure (e.g. Introduction, Literature Review, Methodology, Findings, Conclusions) so the reader can navigate the report efficiently.

2.4 Contents of Each Chapter

Each chapter should open with a brief statement of its purpose, be organised under clear sub-headings, and close with a short summary or transition to the next chapter where useful.

2.5 Executive Summary

A concise, self-contained overview — typically one to two pages — of the problem, method, key findings and recommendations, written so a busy reader can grasp the essentials without reading the full report.

2.6 Introduction to the Report

Sets the context for the study: the background of the problem, why it matters to the organisation, and an outline of what the rest of the report covers.

2.7 Statement of Objectives

Clearly lists the specific objectives the research was designed to achieve, giving the reader a yardstick against which to judge whether the study succeeded.

2.8 Literature Review: Analysis of Previous Research

Summarises and critically evaluates prior research relevant to the problem, showing how the current study builds on, differs from, or fills a gap left by earlier work.

2.9 Research Method

Describes the research design used, the sampling plan, the data collection methods and instruments, and the analytical techniques applied — in enough detail that a reader could assess (or replicate) the approach.

3. Key Terms

Executive Summary
A concise, self-contained overview of a report's problem, method, findings and recommendations.
Literature Review
A section summarising and critically evaluating prior research relevant to the current study's problem.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Front matter sequence: title → table of contents/chapterisation → chapter contents → executive summary → introduction → objectives → literature review → research method.
  • Executive summary = short, self-contained, covers problem/method/findings/recommendations.
  • Literature review = evaluates prior research, shows how the current study fits in.
  • Research method section = design, sampling, data collection, analysis techniques, detailed enough to assess/replicate.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What is the purpose of the executive summary in a research report?
    Show model answer
    The executive summary is a concise, self-contained overview, typically one to two pages, covering the research problem, method, key findings and recommendations. It allows a busy reader, such as senior management, to grasp the essential conclusions of the study without reading the full report.
  2. What should the research method section of a report contain?
    Show model answer
    The research method section should describe the research design used, the sampling plan, the data collection methods and instruments employed, and the analytical techniques applied, in sufficient detail that a reader can assess the soundness of the approach or replicate the study.
  3. Explain the role of the literature review in a research report.
    Show model answer
    The literature review summarises and critically evaluates prior research relevant to the current problem, showing how the current study builds on, differs from, or fills a gap left by earlier work, and thereby justifying the need for the present research.
