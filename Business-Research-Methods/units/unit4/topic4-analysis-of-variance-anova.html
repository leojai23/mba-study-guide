Unit 4Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) extends hypothesis testing to compare the means of three or more groups simultaneously. This topic covers its assumptions, applications, and the one-way and two-way classifications.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Characteristics of Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

ANOVA tests whether the means of three or more groups are statistically different from one another by comparing the variation between group means to the variation within groups, rather than comparing means pair by pair.

4.2 Assumptions of ANOVA

  • Observations are independent of one another.
  • Each group/population is approximately normally distributed.
  • The groups have approximately equal variances (homogeneity of variance).
  • The dependent variable is measured on an interval or ratio scale.

4.3 Applications of ANOVA

  • Comparing average sales performance across more than two sales regions.
  • Comparing customer satisfaction scores across more than two store formats.
  • Comparing the effectiveness of more than two advertising campaigns.

4.4 ANOVA Techniques

ANOVA works by partitioning the total variation in the data into components: variation between groups (explained by the factor being studied) and variation within groups (random/error variation). The ratio of these two forms the F-statistic:

F = (Variance Between Groups) ÷ (Variance Within Groups)

A large F-value suggests the group means differ more than would be expected by chance alone.

4.5 Analysis of Variance for One-Way Classification

One-way ANOVA tests the effect of a single factor (e.g. "region") on a dependent variable (e.g. "sales"), comparing means across the factor's different levels/categories.

4.6 Analysis of Variance for Two-Way Classification

Two-way ANOVA tests the effect of two factors simultaneously (e.g. "region" and "season" on sales), and can also test whether the two factors interact — that is, whether the effect of one factor depends on the level of the other.

3. Key Terms

Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)
A statistical technique that tests whether the means of three or more groups differ by comparing between-group and within-group variation.
F-Statistic
The ratio of between-group variance to within-group variance used in ANOVA to test for significant differences.
One-Way ANOVA
ANOVA used to test the effect of a single factor on a dependent variable across its different levels.
Two-Way ANOVA
ANOVA used to test the effect of two factors, including any interaction between them, on a dependent variable.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • ANOVA compares means of 3+ groups via between-group vs within-group variance.
  • Assumptions: independent observations, normal distribution, equal variances, interval/ratio data.
  • F = Variance Between Groups / Variance Within Groups; large F suggests real group differences.
  • One-way ANOVA: one factor. Two-way ANOVA: two factors, can test interaction effects.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What is ANOVA and what assumptions underlie it?
    Show model answer
    Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) tests whether the means of three or more groups differ significantly by comparing the variation between group means to the variation within groups. It assumes the observations are independent, each group is approximately normally distributed, the groups have roughly equal variances, and the dependent variable is measured on an interval or ratio scale.
  2. Explain how the F-statistic is used in ANOVA.
    Show model answer
    The F-statistic is the ratio of the variance between groups to the variance within groups. A large F-value indicates that the differences between group means are larger than would be expected from random variation alone, leading to rejection of the null hypothesis that all group means are equal.
  3. Differentiate between one-way and two-way ANOVA.
    Show model answer
    One-way ANOVA tests the effect of a single factor on a dependent variable across its different levels, such as sales across regions. Two-way ANOVA tests the effect of two factors simultaneously, such as region and season on sales, and can additionally test whether the two factors interact with each other.
