Unit 2Measurement in Research

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Before data can be collected, the concepts under study must be translated into measurable form. This topic covers the measurement process, the techniques used, and the sources of measurement error.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Introduction to Measurement

Measurement is the process of assigning numbers or labels to objects, events or characteristics according to a defined set of rules, so that abstract concepts can be studied empirically.

5.2 Process of Measurement

  1. Identify and clearly define the concept to be measured.
  2. Develop an operational definition that specifies exactly how the concept will be observed/measured.
  3. Select or design the measurement instrument (scale, questionnaire item, test).
  4. Apply the instrument and record the resulting data.
  5. Assess the quality (validity and reliability) of the measurement.

5.3 Techniques of Measurement

  • Self-report techniques (questionnaires, rating scales completed by respondents).
  • Observational techniques (recording behaviour directly).
  • Physiological/objective measures (e.g. sales figures, attendance records).
  • Projective techniques (indirect methods used to uncover underlying attitudes).

5.4 Errors in Measurement

Error TypeDescription
Systematic (Constant) ErrorA consistent bias that affects measurement in the same direction every time (e.g. a leading question that always skews answers toward one option).
Random ErrorUnpredictable variation from one measurement to another with no consistent direction, caused by chance factors.

5.5 Difficulties in Measurement

  • Many business and social concepts (e.g. "satisfaction", "loyalty") are abstract and not directly observable.
  • Respondents may answer inaccurately due to memory lapses, social desirability bias, or misunderstanding.
  • Different instruments can yield different results for the same underlying concept.

5.6 Criteria for Good Measurement

  • Validity — the instrument actually measures the concept it is intended to measure.
  • Reliability — the instrument produces consistent results on repeated application.
  • Sensitivity — the instrument can detect small but meaningful differences.
  • Practicality — the instrument is economical, convenient and interpretable to use.

3. Key Terms

Measurement
The process of assigning numbers or labels to objects, events or characteristics according to defined rules.
Operational Definition
A precise specification of exactly how a concept will be observed or measured.
Systematic Error
A consistent measurement bias that affects results in the same direction every time.
Validity (Measurement)
The extent to which an instrument actually measures the concept it is intended to measure.
Reliability (Measurement)
The extent to which an instrument produces consistent results on repeated application.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Measurement process: define concept → operational definition → choose instrument → apply → assess quality.
  • Techniques: self-report, observation, objective/physiological measures, projective techniques.
  • Errors: systematic (consistent bias) vs random (unpredictable variation).
  • Good measurement criteria: validity, reliability, sensitivity, practicality.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Describe the process of measurement in research.
    Show model answer
    Measurement begins with identifying and defining the concept to be measured, developing an operational definition of exactly how it will be observed, selecting or designing a measurement instrument, applying it to collect data, and finally assessing the validity and reliability of the resulting measurement.
  2. Distinguish between systematic and random error in measurement.
    Show model answer
    Systematic (constant) error is a consistent bias that skews measurement in the same direction every time, such as a leading survey question. Random error is unpredictable variation from one measurement to another caused by chance factors, with no consistent direction.
  3. What criteria determine whether a measurement is good?
    Show model answer
    Good measurement requires validity (the instrument measures what it is intended to measure), reliability (it produces consistent results on repeated use), sensitivity (it can detect small but meaningful differences), and practicality (it is economical and convenient to use).
