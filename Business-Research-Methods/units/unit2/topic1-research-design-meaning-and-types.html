Unit 2Research Design: Meaning and Types

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

A research design is the master blueprint for a study. This topic covers what makes a design good, the factors that shape it, and the broad categories of research design available.

2. Detailed Notes

1.1 Meaning and Definition of Research Design

A research design is the overall plan or blueprint that specifies the methods and procedures for collecting and analysing the information needed to answer the research problem. It is the framework that links the research questions to the data that will be gathered.

1.2 Features of a Good Research Design

  • Clearly specifies the objectives and the information needed.
  • Minimises bias and maximises the reliability of the data collected.
  • Provides the maximum relevant information at the lowest possible cost.
  • Is flexible enough to allow consideration of different aspects of the problem.
  • Specifies a sound, feasible plan for sampling, data collection and analysis.

1.3 Factors Affecting Research Design

  • The nature and degree of clarity of the research problem.
  • The objectives of the study (exploratory, descriptive or causal).
  • Availability of time, budget and skilled personnel.
  • Availability and accessibility of data sources.
  • The degree of accuracy required in the findings.

1.4 Significance of Research Design

A sound research design ensures that the study collects the right data, in the right way, to answer the research question efficiently, reduces wasted effort and cost, and makes the findings more reliable and defensible.

1.5 Research Design Process

  1. Review the research problem and objectives.
  2. Decide on the type of design (exploratory, descriptive or causal) appropriate to the objectives.
  3. Determine the data sources (primary and/or secondary) and data collection methods.
  4. Decide on the sampling design.
  5. Plan the data analysis approach.
  6. Prepare the research proposal/plan.

1.6 Types of Research Design

TypeUsed When
Exploratory Research DesignThe problem is vague or not well understood; the goal is to gain insight and clarify the issue.
Descriptive Research DesignThe problem is clearly defined; the goal is to describe characteristics of a population or phenomenon.
Experimental / Causal Research DesignThe goal is to establish a cause-and-effect relationship between variables.

3. Key Terms

Research Design
The overall plan or blueprint specifying the methods and procedures for collecting and analysing data to answer the research problem.
Exploratory Research Design
A design used when the problem is not well understood, aimed at gaining insight.
Descriptive Research Design
A design used to describe the characteristics of a population or phenomenon.
Causal Research Design
A design used to establish cause-and-effect relationships between variables.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Research design = blueprint linking the research question to the data plan.
  • Good design: clear objectives, minimises bias, cost-efficient, flexible, sound sampling/analysis plan.
  • Factors affecting design: problem clarity, objectives, time/budget, data availability, accuracy needed.
  • Three broad design types: exploratory, descriptive, causal/experimental.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define research design and list the features of a good research design.
    Show model answer
    A research design is the overall plan or blueprint that specifies the methods and procedures for collecting and analysing data needed to answer the research problem. A good design clearly specifies objectives, minimises bias, maximises reliability, provides maximum relevant information at the lowest cost, and remains flexible.
  2. What factors influence the choice of a research design?
    Show model answer
    The choice of research design is influenced by the nature and clarity of the research problem, the study's objectives, available time and budget, the accessibility of data sources, and the degree of accuracy required in the findings.
  3. Describe the research design process.
    Show model answer
    The process moves from reviewing the problem and objectives, to selecting the design type (exploratory, descriptive or causal), determining data sources and collection methods, deciding the sampling design, planning the analysis approach, and preparing the research proposal.
