Unit 2Exploratory and Descriptive Research Design

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This topic covers the two non-causal research designs in detail: exploratory research, used to clarify an ill-defined problem, and descriptive research, used to portray characteristics of a population once the problem is clear.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Exploratory Research Design

Introduction

Exploratory research is undertaken when the researcher has little prior understanding of the problem. It is flexible, unstructured, and aims to generate insight and hypotheses for further, more structured study rather than to provide final answers.

Methods of Exploratory Research

  • Literature survey of existing published material.
  • Expert/key-informant interviews with individuals knowledgeable about the problem.
  • Focus group discussions with a small set of relevant participants.
  • Case study analysis of similar, insight-generating situations.
  • Pilot surveys on a small scale.

Application

Used at the start of a research project to clarify concepts, identify relevant variables, and sharpen a vague problem into a precise, researchable one.

Significance and Limitations

Significant because it prevents wasted effort on a poorly defined problem and surfaces hypotheses worth testing. Limited because its findings are typically not representative or conclusive enough to be used directly for final decisions.

2.2 Descriptive Research Design

Introduction

Descriptive research is undertaken when the problem is already well structured, to accurately describe the characteristics of a population, a market, or a phenomenon — answering questions of who, what, when, where and how, but not why.

Methods of Descriptive Research

  • Sample surveys using structured questionnaires.
  • Observation studies recording behaviour as it occurs.
  • Analysis of secondary/archival data.

Application

Used for market characteristic studies, usage/attitude studies, and any situation requiring an accurate profile of a defined group.

Significance and Limitations

Significant because it is systematic and produces data that generalises to a wider population when properly sampled. Limited because it only describes relationships observed — it cannot by itself prove that one variable causes another.

Types of Descriptive Research Design

TypeDescription
Cross-Sectional StudiesData collected from a sample of the population at one point in time.
Longitudinal StudiesData collected from the same sample (a "panel") repeatedly over time.

3. Key Terms

Exploratory Research
Flexible, unstructured research undertaken to gain insight and clarify a poorly understood problem.
Descriptive Research
Research undertaken to accurately describe the characteristics of a population or phenomenon once the problem is clearly defined.
Cross-Sectional Study
A descriptive design that collects data from a sample at a single point in time.
Longitudinal Study
A descriptive design that collects data from the same sample repeatedly over time.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Exploratory research: ill-defined problem, flexible methods, generates insight/hypotheses, not conclusive.
  • Exploratory methods: literature survey, expert interviews, focus groups, case studies, pilot surveys.
  • Descriptive research: well-defined problem, answers who/what/when/where/how, not why.
  • Descriptive methods: sample surveys, observation, secondary data analysis.
  • Descriptive design types: cross-sectional (one point in time) vs longitudinal (repeated over time).

5. Practice Questions

  1. When is exploratory research used, and what methods does it employ?
    Show model answer
    Exploratory research is used when the researcher has little prior understanding of the problem and needs to gain insight before a structured study. It uses flexible methods such as literature surveys, expert interviews, focus group discussions, case studies, and small-scale pilot surveys.
  2. Explain descriptive research design and its types.
    Show model answer
    Descriptive research is used once the problem is well defined, to accurately portray the characteristics of a population through sample surveys, observation or secondary data. It has two main types: cross-sectional studies, which collect data at a single point in time, and longitudinal studies, which track the same sample repeatedly over time.
  3. What are the limitations of exploratory and descriptive research?
    Show model answer
    Exploratory research produces findings that are typically not representative or conclusive enough for final decisions, since its methods are flexible and small-scale. Descriptive research can accurately describe relationships observed in the data but cannot by itself establish that one variable causes another.
