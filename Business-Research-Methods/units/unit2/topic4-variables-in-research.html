Unit 2Variables in Research

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Every research design works with variables. This topic defines the different roles a variable can play in a study, which is essential to correctly designing and interpreting any causal research.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Independent and Dependent Variables

The independent variable is the variable the researcher manipulates or that is assumed to cause a change. The dependent variable is the outcome variable that is measured to see the effect of that manipulation.

4.2 Concomitant Variable

A concomitant (or covariate) variable is a variable that is related to the dependent variable and is measured so its influence can be statistically controlled for, separating its effect from that of the independent variable.

4.3 Moderating Variables

A moderating variable affects the strength or direction of the relationship between the independent and dependent variables. For example, the effect of advertising spend on sales may be stronger in urban markets than rural ones — here, location moderates the relationship.

4.4 Extraneous Variables

Extraneous variables are factors other than the independent variable that could influence the dependent variable and distort the results if not controlled through good research design.

4.5 Intervening Variables

An intervening variable is a theoretical link that explains the mechanism through which the independent variable affects the dependent variable, without itself being directly observed (e.g. "employee motivation" intervening between "training" and "productivity").

3. Key Terms

Independent Variable
The variable manipulated or presumed to cause a change in a study.
Dependent Variable
The outcome variable measured to detect the effect of the independent variable.
Moderating Variable
A variable that affects the strength or direction of the relationship between the independent and dependent variables.
Extraneous Variable
A factor other than the independent variable that could influence the dependent variable and distort results if uncontrolled.
Intervening Variable
A theoretical, unobserved link explaining the mechanism through which the independent variable affects the dependent variable.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Independent variable = presumed cause; Dependent variable = measured effect.
  • Concomitant variable = related variable controlled for statistically.
  • Moderating variable = changes strength/direction of the IV-DV relationship.
  • Extraneous variable = uncontrolled factor that can distort results.
  • Intervening variable = unobserved mechanism linking IV to DV.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Differentiate between independent and dependent variables with an example.
    Show model answer
    The independent variable is the variable manipulated or presumed to cause a change, while the dependent variable is the outcome measured to detect that effect. For example, in a study of advertising, advertising spend is the independent variable and resulting sales is the dependent variable.
  2. Explain moderating and extraneous variables.
    Show model answer
    A moderating variable affects the strength or direction of the relationship between the independent and dependent variables, such as market location changing how strongly advertising affects sales. An extraneous variable is an uncontrolled factor other than the independent variable that could also influence the dependent variable and distort the results if not accounted for in the research design.
  3. What is an intervening variable? Give an example.
    Show model answer
    An intervening variable is a theoretical, unobserved link that explains the mechanism by which the independent variable affects the dependent variable. For example, employee motivation may intervene between training (independent variable) and productivity (dependent variable), explaining why training leads to higher output.
