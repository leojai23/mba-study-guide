Unit 1Types of Research

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Research can be classified in several overlapping ways — by purpose, by method, and by the time dimension it covers. This topic surveys the major types used in business research.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Classification by Purpose

TypeDescription
Pure / Basic / Fundamental ResearchUndertaken purely to expand knowledge and understand principles, with no immediate practical application in mind.
Applied / Practical / Need-Based / Action-Based ResearchUndertaken to solve a specific, immediate practical problem faced by an organisation.
Theoretical ResearchFocused on developing or testing theory, often building on pure research.

3.2 Research by Rigour and Approach

  • Empirical Research — based on observed and measured data/experience rather than theory alone.
  • Scientific Research — follows the formal scientific method: observation, hypothesis, testing, conclusion.
  • Social Research — studies human behaviour and social relationships, often using qualitative and quantitative methods together.
  • Historical Research — studies past events and records to understand their causes and consequences.

3.3 Exploratory and Conclusive Research

  • Exploratory / Formulative Research — conducted when the problem is not yet clearly defined, to gain insight and clarify the real issue before a more structured study.
  • Conclusive Research — conducted once the problem is clearly defined, to provide information useful in reaching a decision. It has two sub-types:
    • Descriptive / Statistical Research — describes characteristics of a population or phenomenon (the "what", "who", "when", "where").
    • Causal / Experimental / Explanatory Research — establishes cause-and-effect relationships between variables (the "why").

3.4 Research by Time Dimension

  • Cross-Sectional Research — collects data from a sample at one single point in time, giving a "snapshot".
  • Time-Series Research — collects data on the same variables repeatedly over a period of time, revealing trends and changes.

3.5 Other Types

Other commonly cited types include case study research (in-depth study of a single unit or small number of units), survey research (structured data collection from a sample), and action research (research conducted by practitioners to improve their own practice in real time).

3. Key Terms

Pure/Basic Research
Research undertaken purely to expand knowledge, with no immediate practical application.
Applied Research
Research undertaken to solve a specific, immediate practical problem.
Exploratory Research
Research conducted when a problem is not yet clearly defined, to gain insight before a structured study.
Descriptive Research
Conclusive research that describes the characteristics of a population or phenomenon.
Causal Research
Conclusive research that establishes cause-and-effect relationships between variables.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • By purpose: pure/basic vs applied/practical research.
  • By definedness of problem: exploratory (ill-defined problem) vs conclusive (well-defined problem).
  • Conclusive splits into descriptive (what/who/when/where) and causal (why).
  • By time: cross-sectional (one point in time) vs time-series (repeated over time).
  • Others: empirical, scientific, social, historical, case study, survey, action research.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Distinguish between pure and applied research.
    Show model answer
    Pure (basic/fundamental) research aims to expand knowledge and understand principles with no immediate practical use in mind, while applied (practical/need-based) research is directed at solving a specific, immediate problem faced by an organisation.
  2. Differentiate between exploratory and conclusive research.
    Show model answer
    Exploratory research is undertaken when a problem is not yet clearly defined, to gain insight and clarify the real issue. Conclusive research is undertaken once the problem is clearly defined, to provide decision-useful information, and splits further into descriptive research (describing characteristics) and causal research (establishing cause-and-effect).
  3. Explain cross-sectional and time-series research with examples.
    Show model answer
    Cross-sectional research collects data from a sample at a single point in time, such as a one-time customer satisfaction survey. Time-series research collects data on the same variables repeatedly over time, such as tracking monthly sales figures over several years to identify trends.
