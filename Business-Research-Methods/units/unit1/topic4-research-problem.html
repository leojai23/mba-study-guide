Unit 1The Research Problem

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Every research study begins with a problem. This topic covers how a research problem is identified, its components, how it is defined precisely, and how it is redefined into answerable questions.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Components of a Research Problem

  • The unit of analysis or population affected.
  • The variables or factors involved.
  • The relationship being studied between those variables.
  • The environment or context within which the problem exists.

4.2 Identifying the Research Problem

Major Sources for Identifying a Problem in Business Research

  • Personal experience and observation of business operations.
  • Discussions with managers, employees and customers.
  • Review of academic and trade literature.
  • Analysis of past data, reports and performance trends.
  • Feedback, complaints and suggestions from stakeholders.

Problem Identification Process

Broadly: recognising a symptom (e.g. falling sales) → investigating possible underlying causes → narrowing down to the actual researchable problem.

Criteria for Identifying a Good Problem

  • Researchable — capable of being studied with available methods and resources.
  • Significant — addresses an issue of real managerial importance.
  • Specific and clear, not vague or overly broad.
  • Feasible within the available time and budget.

Precautions in Identification of a Research Problem

Avoid problems that are too broad, already answered elsewhere, based on personal bias, or impossible to investigate with available data and resources.

4.3 Defining the Research Problem

Need for Problem Definition

A precisely defined problem focuses the entire research effort; an ill-defined problem wastes time and resources collecting the wrong data.

Steps in Problem Definition

  1. Understand the management decision that triggered the need for research.
  2. Discuss the problem with relevant stakeholders.
  3. Review any available secondary data.
  4. State the problem in clear, specific, researchable terms.

Guidelines for Problem Definition

State the problem in terms of a clear objective, avoid ambiguous terms, and ensure the problem is narrow enough to be addressed with the time and resources available.

4.4 Redefining the Research Problem

Once broadly defined, the problem is translated into a hierarchy of increasingly specific questions:

Question TypeDescription
Management QuestionsWhat does the manager need to decide? (broadest level)
Research QuestionsWhat information is needed to help answer the management question?
Investigative QuestionsWhat specific sub-questions must be answered to address each research question?
Measurement QuestionsWhat exact questions will be put to respondents or what data will be measured? (most specific level)

3. Key Terms

Research Problem
A specific issue or gap in knowledge that a research study is designed to investigate and resolve.
Management Question
The broad decision-level question a manager needs answered, which triggers the research.
Investigative Question
A specific sub-question that must be answered to address a broader research question.
Measurement Question
The exact, specific question put to respondents or the exact data measured during data collection.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Components of a research problem: unit of analysis, variables, relationship, environment.
  • Sources for identifying problems: experience, discussions, literature, past data, stakeholder feedback.
  • Good problem: researchable, significant, specific, feasible.
  • Problem definition steps: understand decision → discuss → review secondary data → state clearly.
  • Redefinition hierarchy: management question → research question → investigative question → measurement question.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What are the components of a research problem?
    Show model answer
    A research problem comprises the unit of analysis or population affected, the variables or factors involved, the relationship being studied between them, and the environment or context within which the problem exists.
  2. Explain the process and criteria for identifying a good research problem.
    Show model answer
    Problem identification moves from recognising a symptom, through investigating possible causes, to narrowing down to the actual researchable problem, drawing on sources such as personal experience, stakeholder discussion, literature and past data. A good problem must be researchable, significant, specific and feasible within available time and budget.
  3. Describe the hierarchy used to redefine a research problem into measurement questions.
    Show model answer
    The broad management question (what the manager needs to decide) is translated into research questions (what information is needed), which are broken into investigative questions (specific sub-questions), and finally into measurement questions (the exact questions put to respondents or data measured).
