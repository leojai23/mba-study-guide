Unit 1Research Objectives and Hypotheses

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Once the problem is defined, the researcher sets clear objectives and, where appropriate, formulates testable hypotheses that the study will investigate.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Research Objectives

Types of Research Objectives

  • General objective — the overall purpose or goal of the study.
  • Specific objectives — the concrete, measurable sub-goals that together fulfil the general objective.

Components of a Research Objective

A well-stated objective identifies the variable(s) to be studied, the population or unit of analysis, and the relationship or outcome to be examined.

Guidelines for Statement of Research Objectives

  • State objectives in clear, specific, and measurable terms.
  • Use action verbs (e.g. "to determine", "to compare", "to examine").
  • Ensure objectives are realistic given the available time and resources.
  • Keep each objective focused on a single, well-defined outcome.

5.2 Research Hypotheses

A hypothesis is a tentative, testable statement about the expected relationship between two or more variables, proposed as a possible answer to the research problem before data is collected.

Characteristics of a Good Hypothesis

  • Clear and precisely stated.
  • Testable through empirical data.
  • Consistent with existing knowledge, yet capable of extending it.
  • States a relationship between two or more variables.
  • Limited in scope, not overly broad or vague.

Types of Hypotheses

TypeDescription
Null Hypothesis (H₀)States there is no relationship or difference between the variables studied.
Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)States that a relationship or difference does exist.
Directional HypothesisSpecifies the expected direction of the relationship (e.g. "higher advertising spend increases sales").
Non-Directional HypothesisStates a relationship exists without specifying its direction.

Significance of Hypotheses

Hypotheses give the research direction and focus, suggest the type of data needed, and provide a framework against which the collected data can be formally tested.

Limitations of Hypotheses

A poorly formulated hypothesis can bias data collection toward confirming a preconceived belief, and not every exploratory study requires (or can support) a formal hypothesis.

3. Key Terms

Research Objective
A clear, specific statement of what a research study intends to achieve or discover.
Hypothesis
A tentative, testable statement about the expected relationship between two or more variables.
Null Hypothesis (H0)
A hypothesis stating there is no relationship or difference between the variables studied.
Alternative Hypothesis (H1)
A hypothesis stating that a relationship or difference does exist between the variables studied.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Objectives: general (overall purpose) vs specific (measurable sub-goals).
  • Good objective: clear, specific, measurable, realistic, uses action verbs.
  • Hypothesis = tentative, testable statement of an expected relationship between variables.
  • Good hypothesis: clear, testable, consistent with knowledge, states a relationship, limited in scope.
  • Types: null (H0, no relationship) vs alternative (H1, relationship exists); directional vs non-directional.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define research objectives and state the guidelines for writing them.
    Show model answer
    Research objectives are clear, specific statements of what a study intends to achieve. They should be stated in clear, measurable terms using action verbs, kept realistic given available time and resources, and each should focus on a single well-defined outcome.
  2. Define hypothesis and explain the characteristics of a good hypothesis.
    Show model answer
    A hypothesis is a tentative, testable statement about the expected relationship between two or more variables, proposed before data is collected. A good hypothesis is clear, testable, consistent with existing knowledge, states a specific relationship between variables, and is limited enough in scope to be investigated.
  3. Distinguish between null and alternative hypotheses.
    Show model answer
    The null hypothesis (H0) states that no relationship or difference exists between the variables studied, while the alternative hypothesis (H1) states that a relationship or difference does exist. Research uses statistical tests on sample data to decide whether to reject the null hypothesis in favour of the alternative.
