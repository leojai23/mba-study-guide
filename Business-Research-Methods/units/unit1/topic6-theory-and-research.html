Unit 1Theory and Research

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This closing topic of Unit 1 looks at the relationship between theory and research — how theory guides research design and interpretation, and the building blocks that make up a theory.

2. Detailed Notes

6.1 Introduction

Theory and research are closely interlinked: theory provides the conceptual framework that guides what to study and how to interpret findings, while research in turn tests, refines, or builds new theory.

6.2 Role of Theory in Research

  • Provides a framework for defining the research problem and relevant variables.
  • Helps generate hypotheses that research can test.
  • Guides the choice of research design and the interpretation of results.
  • Helps explain why an observed relationship between variables exists, not just that it exists.

6.3 Importance of Theory in Research

  • Prevents research from being a disconnected collection of isolated facts.
  • Allows findings from different studies to be compared and accumulated into a coherent body of knowledge.
  • Improves the ability to generalise and predict beyond the specific sample studied.
  • Gives researchers a basis for proposing practical recommendations grounded in established principles.

6.4 Components of Theory

ComponentDescription
ConceptsAbstract ideas or categories used to classify observations (e.g. "motivation", "customer loyalty").
ConstructsConcepts specifically defined and used for a particular research purpose, often built from more basic concepts.
VariablesMeasurable representations of constructs that can take different values (e.g. a loyalty score).
PropositionsStatements about the relationship between two or more concepts or constructs.
HypothesesPropositions stated in a testable form, ready to be examined with empirical data.

3. Key Terms

Theory
A systematic set of interrelated concepts, definitions and propositions that explains or predicts a phenomenon.
Concept
An abstract idea or category used to classify observations.
Construct
A concept specifically defined and used for a particular research purpose.
Proposition
A statement about the relationship between two or more concepts or constructs.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Theory guides what to study, how to design research, and how to interpret results.
  • Importance of theory: avoids disconnected facts, enables comparison across studies, supports generalisation.
  • Components of theory (building up): concepts → constructs → variables → propositions → hypotheses.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain the role of theory in research.
    Show model answer
    Theory provides the conceptual framework that guides the definition of the research problem and relevant variables, helps generate testable hypotheses, guides the choice of research design, and helps explain why an observed relationship exists rather than simply that it exists.
  2. Why is theory important in research?
    Show model answer
    Theory prevents research from producing a disconnected collection of isolated facts, allows findings across different studies to be compared and accumulated into a coherent body of knowledge, improves the ability to generalise beyond the specific sample studied, and grounds practical recommendations in established principles.
  3. List and briefly explain the components of theory.
    Show model answer
    Theory is built from concepts (abstract classifying ideas), constructs (concepts specifically defined for a research purpose), variables (measurable representations of constructs), propositions (statements of relationship between concepts), and hypotheses (propositions stated in a testable form).
