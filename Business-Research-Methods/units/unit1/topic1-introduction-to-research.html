Unit 1Introduction to Research

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This opening topic introduces research itself — what it means, why organisations undertake it, the broad process it follows, and how the idea of research has evolved over time.

2. Detailed Notes

1.1 Introduction to Research

Research is a systematic, organised and objective process of inquiry undertaken to find answers to questions or solutions to problems. It involves the careful collection, recording and analysis of data in order to establish facts, verify existing knowledge, or develop new understanding.

1.2 Nature of Research

  • Systematic — follows a planned, step-by-step process rather than random inquiry.
  • Objective — aims to minimise personal bias and rely on verifiable evidence.
  • Empirical — grounded in observation and data, not mere opinion.
  • Logical — proceeds through reasoned, replicable steps.
  • Analytical — uses appropriate methods to critically evaluate data and draw conclusions.

1.3 Objectives of Research

  • To gain familiarity with a phenomenon or achieve new insights into it (exploratory objective).
  • To accurately portray the characteristics of a particular individual, group or situation (descriptive objective).
  • To determine the frequency with which something occurs or with which it is associated with something else (diagnostic objective).
  • To test a hypothesis of a causal relationship between variables (hypothesis-testing objective).

1.4 Research Process

Although the exact steps vary by study, research generally follows this broad sequence:

  1. Identifying and formulating the research problem.
  2. Reviewing the related literature.
  3. Formulating a hypothesis (where applicable).
  4. Designing the research (choosing the research design and methodology).
  5. Collecting the data.
  6. Analysing the data.
  7. Interpreting the results and drawing conclusions.
  8. Preparing and presenting the research report.

1.5 Significance of Research

  • Provides the basis for almost all management decisions by replacing guesswork with evidence.
  • Helps identify and solve operational and strategic business problems.
  • Provides a rational, systematic basis for decision-making in uncertain conditions.
  • Generates new knowledge and contributes to the overall body of theory in a field.
  • Supports policy formulation at both organisational and governmental levels.

1.6 Limitations of Research

  • Findings are often specific to the time, place and context studied, limiting generalisability.
  • Constrained by the availability of time, money and skilled personnel.
  • Data quality depends on respondent honesty and researcher objectivity, both of which can introduce error.
  • Complex social and business phenomena cannot always be fully captured through measurement.

1.7 Research in an Evolutionary Perspective

Research as a formal discipline has evolved from simple trial-and-error problem solving in ancient times, through the development of the scientific method during the Renaissance and Enlightenment, to today's highly specialised, technology-enabled research methodologies. In business, research has grown from basic record-keeping and intuition-based decisions into a structured management function supported by statistics, computing and dedicated research departments.

3. Key Terms

Research
A systematic, objective and organised process of inquiry undertaken to find answers to questions or solutions to problems.
Exploratory Objective
A research objective aimed at gaining familiarity with, or new insight into, a phenomenon.
Descriptive Objective
A research objective aimed at accurately portraying the characteristics of an individual, group or situation.
Diagnostic Objective
A research objective aimed at determining the frequency of occurrence or association between variables.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Research = systematic + objective + empirical + logical + analytical inquiry.
  • Four objectives: exploratory, descriptive, diagnostic, hypothesis-testing.
  • Research process: problem → literature review → hypothesis → design → data collection → analysis → interpretation → report.
  • Significance: basis for decisions, problem-solving, new knowledge, policy formulation.
  • Limitations: context-specific findings, resource constraints, data-quality dependence.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define research and explain its nature.
    Show model answer
    Research is a systematic, objective and organised process of inquiry undertaken to find answers to questions or solutions to problems through the collection, recording and analysis of data. Its nature is systematic, objective, empirical, logical and analytical.
  2. What are the main objectives of research?
    Show model answer
    Research has four broad objectives: exploratory (gaining familiarity with a phenomenon), descriptive (accurately portraying characteristics), diagnostic (determining frequency or association), and hypothesis-testing (testing a causal relationship between variables).
  3. Outline the steps in the research process.
    Show model answer
    The research process typically moves through: identifying and formulating the problem, reviewing related literature, formulating a hypothesis, designing the research, collecting data, analysing data, interpreting results, and preparing the research report.
  4. Discuss the significance and limitations of research in business.
    Show model answer
    Research provides a rational, evidence-based foundation for management decisions, helps solve operational problems and generates new knowledge, but is limited by context-specific findings, constraints of time/money/skill, and dependence on data quality and researcher objectivity.
