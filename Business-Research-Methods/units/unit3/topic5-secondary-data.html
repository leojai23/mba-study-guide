Unit 3Secondary Data

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Not all research data needs to be collected firsthand. This topic covers secondary data — data already gathered by someone else — and when it is appropriate to rely on it instead of, or alongside, primary data.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Introduction to Secondary Data

Secondary data is data that has already been collected, processed and published by some other individual or organisation for a purpose other than the current research problem, but which the researcher can reuse.

5.2 Primary versus Secondary Data

AspectPrimary DataSecondary Data
SourceCollected firsthand by the researcherAlready collected by someone else
Cost and timeHigherLower, usually faster to obtain
Relevance to the specific problemExact fitMay not perfectly match the current problem
Control over qualityFull controlDepends entirely on the original source

5.3 Methods/Sources of Secondary Data

  • Government publications and official statistics (census, trade, economic data).
  • Industry/trade association reports.
  • Company records, annual reports and internal databases.
  • Academic journals, books and prior research studies.
  • Commercial market research reports and databases.
  • Online/digital sources, including news archives and statistical portals.

5.4 Significance of Secondary Data

  • Saves considerable time and cost compared to fresh primary data collection.
  • Useful for exploratory research, establishing background and context, and defining the research problem.
  • Can provide historical/time-series data otherwise impossible to gather within a study's timeframe.

5.5 Limitations of Secondary Data

  • May be outdated by the time it is used.
  • May not match the exact definitions, units or population of the current study.
  • Quality and accuracy depend on the original source, which the researcher cannot verify directly.
  • May have been collected for a different purpose, limiting its direct applicability.

3. Key Terms

Secondary Data
Data already collected, processed and published by someone else for a purpose other than the current research problem.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Secondary data = already collected by someone else, reused for the current study.
  • Sources: government publications, trade reports, company records, journals, commercial databases, online sources.
  • Significance: saves time/cost, good for exploratory work and historical/time-series data.
  • Limitations: may be outdated, mismatched definitions/units, unverifiable quality, different original purpose.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Define secondary data and distinguish it from primary data.
    Show model answer
    Secondary data is data that has already been collected, processed and published by someone else for a purpose other than the current study, but which the researcher reuses. Unlike primary data, which is collected firsthand and fits the specific problem exactly, secondary data is cheaper and faster to obtain but may not perfectly match the current research problem, and its quality depends entirely on the original source.
  2. List the major sources of secondary data.
    Show model answer
    Major sources include government publications and official statistics, industry/trade association reports, company records and annual reports, academic journals and prior research, commercial market research databases, and online sources such as statistical portals and news archives.
  3. What are the limitations of using secondary data?
    Show model answer
    Secondary data may be outdated by the time it is used, may not match the exact definitions, units or population of the current study, its quality cannot be directly verified by the researcher, and it may have been collected for a different purpose, limiting how directly it applies to the current problem.
