Unit 3Questionnaire Design

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

The questionnaire is the most widely used tool for collecting primary data. This topic covers how questionnaires are constructed, the types of questions and questionnaires used, and the precautions needed to ensure they produce valid data.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Introduction to the Questionnaire

A questionnaire is a structured set of questions designed to collect information from respondents in a standardised format, allowing responses from many people to be compared and analysed systematically.

2.2 Types of Questions

TypeDescription
Open-Ended QuestionsAllow respondents to answer in their own words; rich detail but harder to code and analyse.
Closed-Ended QuestionsProvide fixed response options (multiple-choice, yes/no, rating scales); easy to code and analyse, but may limit expression.
Dichotomous QuestionsOffer exactly two response options (e.g. Yes/No).
Multiple-Choice QuestionsOffer several predefined options, of which the respondent selects one or more.

2.3 Types of Questionnaires

  • Structured Questionnaire — fixed wording and order of questions, mostly closed-ended.
  • Unstructured Questionnaire — flexible, open-ended, used in exploratory research.
  • Mail/Online Questionnaire — self-administered, distributed by post or electronically.
  • Personally Administered Questionnaire — completed in the presence of a researcher or field investigator.

2.4 Construction of a Questionnaire

  1. Decide the exact information needed based on the research objectives.
  2. Decide the question types and response format.
  3. Decide the wording of each question.
  4. Decide the sequence of questions (general to specific, easy to sensitive).
  5. Decide the physical layout and length.
  6. Pre-test the questionnaire and revise.

2.5 Precautions in Questionnaire Design

  • Avoid leading, loaded or ambiguous questions.
  • Avoid double-barrelled questions (asking two things at once).
  • Keep language simple and avoid jargon.
  • Keep the questionnaire as short as the objectives permit.

2.6 Validity, Significance and Limitations

A valid questionnaire actually measures what it claims to, which is why careful wording and pre-testing matter. Questionnaires are significant because they allow large-scale, standardised, low-cost data collection, but are limited by the risk of non-response, misunderstood questions, and respondents' willingness to answer honestly.

3. Key Terms

Questionnaire
A structured set of questions designed to collect information from respondents in a standardised format.
Open-Ended Question
A question that allows respondents to answer freely in their own words.
Closed-Ended Question
A question that provides fixed response options for the respondent to choose from.
Double-Barrelled Question
A poorly worded question that asks about two issues at once, making the answer ambiguous.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Questions: open-ended (free response) vs closed-ended (fixed options, incl. dichotomous & multiple-choice).
  • Questionnaire types: structured vs unstructured; mail/online vs personally administered.
  • Construction steps: decide info needed → question types → wording → sequence → layout → pre-test.
  • Precautions: avoid leading/loaded/double-barrelled questions, keep language simple and length short.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Differentiate between open-ended and closed-ended questions.
    Show model answer
    Open-ended questions allow respondents to answer freely in their own words, providing rich detail but making coding and analysis harder. Closed-ended questions provide fixed response options such as multiple-choice or yes/no, which are easier to code and analyse but may limit how respondents can express themselves.
  2. Outline the steps in constructing a questionnaire.
    Show model answer
    Questionnaire construction involves deciding the exact information needed based on research objectives, choosing question types and response formats, carefully wording each question, sequencing questions logically, deciding on physical layout and length, and finally pre-testing and revising the instrument.
  3. What precautions should be taken when designing questionnaire items?
    Show model answer
    Designers should avoid leading, loaded or ambiguous questions, avoid double-barrelled questions that ask two things at once, use simple language free of jargon, and keep the questionnaire as short as the research objectives allow.
