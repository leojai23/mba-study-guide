Unit 3Interview Method and Experiments

Book: Business Research Methods (BA4205, Anna University MBA Sem II, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This topic covers two more primary data collection approaches: the interview, where data is gathered through direct conversation, and experiments, a brief recap of using controlled manipulation to generate data.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Interview

Introduction

An interview is a method of data collection involving direct, purposeful conversation between an interviewer and a respondent, allowing for more depth and flexibility than a self-administered questionnaire.

Characteristics

  • Involves direct interaction, allowing the interviewer to clarify questions and probe deeper.
  • Can capture non-verbal cues and context that a written questionnaire cannot.
  • Requires skilled interviewers to minimise bias from how questions are asked.

Types of Interviews

TypeDescription
Structured InterviewFixed set of questions asked in the same order to every respondent.
Unstructured InterviewOpen, flexible conversation guided by broad topics rather than fixed questions.
Semi-Structured InterviewCombines a fixed core of questions with the flexibility to probe further.
Depth InterviewIn-depth, one-on-one interview aimed at uncovering underlying motivations and attitudes.

Significance and Limitations

Interviews allow rich, detailed and flexible data collection with high response quality, but are costly, time-consuming, difficult to scale to large samples, and susceptible to interviewer bias.

4.2 Experiments

As covered under experimental research design, experiments generate primary data by deliberately manipulating an independent variable under controlled conditions and measuring the resulting change in a dependent variable. As a data collection method, experiments provide the strongest basis for establishing cause-and-effect relationships, though they are often costly and may not reflect real-world conditions as faithfully as field-based methods.

3. Key Terms

Interview
A data collection method involving direct, purposeful conversation between an interviewer and a respondent.
Structured Interview
An interview using a fixed set of questions asked in the same order to every respondent.
Depth Interview
An in-depth, one-on-one interview aimed at uncovering underlying motivations and attitudes.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Interview = direct conversation; allows clarification, probing, capturing non-verbal cues.
  • Interview types: structured, unstructured, semi-structured, depth interview.
  • Interviews: rich/flexible data but costly, hard to scale, risk of interviewer bias.
  • Experiments: manipulate IV under controlled conditions, strongest for establishing causality.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What are the characteristics of the interview method of data collection?
    Show model answer
    Interviews involve direct interaction, allowing the interviewer to clarify questions and probe deeper, and can capture non-verbal cues and context that a written questionnaire cannot. They require skilled interviewers to minimise bias introduced by how questions are asked.
  2. Differentiate between structured and unstructured interviews.
    Show model answer
    A structured interview uses a fixed set of questions asked in the same order to every respondent, producing standardised, comparable data. An unstructured interview is an open, flexible conversation guided by broad topics rather than fixed questions, allowing deeper exploration at the cost of standardisation.
  3. What are the significance and limitations of interviews as a data collection method?
    Show model answer
    Interviews allow rich, detailed and flexible data collection with generally high response quality, since the interviewer can clarify questions and probe further. Their limitations include high cost, difficulty scaling to large samples, and the risk of interviewer bias influencing responses.
