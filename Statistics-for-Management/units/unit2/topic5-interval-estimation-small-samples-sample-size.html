Unit 2Interval Estimation for Small Samples and Determining Sample Size

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This closing topic of Unit 2 covers interval estimation when the sample size is small, which requires the t-distribution rather than the normal distribution, and how to determine the sample size needed before data collection even begins.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Why Small Samples Need a Different Approach

When the sample size is small (commonly, n < 30) and the population standard deviation σ is unknown, the sample standard deviation s is a less reliable estimate of σ. Using the normal Z-distribution in this situation understates the true uncertainty, so the t-distribution is used instead.

5.2 The t-Distribution

The t-distribution is similar in shape to the normal distribution — symmetric and bell-shaped — but with heavier tails, reflecting the extra uncertainty from estimating σ with a small sample. Its exact shape depends on the degrees of freedom (df), df = n − 1 for a single sample. As the sample size increases, the t-distribution approaches the normal distribution.

5.3 Confidence Interval for the Mean (Small Sample)

x̄ ± tα/2, df × (s ÷ √n)

where tα/2, df is the critical t-value for the desired confidence level with df = n − 1 degrees of freedom, taken from t-distribution tables, and s is the sample standard deviation.

5.4 Determining the Sample Size

Before collecting data, researchers often need to determine the minimum sample size required to estimate a parameter within a desired margin of error, E, at a given confidence level.

Sample Size for Estimating a Mean

n = (Zα/2 × σ ÷ E)²

Sample Size for Estimating a Proportion

n = [Zα/2² × p(1−p)] ÷ E²

where E is the desired margin of error. If no prior estimate of p is available, p = 0.5 is used, since this produces the most conservative (largest) required sample size.

5.5 Factors Affecting Required Sample Size

  • A smaller desired margin of error E requires a larger sample size.
  • A higher confidence level requires a larger sample size (larger Zα/2).
  • Greater population variability (larger σ, or p closer to 0.5) requires a larger sample size.

3. Key Terms

t-Distribution
A probability distribution similar to the normal distribution but with heavier tails, used for small-sample inference when the population standard deviation is unknown.
Degrees of Freedom (df)
A parameter of the t-distribution, equal to n - 1 for a single-sample confidence interval, that determines its exact shape.
Margin of Error (E)
The maximum expected difference between a point estimate and the true population parameter, used when determining required sample size.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Small sample (n < 30), sigma unknown: use t-distribution instead of Z; df = n - 1; t-distribution has heavier tails than normal, approaches normal as n grows.
  • CI for mean (small sample): x-bar +/- t(alpha/2, df) x (s / sqrt(n)).
  • Sample size for mean: n = (Z(alpha/2) x sigma / E)^2.
  • Sample size for proportion: n = [Z(alpha/2)^2 x p(1-p)] / E^2; use p = 0.5 if no prior estimate (most conservative, largest n).
  • Smaller margin of error, higher confidence level, or greater variability all increase the required sample size.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Why is the t-distribution used instead of the normal distribution for small samples?
    Show model answer
    When the sample size is small (commonly n < 30) and the population standard deviation is unknown, the sample standard deviation is a less reliable estimate, so using the normal distribution would understate the true uncertainty. The t-distribution, with its heavier tails that reflect this extra uncertainty, is used instead, with the exact shape depending on the degrees of freedom (df = n - 1).
  2. Write the formula for the confidence interval of the mean for a small sample.
    Show model answer
    x-bar +/- t(alpha/2, df) x (s / sqrt(n)), where t(alpha/2, df) is the critical t-value for the desired confidence level with df = n - 1 degrees of freedom, and s is the sample standard deviation.
  3. How is the required sample size determined for estimating a population mean?
    Show model answer
    Using n = (Z(alpha/2) x sigma / E)^2, where E is the desired margin of error, sigma is the population standard deviation (or an estimate), and Z(alpha/2) is the Z-value for the desired confidence level.
  4. What value of p is used when determining sample size for a proportion if no prior estimate exists? Why?
    Show model answer
    p = 0.5 is used, because p(1-p) is maximised at p = 0.5, which produces the largest (most conservative) required sample size, ensuring the sample is large enough regardless of the true proportion.
