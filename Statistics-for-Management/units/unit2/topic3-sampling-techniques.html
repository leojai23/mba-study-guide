Unit 2Sampling Techniques

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

How a sample is selected affects how well it represents the population. This topic covers the main sampling techniques used in management research, divided into probability and non-probability methods.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Probability Sampling Techniques

In probability sampling, every unit in the population has a known, non-zero chance of being selected, which allows sampling error to be objectively measured.

  1. Simple Random Sampling: Every unit in the population has an equal chance of being selected, typically using random number generation.
  2. Systematic Sampling: Units are selected at a fixed interval from a randomly chosen starting point (e.g. every 10th customer).
  3. Stratified Sampling: The population is divided into relevant sub-groups (strata), and a random sample is drawn from each stratum, ensuring every sub-group is represented proportionally.
  4. Cluster Sampling: The population is divided into clusters (often geographic), a random sample of clusters is selected, and all (or a sample of) units within the chosen clusters are studied.

3.2 Non-Probability Sampling Techniques

In non-probability sampling, units are selected based on convenience or judgement rather than random chance, so the probability of selection is unknown and sampling error cannot be objectively calculated.

  1. Convenience Sampling: Units are selected simply because they are easy to access (e.g. surveying shoppers at a nearby mall).
  2. Judgemental (Purposive) Sampling: The researcher deliberately selects units believed to be most representative or informative.
  3. Quota Sampling: The researcher ensures certain sub-group quotas are filled, but without randomly selecting within each sub-group.
  4. Snowball Sampling: Existing study participants recruit future participants from among their own acquaintances, often used for hard-to-reach populations.

3.3 Choosing a Sampling Technique

Probability sampling methods are generally preferred for formal statistical inference, since they allow sampling error to be measured and confidence intervals to be constructed validly. Non-probability methods are often used when they are faster, cheaper, or the only practical option, but any conclusions drawn carry greater risk of bias.

3. Key Terms

Probability Sampling
A sampling method in which every unit in the population has a known, non-zero chance of selection.
Stratified Sampling
A probability sampling method in which the population is divided into sub-groups (strata), and a random sample is drawn from each.
Cluster Sampling
A probability sampling method in which the population is divided into clusters, a sample of clusters is randomly chosen, and units within those clusters are studied.
Non-Probability Sampling
A sampling method in which units are selected based on convenience or judgement, so the probability of selection is unknown.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Probability sampling (known selection chance): Simple Random, Systematic, Stratified, Cluster.
  • Non-probability sampling (unknown selection chance): Convenience, Judgemental/Purposive, Quota, Snowball.
  • Probability sampling preferred for formal statistical inference since sampling error can be measured.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Explain the main probability sampling techniques.
    Show model answer
    Simple Random Sampling gives every unit an equal chance of selection; Systematic Sampling selects units at a fixed interval from a random start; Stratified Sampling divides the population into sub-groups and samples randomly from each; Cluster Sampling selects whole clusters at random and studies units within them.
  2. Explain the main non-probability sampling techniques.
    Show model answer
    Convenience Sampling selects units that are easy to access; Judgemental (Purposive) Sampling deliberately selects units believed to be representative; Quota Sampling fills sub-group quotas without random selection within them; Snowball Sampling has existing participants recruit further participants, useful for hard-to-reach populations.
  3. Why is probability sampling generally preferred for statistical inference?
    Show model answer
    Because every unit has a known chance of selection, probability sampling allows sampling error to be objectively measured and valid confidence intervals to be constructed, whereas non-probability sampling carries unknown and potentially greater bias.
