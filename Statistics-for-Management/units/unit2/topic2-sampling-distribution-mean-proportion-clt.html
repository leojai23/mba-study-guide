Unit 2Sampling Distribution of Mean and Proportion, and the Central Limit Theorem

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This topic covers the sampling distributions of two of the most commonly used statistics — the sample mean and the sample proportion — and the Central Limit Theorem, one of the most important results in all of statistics.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Sampling Distribution of the Mean

If repeated random samples of size n are drawn from a population with mean μ and standard deviation σ, the sampling distribution of the sample mean x̄ has:

  • Mean of the sampling distribution: equal to the population mean, μx̄ = μ.
  • Standard Error of the Mean: σx̄ = σ ÷ √n

Notice that as the sample size n increases, the standard error decreases — larger samples give more precise (less variable) estimates of the population mean.

2.2 The Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

The Central Limit Theorem states that, for a sufficiently large sample size n (commonly, n ≥ 30), the sampling distribution of the sample mean x̄ will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the shape of the population distribution from which the sample is drawn.

Why the CLT Matters

  • It allows the normal distribution (and the Z-score method from Unit 1) to be used for inference about the sample mean, even when the population itself is not normally distributed.
  • It is the theoretical foundation for virtually all large-sample hypothesis tests and confidence intervals covered in the remaining units.
  • The larger the sample size, the closer the sampling distribution of x̄ gets to a perfect normal shape.

2.3 Sampling Distribution of the Proportion

When a population proportion p (e.g. the proportion of defective items) is being estimated from a sample, the sample proportion is denoted p̅ (p-hat). For a sufficiently large sample size, the sampling distribution of p̅ is also approximately normal, with:

  • Mean: μp̅ = p
  • Standard Error of the Proportion: σp̅ = √[p(1−p) ÷ n]

As with the mean, the standard error of the proportion decreases as the sample size n increases, giving a more precise estimate of the true population proportion.

3. Key Terms

Standard Error of the Mean
The standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample mean, equal to sigma divided by the square root of n.
Central Limit Theorem (CLT)
The theorem stating that, for a sufficiently large sample size, the sampling distribution of the sample mean is approximately normal regardless of the population's shape.
Sample Proportion (p-hat)
The proportion of a characteristic observed in a sample, used to estimate the population proportion p.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Sampling distribution of the mean: mean = mu, standard error = sigma / sqrt(n).
  • Central Limit Theorem: for n >= 30 (roughly), sampling distribution of x-bar is approximately normal regardless of the population's shape.
  • Sampling distribution of the proportion: mean = p, standard error = sqrt[p(1-p)/n].
  • Standard error (of mean or proportion) decreases as sample size n increases.

5. Practice Questions

  1. State the mean and standard error of the sampling distribution of the sample mean.
    Show model answer
    The mean of the sampling distribution of the sample mean equals the population mean, mu. The standard error is sigma / sqrt(n), where sigma is the population standard deviation and n is the sample size.
  2. State the Central Limit Theorem. Why is it important?
    Show model answer
    For a sufficiently large sample size (commonly n >= 30), the sampling distribution of the sample mean is approximately normally distributed, regardless of the shape of the population distribution. It is important because it allows normal-distribution-based methods to be used for inference about the sample mean even when the underlying population is not normal, and it underlies most large-sample hypothesis tests and confidence intervals.
  3. State the mean and standard error of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion.
    Show model answer
    The mean of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion p-hat equals the population proportion p. The standard error is the square root of [p(1-p)/n].
