Unit 2Introduction to Sampling Distributions

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This unit moves from probability theory to statistical inference — using a sample to draw conclusions about a population. This opening topic introduces the key idea that makes this possible: the sampling distribution.

2. Detailed Notes

1.1 Population and Sample

A population is the complete set of all items or individuals of interest in a study. A sample is a subset of the population actually observed and measured. Since studying an entire population is often impractical, statistics uses samples to estimate population characteristics.

1.2 Parameter and Statistic

TermMeaningExample Symbol
ParameterA numerical characteristic of a population (usually unknown).Population mean μ, population standard deviation σ
StatisticA numerical characteristic calculated from a sample, used to estimate the corresponding parameter.Sample mean x̄, sample standard deviation s

1.3 Introduction to Sampling Distributions

If repeated samples of the same size are drawn from a population, the value of a statistic (such as the sample mean) will differ from sample to sample. The sampling distribution of a statistic is the probability distribution of that statistic across all possible samples of a given size drawn from the population.

Sampling distributions are the theoretical bridge that lets us use a single sample's statistic to make a probability-based inference about the unknown population parameter — the entire logic of hypothesis testing and interval estimation in the next units rests on this idea.

1.4 Sampling Error

Sampling error is the difference between a sample statistic and the true population parameter, arising purely because a sample (rather than the whole population) was observed. Sampling error decreases, on average, as sample size increases.

1.5 Standard Error

The standard error of a statistic is the standard deviation of its sampling distribution — it measures how much the statistic is expected to vary from sample to sample. A smaller standard error means the sample statistic is likely to be closer to the true population parameter.

3. Key Terms

Population
The complete set of all items or individuals of interest in a study.
Sample
A subset of the population that is actually observed and measured.
Parameter
A numerical characteristic of a population, usually unknown, such as the population mean mu.
Statistic
A numerical characteristic calculated from a sample, used to estimate a population parameter.
Sampling Distribution
The probability distribution of a statistic across all possible samples of a given size drawn from a population.
Standard Error
The standard deviation of the sampling distribution of a statistic, measuring how much it varies from sample to sample.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Population = entire group of interest; Sample = subset actually observed.
  • Parameter (population, e.g. mu) vs Statistic (sample, e.g. x-bar, used to estimate the parameter).
  • Sampling distribution = probability distribution of a statistic across all possible samples of a given size.
  • Sampling error = difference between sample statistic and true population parameter; decreases as sample size increases.
  • Standard error = standard deviation of the sampling distribution; smaller standard error = more precise estimate.
