Unit 4Rank Sum Test: Mann-Whitney U Test

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

The Mann-Whitney U Test is the non-parametric counterpart to the independent two-sample t-test, used to compare two independent groups when the data is ranked rather than normally distributed.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 Purpose of the Mann-Whitney U Test

The Mann-Whitney U Test (also called the Rank Sum Test or Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney Test) compares two independent samples to determine whether they come from populations with the same distribution, without assuming normality. It is the non-parametric equivalent of the independent two-sample t-test.

2.2 Hypotheses

H₀: The two populations have identical distributions (no difference between the groups).
H₁: The two populations differ (one tends to produce larger values than the other).

2.3 Procedure

  1. Combine and Rank: Combine both samples into a single group and rank all observations together from smallest (rank 1) to largest, regardless of which sample each value came from.
  2. Sum the Ranks: Sum the ranks separately for each original sample, giving R₁ and R₂.
  3. Calculate the U-Statistics:

U₁ = n₁n₂ + [n₁(n₁+1) ÷ 2] − R₁

U₂ = n₁n₂ − U₁

where n₁, n₂ are the two sample sizes. The smaller of U₁ and U₂ is used as the test statistic, U.

  1. Compare and Decide: For small samples, U is compared against critical values from Mann-Whitney U tables. For larger samples, U can be converted to an approximately normal Z-statistic and compared against standard normal critical values.

2.4 Handling Tied Ranks

When two or more observations have the same value, each is assigned the average of the ranks they would have occupied (e.g. if two values tie for ranks 3 and 4, both receive rank 3.5).

2.5 When to Use the Mann-Whitney U Test

  • Comparing two independent groups when the data is ordinal, or numerical but not normally distributed.
  • Comparing two independent groups when sample sizes are too small to assess normality reliably.
  • As a more robust alternative to the two-sample t-test when outliers are present.

3. Key Terms

Mann-Whitney U Test
A non-parametric test comparing two independent samples by ranking all observations together, used as an alternative to the independent two-sample t-test.
U-Statistic
The test statistic in the Mann-Whitney U Test, based on the sum of ranks for each sample; the smaller of U1 and U2 is used.
Tied Ranks
Ranks assigned when two or more observations have equal values, calculated as the average of the ranks the tied values would otherwise occupy.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Mann-Whitney U = non-parametric alternative to independent two-sample t-test; H0: identical distributions; H1: distributions differ.
  • Procedure: combine and rank all observations together → sum ranks per sample (R1, R2) → calculate U1 = n1n2 + n1(n1+1)/2 - R1, U2 = n1n2 - U1 → use smaller U.
  • Tied values: assign the average of the ranks they would occupy.
  • Used for ordinal data, non-normal data, small samples, or when outliers would distort a t-test.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What is the Mann-Whitney U Test used for?
    Show model answer
    It is used to compare two independent samples to determine whether they come from populations with the same distribution, without assuming normality; it is the non-parametric equivalent of the independent two-sample t-test.
  2. Explain the procedure for conducting the Mann-Whitney U Test.
    Show model answer
    Combine both samples and rank all observations together from smallest to largest; sum the ranks separately for each sample (R1 and R2); calculate U1 = n1n2 + n1(n1+1)/2 - R1 and U2 = n1n2 - U1; use the smaller of U1 and U2 as the test statistic and compare it against critical values (or a normal approximation for larger samples).
  3. How are tied ranks handled in the Mann-Whitney U Test?
    Show model answer
    When two or more observations have the same value, each is assigned the average of the ranks they would have occupied had they not been tied.
