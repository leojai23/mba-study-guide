Unit 4Chi-Square Test for Independence of Attributes

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This closing topic of Unit 4 covers the Chi-Square Test for Independence, used to determine whether two categorical attributes (e.g. gender and product preference) are statistically related or independent of each other.

2. Detailed Notes

6.1 Purpose of the Test

The Chi-Square Test for Independence determines whether two categorical variables (attributes) are statistically independent of each other, or whether there is a significant association between them, based on data in a contingency table (also called a cross-tabulation).

6.2 Hypotheses

H₀: The two attributes are independent (no association between them).
H₁: The two attributes are not independent (there is an association between them).

6.3 Contingency Table

Data is arranged in a table with the categories of one attribute as rows and the categories of the other as columns, with each cell showing the observed frequency (count) of items falling into that combination of categories.

6.4 Calculating Expected Frequencies

Under the assumption of independence (H₀), the expected frequency for each cell is:

E = (Row Total × Column Total) ÷ Grand Total

6.5 The Chi-Square Statistic

χ² = Σ [(O − E)² ÷ E]

summed across every cell in the contingency table, where O is the observed frequency and E is the expected frequency (calculated as above) for that cell.

6.6 Degrees of Freedom

df = (r − 1) × (c − 1)

where r is the number of rows (categories of the first attribute) and c is the number of columns (categories of the second attribute) in the contingency table.

6.7 Decision Rule

The calculated χ² is compared against the critical χ² value for the chosen significance level and df = (r−1)(c−1). If calculated χ² exceeds the critical value, H₀ is rejected, concluding there is a statistically significant association between the two attributes.

6.8 Applications in Management

  • Testing whether customer satisfaction level is associated with the store branch visited.
  • Testing whether employee department is associated with preferred training format.
  • Testing whether product defect type is associated with the production shift during which the item was made.
  • Market research — testing whether brand preference is associated with demographic categories such as age group or region.

Important Caveat

A statistically significant Chi-Square result shows an association exists, but it does not by itself indicate the strength or the direction of that association — further measures (such as Cramer's V) or examination of the contingency table itself are needed for that.

3. Key Terms

Chi-Square Test for Independence
A test determining whether two categorical attributes are statistically independent, based on data in a contingency table.
Contingency Table
A table cross-tabulating the observed frequencies of two categorical attributes, with one attribute's categories as rows and the other's as columns.
Expected Frequency (Independence Test)
The frequency expected in a contingency table cell under the assumption of independence, calculated as (Row Total x Column Total) / Grand Total.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Chi-Square Test for Independence: H0 = two attributes are independent; uses a contingency table (rows x columns).
  • Expected frequency per cell: E = (Row Total x Column Total) / Grand Total.
  • chi^2 = Sum[(O-E)^2/E] across all cells; df = (r-1)(c-1).
  • Significant result = association exists, but doesn't show strength/direction by itself.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What does the Chi-Square Test for Independence check for?
    Show model answer
    It checks whether two categorical attributes (such as gender and product preference) are statistically independent of each other, or whether there is a significant association between them, using data arranged in a contingency table.
  2. How is the expected frequency calculated for a cell in a contingency table?
    Show model answer
    E = (Row Total x Column Total) / Grand Total, calculated under the assumption that the two attributes are independent.
  3. State the formula for degrees of freedom in the Chi-Square Test for Independence.
    Show model answer
    df = (r - 1) x (c - 1), where r is the number of rows (categories of the first attribute) and c is the number of columns (categories of the second attribute) in the contingency table.
  4. Give two management applications of the Chi-Square Test for Independence.
    Show model answer
    Testing whether customer satisfaction level is associated with the store branch visited, and testing whether brand preference is associated with a demographic category such as age group, in market research.
