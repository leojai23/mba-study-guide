Unit 4Kruskal-Wallis Test

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

The Kruskal-Wallis Test extends the logic of the Mann-Whitney U Test from two groups to three or more, serving as the non-parametric counterpart to One-Way ANOVA.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Purpose of the Kruskal-Wallis Test

The Kruskal-Wallis Test compares three or more independent samples to determine whether they come from populations with the same distribution, without assuming normality. It is the non-parametric equivalent of One-Way ANOVA.

3.2 Hypotheses

H₀: All k populations have identical distributions (no difference among the groups).
H₁: At least one population differs from the others.

3.3 Procedure

  1. Combine and Rank: Combine all k samples into one group and rank all observations together from smallest to largest, using average ranks for ties.
  2. Sum the Ranks per Group: Calculate the sum of ranks R₁, R₂, …, Rk for each of the k groups.
  3. Calculate the Test Statistic, H:

H = [12 ÷ N(N+1)] × Σ(Ri² ÷ ni) − 3(N+1)

where N is the total number of observations across all groups, ni is the sample size of group i, and Ri is the rank sum of group i.

3.4 Distribution of the Test Statistic

For reasonably sized samples, H approximately follows a Chi-Square (χ²) distribution with df = k − 1 degrees of freedom, where k is the number of groups.

3.5 Decision Rule

The calculated H is compared against the critical χ² value at the chosen significance level with df = k − 1. If H exceeds this critical value, H₀ is rejected, concluding that at least one group's distribution differs from the others.

3.6 Kruskal-Wallis vs One-Way ANOVA

BasisOne-Way ANOVAKruskal-Wallis Test
Data requiredInterval/ratio, approximately normal within groupsOrdinal, or numerical without a normality assumption
Works onActual values (via means)Ranks of the combined data
Test statisticFH (approximately χ²)

As with ANOVA, a significant Kruskal-Wallis result indicates that at least one group differs, but does not by itself identify which group(s); a follow-up test is needed for that.

3. Key Terms

Kruskal-Wallis Test
A non-parametric test comparing three or more independent samples using ranks, equivalent to One-Way ANOVA.
H-Statistic
The test statistic of the Kruskal-Wallis Test, which approximately follows a Chi-Square distribution with k-1 degrees of freedom.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Kruskal-Wallis = non-parametric alternative to One-Way ANOVA, for 3+ independent groups; H0: all groups have identical distributions.
  • Procedure: combine and rank all observations → sum ranks per group → H = [12/N(N+1)] * Sum(Ri^2/ni) - 3(N+1).
  • H approximately follows Chi-Square distribution with df = k-1 (k = number of groups).
  • Reject H0 if H(calculated) > critical Chi-Square value; doesn't identify which specific group(s) differ.
