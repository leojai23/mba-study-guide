Unit 5Regression: Regression Lines and the Method of Least Squares

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

While correlation measures how strongly two variables are related, regression goes further, providing an equation that can be used to predict one variable from the other. This topic introduces regression lines and the method used to fit them: Least Squares.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Meaning of Regression

Regression analysis is a statistical technique for modelling the relationship between a dependent variable (Y, the variable being predicted) and one or more independent variables (X, the variable(s) used to make the prediction), expressed as a mathematical equation.

3.2 Correlation vs Regression

BasisCorrelationRegression
PurposeMeasures the strength and direction of associationModels the relationship to allow prediction
VariablesSymmetric — no distinction between X and YAsymmetric — a clear dependent (Y) and independent (X) variable
OutputA single coefficient (r)An equation (regression line)

3.3 The Simple Linear Regression Line

The regression of Y on X is expressed as a straight-line equation:

Y = a + bX

where a is the Y-intercept (the predicted value of Y when X = 0), and b is the slope (the regression coefficient, representing the average change in Y for a one-unit increase in X).

3.4 The Method of Least Squares

The Method of Least Squares is used to find the specific values of a and b that make the regression line the "best fit" to the data — specifically, the line that minimises the sum of squared vertical distances between each actual observed Y-value and the Y-value predicted by the line.

Formulas for a and b

b = [nΣXY − (ΣX)(ΣY)] ÷ [nΣX² − (ΣX)²]

a = Ȳ − bX̄

where X̄ and Ȳ are the means of X and Y respectively.

3.5 Two Regression Lines

In general, there are two different regression lines for a given pair of variables: the regression of Y on X (used to predict Y from X, as above) and the regression of X on Y (used to predict X from Y). These two lines are generally different from each other, and only coincide exactly when r = ±1 (perfect correlation).

3.6 Relationship Between r, and the Two Regression Coefficients

If bYX is the slope of Y on X, and bXY is the slope of X on Y, then:

r² = bYX × bXY

This relationship is a useful check: since r² must lie between 0 and 1, the product of the two regression coefficients must also lie between 0 and 1.

3. Key Terms

Regression Analysis
A statistical technique modelling the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables as an equation.
Dependent Variable (Y)
The variable being predicted or explained in a regression model.
Independent Variable (X)
The variable used to predict or explain the dependent variable in a regression model.
Method of Least Squares
A method for fitting a regression line by minimising the sum of squared vertical distances between observed and predicted Y-values.
Regression Coefficient (b)
The slope of a regression line, representing the average change in the dependent variable for a one-unit increase in the independent variable.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Regression: dependent variable (Y, predicted) vs independent variable (X, predictor); models relationship as an equation, unlike correlation's single coefficient.
  • Simple linear regression: Y = a + bX (a = intercept, b = slope).
  • Least Squares: b = [nSumXY - (SumX)(SumY)] / [nSumX^2 - (SumX)^2]; a = Ybar - b*Xbar.
  • Two regression lines exist (Y on X, X on Y); they coincide only when r = +/-1.
  • r^2 = bYX * bXY (product of the two regression slopes equals r-squared).

5. Practice Questions

  1. Differentiate between correlation and regression.
    Show model answer
    Correlation measures the strength and direction of association between two variables symmetrically, producing a single coefficient. Regression models the relationship as an equation with a clear dependent (Y) and independent (X) variable, allowing prediction of Y from X.
  2. Explain the method of least squares.
    Show model answer
    The method of least squares finds the regression line values of a (intercept) and b (slope) that minimise the sum of squared vertical distances between each actual observed Y-value and the Y-value predicted by the line, giving the 'best fit' straight line to the data.
  3. Why are there two regression lines for a pair of variables? When do they coincide?
    Show model answer
    There is a regression of Y on X (predicting Y from X) and a regression of X on Y (predicting X from Y), which are generally different lines. They coincide exactly only when the correlation coefficient r = +1 or r = -1, i.e. when there is perfect correlation.
  4. State the relationship between r-squared and the two regression coefficients.
    Show model answer
    r-squared equals the product of the two regression coefficients: r^2 = bYX x bXY, where bYX is the slope of Y on X and bXY is the slope of X on Y.
