Unit 1Theorems of Probability, Conditional Probability and Bayes' Theorem

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

This topic covers the two core theorems used to combine probabilities — addition and multiplication — followed by conditional probability and Bayes' Theorem, which lets probabilities be revised in light of new information.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 Addition Theorem of Probability

For any two events A and B:

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)

If A and B are mutually exclusive (cannot occur together), then P(A ∩ B) = 0, which simplifies the rule to:

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)

3.2 Multiplication Theorem of Probability

For any two events A and B:

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B | A)

If A and B are independent (the occurrence of one does not affect the probability of the other), then P(B | A) = P(B), which simplifies the rule to:

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)

3.3 Conditional Probability

Conditional probability is the probability that event B occurs, given that event A has already occurred, written P(B | A):

P(B | A) = P(A ∩ B) ÷ P(A), provided P(A) > 0

Conditional probability is the basis for understanding independence: A and B are independent exactly when P(B | A) = P(B), i.e. knowing A occurred does not change the probability of B.

3.4 Bayes' Theorem

Need for Bayes' Theorem

In many real situations, we know the probability of an effect given a cause, but we actually want the reverse — the probability of a particular cause given that we have observed an effect. Bayes' Theorem provides a formal way to "invert" conditional probabilities to answer this kind of question.

Statement of Bayes' Theorem

If B₁, B₂, …, Bₙ are mutually exclusive and exhaustive events, and A is any event, then for a specific Bᵢ:

P(Bᵢ | A) = [P(Bᵢ) × P(A | Bᵢ)] ÷ Σ [P(Bᵇ) × P(A | Bᵇ)]

In words: the posterior probability of a cause Bᵢ, given that effect A was observed, is proportional to the prior probability of Bᵢ multiplied by the likelihood of observing A given Bᵢ, normalised by summing this product across all possible causes.

Applications of Bayes' Theorem

  • Revising the probability a product is defective, after a quality check gives a positive/negative signal.
  • Medical diagnosis — updating the probability of a disease given a test result.
  • Market research — updating the probability a customer segment will respond to a campaign, given early response data.
  • Credit risk assessment — updating the probability of default given new financial information about a borrower.

3. Key Terms

Addition Theorem
P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B), simplifying to P(A) + P(B) for mutually exclusive events.
Multiplication Theorem
P(A ∩ B) = P(A) x P(B|A), simplifying to P(A) x P(B) for independent events.
Conditional Probability
The probability of event B occurring given that event A has already occurred, P(B|A) = P(A ∩ B) / P(A).
Bayes' Theorem
A rule for finding the posterior probability of a cause given an observed effect, based on prior probabilities and likelihoods.
Prior Probability
The probability of an event assigned before new evidence is taken into account.
Posterior Probability
The revised probability of an event after new evidence has been incorporated, calculated using Bayes' Theorem.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Addition theorem: P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A∩B); mutually exclusive ⇒ P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B).
  • Multiplication theorem: P(A∩B) = P(A) x P(B|A); independent events ⇒ P(A∩B) = P(A) x P(B).
  • Conditional probability: P(B|A) = P(A∩B) / P(A).
  • Bayes' Theorem revises prior probabilities into posterior probabilities using observed evidence; used in quality control, diagnosis, market research, credit risk.

5. Practice Questions

  1. State the addition theorem of probability. When does it simplify?
    Show model answer
    P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B). It simplifies to P(A) + P(B) when A and B are mutually exclusive, since P(A ∩ B) = 0 in that case.
  2. State the multiplication theorem of probability. When does it simplify?
    Show model answer
    P(A ∩ B) = P(A) x P(B|A). It simplifies to P(A) x P(B) when A and B are independent, since P(B|A) = P(B) in that case.
  3. Define conditional probability with its formula.
    Show model answer
    Conditional probability is the probability of event B occurring given that event A has already occurred: P(B|A) = P(A ∩ B) / P(A), provided P(A) > 0.
  4. Why is Bayes' Theorem needed? State the theorem.
    Show model answer
    Bayes' Theorem is needed because we often know the probability of an effect given a cause but want the reverse: the probability of a cause given an observed effect. For mutually exclusive, exhaustive events B1...Bn and event A: P(Bi|A) = [P(Bi) x P(A|Bi)] / Sum of [P(Bk) x P(A|Bk)] across all k.
  5. State any two business applications of Bayes' Theorem.
    Show model answer
    Bayes' Theorem is used in quality control to revise the probability a product is defective after a test result, and in credit risk assessment to update the probability of a borrower defaulting given new financial information.
