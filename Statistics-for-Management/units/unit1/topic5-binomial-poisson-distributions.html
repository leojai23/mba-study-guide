Unit 1Binomial and Poisson Distributions

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Binomial and Poisson distributions are the two most widely used discrete probability distributions in management. This topic covers their definitions, assumptions, characteristics and applications.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Binomial Distribution

5.1.1 Definition and Probability Function

The Binomial Distribution describes the number of "successes" in a fixed number of independent trials, each with the same probability of success. If X is the number of successes in n trials with probability of success p on each trial:

P(X = x) = nCx × px × q(n−x)

where q = 1 − p (probability of failure), and x = 0, 1, 2, …, n.

5.1.2 Assumptions of the Binomial Distribution

  • There are a fixed number of trials, n.
  • Each trial has only two possible outcomes — success or failure.
  • The probability of success, p, is constant across all trials.
  • The trials are statistically independent of each other.

5.1.3 Characteristics of the Binomial Distribution

  • Mean = n × p.
  • Variance = n × p × q.
  • The distribution is symmetric when p = 0.5, and skewed otherwise.

5.1.4 & 5.1.5 Fitting and Applications of the Binomial Distribution

A binomial distribution is "fitted" to real data by estimating n and p from the context, then using the formula to generate expected frequencies for comparison with observed data. It is applied in quality control (number of defectives in a sample), market research (number of customers preferring a product in a survey), and any scenario with a fixed number of independent yes/no trials.

5.2 Poisson Distribution

5.2.1 Definition and Probability Function

The Poisson Distribution describes the number of times an event occurs in a fixed interval of time or space, when these events happen independently and at a constant average rate:

P(X = x) = (e−λ × λx) ÷ x!

where λ (lambda) is the average number of occurrences in the interval, e ≈ 2.71828, and x = 0, 1, 2, …

5.2.2 & 5.2.3 Characteristics and Assumptions of the Poisson Distribution

  • Events occur independently of one another.
  • The average rate of occurrence, λ, is constant over the interval.
  • Two events cannot occur at exactly the same instant.
  • Mean = λ and Variance = λ (the mean and variance are equal).

5.2.4 & 5.2.5 Fitting and Applications of the Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution is especially useful as an approximation to the Binomial distribution when n is large and p is small (rare events over many trials). Typical applications include the number of customer complaints per day, the number of machine breakdowns per week, the number of accidents at an intersection per month, and the number of calls arriving at a call centre per hour.

3. Key Terms

Binomial Distribution
A discrete probability distribution for the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials, each with the same success probability.
Poisson Distribution
A discrete probability distribution for the number of times a rare, independent event occurs in a fixed interval of time or space.
Lambda (λ)
The average rate of occurrence of an event in a given interval, used as the parameter of the Poisson distribution.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Binomial: P(X=x) = nCx * p^x * q^(n-x); Mean = np; Variance = npq; needs fixed n, two outcomes, constant p, independent trials.
  • Poisson: P(X=x) = (e^-lambda * lambda^x) / x!; Mean = Variance = lambda; used for rare events over many trials (Binomial approximation when n large, p small).
  • Binomial applications: quality control, survey preference counts; Poisson applications: complaints, breakdowns, accidents, call arrivals.

5. Practice Questions

  1. State the probability function of the Binomial distribution and its assumptions.
    Show model answer
    P(X=x) = nCx x p^x x q^(n-x), where q = 1-p. Assumptions: a fixed number of trials n, only two possible outcomes per trial, a constant probability of success p across trials, and statistically independent trials.
  2. State the mean and variance of the Binomial distribution.
    Show model answer
    Mean = n x p; Variance = n x p x q, where q = 1 - p.
  3. State the probability function of the Poisson distribution. When is it used?
    Show model answer
    P(X=x) = (e^-lambda x lambda^x) / x!, where lambda is the average rate of occurrence. It is used for the number of times a rare, independent event occurs in a fixed interval, and as an approximation to the Binomial distribution when n is large and p is small.
  4. What is a special property of the Poisson distribution's mean and variance?
    Show model answer
    For the Poisson distribution, the mean and variance are equal, both equal to lambda, the average rate of occurrence.
  5. Give two practical applications each of the Binomial and Poisson distributions.
    Show model answer
    Binomial: counting defective items in a quality-control sample, and counting customers who prefer a product in a market survey. Poisson: counting customer complaints received per day, and counting machine breakdowns per week.
