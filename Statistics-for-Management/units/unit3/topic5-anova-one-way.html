Unit 3ANOVA — One Way

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

While a t-test compares two group means, One-Way ANOVA (Analysis of Variance) extends the comparison to three or more groups at once, testing whether at least one group's mean differs from the others.

2. Detailed Notes

5.1 Why ANOVA Is Needed

Running repeated t-tests on every possible pair among three or more groups inflates the overall chance of a Type I error. One-Way ANOVA instead tests all group means simultaneously in a single test, comparing variation between groups to variation within groups.

5.2 Hypotheses for One-Way ANOVA

H₀: μ₁ = μ₂ = μ₃ = … = μk (all k population means are equal)
H₁: At least one population mean is different from the others.

5.3 The Logic of ANOVA

ANOVA partitions the total variability in the data into two sources:

  • Between-Group Variation (SSB): Variation due to differences between the group means themselves.
  • Within-Group Variation (SSW): Variation among individual observations within each group, around their own group mean (random/error variation).

If the between-group variation is large relative to the within-group variation, this suggests the group means genuinely differ.

5.4 The F-Statistic for ANOVA

F = MSB ÷ MSW

where MSB (Mean Square Between) = SSB ÷ (k − 1), and MSW (Mean Square Within) = SSW ÷ (N − k), with k = number of groups and N = total number of observations across all groups.

5.6 Decision Rule and Interpretation

The calculated F is compared to the critical F-value (using df₁ = k−1 and df₂ = N−k). If Fcalculated > Fcritical, H₀ is rejected, concluding that at least one group mean differs significantly from the others. ANOVA itself does not identify which group(s) differ — that requires a follow-up post-hoc test.

5.7 Assumptions of ANOVA

  • Observations are independent, both within and between groups.
  • Each population from which the groups are drawn is approximately normally distributed.
  • The populations have approximately equal variances (homogeneity of variance), which can be checked using the F-test from the previous topic.

3. Key Terms

One-Way ANOVA
A statistical test comparing the means of three or more groups simultaneously by comparing between-group and within-group variation.
Between-Group Variation (SSB)
The portion of total variability in ANOVA due to differences between the group means.
Within-Group Variation (SSW)
The portion of total variability in ANOVA due to differences among individual observations within each group.
Mean Square
A sum of squares divided by its corresponding degrees of freedom, used to calculate the F-statistic in ANOVA.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • ANOVA tests H0: all group means equal, vs H1: at least one group mean differs; avoids inflated Type I error from repeated t-tests.
  • Total variation = Between-Group (SSB) + Within-Group (SSW).
  • F = MSB / MSW, where MSB = SSB/(k-1) and MSW = SSW/(N-k); k = number of groups, N = total observations.
  • Reject H0 if F(calculated) > F(critical); ANOVA alone doesn't say which group(s) differ (needs post-hoc test).
  • Assumptions: independence, approximate normality within each group, homogeneity of variance across groups.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Why is ANOVA used instead of multiple t-tests when comparing more than two groups?
    Show model answer
    Running repeated t-tests on every pair of groups inflates the overall probability of a Type I error. ANOVA tests all group means simultaneously in a single test, avoiding this inflation.
  2. State the hypotheses for One-Way ANOVA.
    Show model answer
    H0: mu1 = mu2 = mu3 = ... = muk (all population means are equal). H1: at least one population mean differs from the others.
  3. Explain the logic behind the ANOVA F-statistic.
    Show model answer
    ANOVA partitions total variability into between-group variation (differences between group means) and within-group variation (random variation within each group). The F-statistic is the ratio of Mean Square Between to Mean Square Within; a large F suggests the group means differ more than would be expected from random variation alone.
  4. State the assumptions of One-Way ANOVA.
    Show model answer
    Observations must be independent, each group's population should be approximately normally distributed, and the populations should have approximately equal variances (homogeneity of variance).
