Unit 3F-Test for Two Sample Standard Deviations

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

The F-test compares the variability (not the mean) of two samples, and is also the foundation of the ANOVA technique covered in the next two topics.

2. Detailed Notes

4.1 Purpose of the F-Test

The F-test is used to test whether two populations have equal variances (or standard deviations), based on two independent samples. It is also a prerequisite check before using the equal-variance, two-sample t-test from the previous topic, and is the test statistic underlying ANOVA.

4.2 Hypotheses for the F-Test

H₀: σ₁² = σ₂² (the two population variances are equal)
H₁: σ₁² ≠ σ₂² (the two population variances are not equal)

4.3 The F-Statistic

F = s₁² ÷ s₂²

where s₁² is the larger of the two sample variances, and s₂² is the smaller — by convention, the larger variance is always placed in the numerator, so the calculated F-value is always ≥ 1.

4.4 Degrees of Freedom

The F-distribution has two separate degrees of freedom: df₁ = n₁ − 1 (numerator, from the sample with the larger variance), and df₂ = n₂ − 1 (denominator, from the sample with the smaller variance).

4.5 Decision Rule

The calculated F-value is compared against the critical F-value from F-tables, found using df₁, df₂, and the chosen significance level α. If the calculated F exceeds the critical F, H₀ is rejected, meaning there is evidence the two population variances are not equal.

4.6 Characteristics of the F-Distribution

  • The F-distribution is not symmetric — it is positively skewed.
  • F-values are always non-negative, since they are a ratio of two variances (which cannot be negative).
  • Its exact shape depends on both df₁ and df₂.

3. Key Terms

F-Test
A hypothesis test comparing the variances of two independent populations using the ratio of their sample variances.
F-Statistic
The ratio of the larger sample variance to the smaller sample variance, F = s1^2 / s2^2.
F-Distribution
A positively skewed, non-negative probability distribution used for the F-test, with two separate degrees of freedom (numerator and denominator).

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • F-test H0: sigma1^2 = sigma2^2 (equal variances); H1: sigma1^2 != sigma2^2.
  • F = s1^2 / s2^2, with the larger variance always placed in the numerator so F >= 1.
  • df1 = n1-1 (numerator, larger-variance sample), df2 = n2-1 (denominator, smaller-variance sample).
  • F-distribution: positively skewed, non-negative; reject H0 if calculated F > critical F.

5. Practice Questions

  1. What is the purpose of the F-test?
    Show model answer
    The F-test is used to test whether two populations have equal variances, based on two independent samples. It serves as a check before using the equal-variance two-sample t-test, and underlies the ANOVA technique.
  2. State the F-statistic formula and explain the convention used.
    Show model answer
    F = s1^2 / s2^2, where by convention the larger sample variance is always placed in the numerator and the smaller in the denominator, ensuring the calculated F-value is always greater than or equal to 1.
  3. State the degrees of freedom used in the F-test.
    Show model answer
    The F-distribution has two degrees of freedom: df1 = n1 - 1 for the numerator (the sample with the larger variance), and df2 = n2 - 1 for the denominator (the sample with the smaller variance).
  4. State the characteristics of the F-distribution.
    Show model answer
    The F-distribution is positively skewed rather than symmetric, its values are always non-negative since it is a ratio of variances, and its exact shape depends on both the numerator and denominator degrees of freedom.
