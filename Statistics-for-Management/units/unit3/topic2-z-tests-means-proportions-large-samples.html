Unit 3Z-Tests for Means and Proportions (Large Samples)

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

For large samples, the Z-test is used to test hypotheses about means and proportions, for both a single sample compared to a claimed value, and two samples compared to each other.

2. Detailed Notes

2.1 One-Sample Z-Test for the Mean

Used to test whether a population mean equals a specific claimed value, when the sample size is large (n ≥ 30) or the population standard deviation σ is known.

Z = (x̄ − μ₀) ÷ (σ ÷ √n)

where x̄ is the sample mean, μ₀ is the hypothesised population mean under H₀, σ is the population (or large-sample estimated) standard deviation, and n is the sample size.

2.2 Two-Sample Z-Test for Means

Used to test whether two independent population means are equal, based on two large samples.

Z = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) ÷ √[(σ₁²/n₁) + (σ₂²/n₂)]

where x̄₁, x̄₂ are the two sample means, σ₁², σ₂² are the (known or large-sample estimated) population variances, and n₁, n₂ are the two sample sizes.

2.3 One-Sample Z-Test for the Proportion

Used to test whether a population proportion equals a specific claimed value.

Z = (p̅ − p₀) ÷ √[p₀(1−p₀) ÷ n]

where p̅ is the sample proportion and p₀ is the hypothesised population proportion under H₀.

2.4 Two-Sample Z-Test for Proportions

Used to test whether two independent population proportions are equal.

Z = (p̅₁ − p̅₂) ÷ √[p̅(1−p̅)(1/n₁ + 1/n₂)]

where p̅ is the pooled proportion, combining both samples: p̅ = (x₁ + x₂) ÷ (n₁ + n₂), with x₁, x₂ the number of successes in each sample.

2.5 Decision Rule

For a two-tailed test at α = 0.05, the critical Z-value is ±1.96; for a one-tailed test at α = 0.05, it is ±1.645. If the calculated |Z| exceeds the critical value, H₀ is rejected.

3. Key Terms

One-Sample Z-Test
A hypothesis test comparing a single large sample's mean or proportion against a claimed population value.
Two-Sample Z-Test
A hypothesis test comparing the means or proportions of two independent large samples against each other.
Pooled Proportion
The combined proportion from two samples, used in the two-sample Z-test for proportions, calculated as total successes divided by total sample size.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • One-sample Z (mean): Z = (x-bar - mu0) / (sigma/sqrt(n)).
  • Two-sample Z (means): Z = (x-bar1 - x-bar2) / sqrt[(sigma1^2/n1) + (sigma2^2/n2)].
  • One-sample Z (proportion): Z = (p-hat - p0) / sqrt[p0(1-p0)/n].
  • Two-sample Z (proportions): Z = (p-hat1 - p-hat2) / sqrt[p-bar(1-p-bar)(1/n1 + 1/n2)], using pooled proportion p-bar.
  • Decision: two-tailed critical Z at alpha=0.05 is +/-1.96; one-tailed is +/-1.645; reject H0 if |Z calculated| > critical Z.

5. Practice Questions

  1. Write the formula for the one-sample Z-test for the mean, and explain each term.
    Show model answer
    Z = (x-bar - mu0) / (sigma/sqrt(n)), where x-bar is the sample mean, mu0 is the hypothesised population mean under H0, sigma is the population standard deviation, and n is the sample size.
  2. Write the formula for the two-sample Z-test for means.
    Show model answer
    Z = (x-bar1 - x-bar2) / sqrt[(sigma1^2/n1) + (sigma2^2/n2)], comparing the means of two independent large samples with known or large-sample-estimated variances.
  3. What is a pooled proportion? Why is it used in the two-sample Z-test for proportions?
    Show model answer
    The pooled proportion combines the successes from both samples into a single estimate, p-bar = (x1 + x2)/(n1 + n2). It is used because, under H0, both samples are assumed to come from populations with the same proportion, so pooling gives a better combined estimate of that common proportion for the standard error calculation.
  4. State the decision rule for a two-tailed Z-test at the 5% level of significance.
    Show model answer
    For a two-tailed test at alpha = 0.05, the critical Z-values are +/-1.96. If the calculated |Z| exceeds 1.96, the null hypothesis is rejected; otherwise, it is not rejected.
