Unit 3ANOVA — Two Way

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

Two-Way ANOVA extends One-Way ANOVA to examine the effect of two different factors simultaneously, and whether they interact with each other in their effect on the outcome.

2. Detailed Notes

6.1 When Two-Way ANOVA Is Used

Two-Way ANOVA is used when data is classified by two independent factors at once (e.g. testing both the effect of three different training methods and three different regions on sales performance), rather than just one factor as in One-Way ANOVA.

6.2 Hypotheses for Two-Way ANOVA

Two-Way ANOVA tests three separate hypotheses at once:

  1. Main Effect of Factor A: H₀: all levels of Factor A have equal means.
  2. Main Effect of Factor B: H₀: all levels of Factor B have equal means.
  3. Interaction Effect (A × B): H₀: there is no interaction between Factor A and Factor B (the effect of one factor does not depend on the level of the other).

6.3 Partitioning Total Variation

Two-Way ANOVA splits the total variation (SST) into four components:

  • SSA: Variation due to Factor A.
  • SSB: Variation due to Factor B.
  • SSAB: Variation due to the interaction between Factor A and Factor B.
  • SSE: Residual/error (within-cell) variation, not explained by either factor or their interaction.

SST = SSA + SSB + SSAB + SSE

6.5 Interpreting the Results

Each F-ratio is compared against its own critical F-value (using the relevant degrees of freedom). An important practical point: if the interaction effect is found to be statistically significant, the two main effects should be interpreted cautiously, since the effect of one factor genuinely depends on the level of the other — the interaction should usually be examined and discussed first.

6.6 Why Two-Way ANOVA Matters for Management

Two-Way ANOVA lets managers test two business variables and their interaction in one study rather than two separate ones — for example, simultaneously assessing the effect of advertising medium and target age group on campaign response, including whether certain media work better for certain age groups specifically.

3. Key Terms

Two-Way ANOVA
An ANOVA test examining the effect of two independent factors, and their interaction, on an outcome.
Main Effect
The effect of a single factor on the outcome, averaged across the levels of the other factor, in Two-Way ANOVA.
Interaction Effect
The extent to which the effect of one factor on the outcome depends on the level of the other factor.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • Two-Way ANOVA tests two factors (A, B) plus their interaction (A x B) at once; 3 sets of hypotheses: Factor A main effect, Factor B main effect, A x B interaction.
  • Total variation SST = SSA + SSB + SSAB + SSE.
  • Each effect's F = its Mean Square / MSE (Mean Square Error); compared to its own critical F value.
  • If interaction is significant, interpret main effects cautiously — examine the interaction first.

5. Practice Questions

  1. When is Two-Way ANOVA used instead of One-Way ANOVA?
    Show model answer
    Two-Way ANOVA is used when data is classified by two independent factors simultaneously, allowing the researcher to test the main effect of each factor as well as whether the two factors interact in their effect on the outcome, rather than examining just one factor at a time.
  2. State the three hypotheses tested in Two-Way ANOVA.
    Show model answer
    The main effect of Factor A (H0: all levels of Factor A have equal means), the main effect of Factor B (H0: all levels of Factor B have equal means), and the interaction effect (H0: there is no interaction between Factor A and Factor B).
  3. How is total variation partitioned in Two-Way ANOVA?
    Show model answer
    Total variation (SST) is partitioned into SSA (due to Factor A), SSB (due to Factor B), SSAB (due to the interaction between A and B), and SSE (residual error variation), such that SST = SSA + SSB + SSAB + SSE.
  4. Why should a significant interaction effect be interpreted before the main effects?
    Show model answer
    If the interaction effect is statistically significant, it means the effect of one factor genuinely depends on the level of the other factor, so interpreting either main effect alone (as if it applied uniformly) could be misleading; the interaction should be examined first to understand the fuller picture.
