Unit 3T-Tests for Means (Small Samples)

Book: Statistics for Management (BA4101, Anna University MBA Sem I, Regulation 2021)

1. Overview

When the sample size is small, the t-test replaces the Z-test for hypotheses about means, using the t-distribution introduced in Unit 2 to properly account for the extra uncertainty of a small sample.

2. Detailed Notes

3.1 When to Use the t-Test

The t-test is used to test hypotheses about a population mean when the sample size is small (commonly n < 30) and the population standard deviation σ is unknown, so the sample standard deviation s must be used instead.

3.2 One-Sample t-Test

t = (x̄ − μ₀) ÷ (s ÷ √n)

with degrees of freedom df = n − 1. The calculated t-value is compared against the critical t-value from t-tables at the chosen significance level and df.

3.3 Two-Sample t-Test (Independent Samples, Equal Variances Assumed)

First, the pooled variance combining both samples is calculated:

sp² = [(n₁−1)s₁² + (n₂−1)s₂²] ÷ (n₁ + n₂ − 2)

Then the test statistic is:

t = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) ÷ √[sp²(1/n₁ + 1/n₂)]

with degrees of freedom df = n₁ + n₂ − 2.

3.4 Paired-Sample t-Test

Used when the two samples are not independent, but are paired/matched observations (e.g. the same employees' performance before and after a training programme). The test works on the differences, d, between each pair:

t = d̄ ÷ (sd ÷ √n)

where d̄ is the mean of the paired differences, sd is the standard deviation of the differences, and n is the number of pairs, with df = n − 1.

3.5 Comparing the t-Test and Z-Test

BasisZ-Testt-Test
Sample sizeLarge (n ≥ 30)Small (n < 30)
Population σKnown, or estimated from a large sampleUnknown; sample s used instead
Distribution usedStandard Normal (Z)t-distribution (depends on df)

3. Key Terms

One-Sample t-Test
A test for whether a small sample's mean differs from a claimed population mean, using the sample standard deviation and t-distribution.
Pooled Variance
A combined estimate of variance from two independent samples, used in the two-sample t-test assuming equal population variances.
Paired-Sample t-Test
A t-test used for two related/matched samples, working on the differences between each pair of observations.

4. Exam-Prep Summary

  • One-sample t: t = (x-bar - mu0) / (s/sqrt(n)), df = n-1.
  • Two-sample t (independent, equal variances): pooled variance sp^2 = [(n1-1)s1^2 + (n2-1)s2^2] / (n1+n2-2); t = (x-bar1 - x-bar2) / sqrt[sp^2(1/n1 + 1/n2)], df = n1+n2-2.
  • Paired t-test: t = d-bar / (sd/sqrt(n)), df = n-1, works on differences between matched pairs.
  • t-test used instead of Z-test when n is small and sigma is unknown.

5. Practice Questions

  1. When is the t-test used instead of the Z-test?
    Show model answer
    The t-test is used when the sample size is small (commonly n < 30) and the population standard deviation is unknown, so the sample standard deviation must be used, which the t-distribution's heavier tails account for.
  2. Write the formula for the one-sample t-test and state its degrees of freedom.
    Show model answer
    t = (x-bar - mu0) / (s/sqrt(n)), with degrees of freedom df = n - 1.
  3. Explain the paired-sample t-test. When is it used?
    Show model answer
    The paired-sample t-test is used when two samples consist of matched or related observations, such as the same subjects measured before and after a treatment. It works on the differences d between each pair, using t = d-bar / (sd/sqrt(n)), with df = n - 1, where n is the number of pairs.
  4. Differentiate between the Z-test and the t-test.
    Show model answer
    The Z-test is used for large samples (n >= 30) with a known or large-sample-estimated population standard deviation, using the standard normal distribution. The t-test is used for small samples (n < 30) with an unknown population standard deviation, using the sample standard deviation and the t-distribution, whose shape depends on degrees of freedom.
